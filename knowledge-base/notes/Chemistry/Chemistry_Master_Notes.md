## u1 — Fundamental Concepts (moles, stoichiometry, yield, solutions)

**The mole**
- 1 mole = **6.02 × 10²³** particles (Avogadro's number). At STP one mole of gas = **22.4 dm³**.
- **2 moles of water** = 3 atoms per molecule, so **1.92 × 10²⁴ atoms** total. ×7
- Molecules in **9 g of ice** = 0.5 mol = **3.01 × 10²³**.
- Molecules in **22.4 dm³ of H₂ at 0 °C, 1 atm** = **6.02 × 10²³** (1 mole).
- Moles of CO₂ containing **8.00 g of oxygen** = oxygen is 0.5 mol, so CO₂ = **0.25 mol**.
- Moles in an element = **mass / molar mass**, i.e. directly proportional to the **mass** of the
 element. ×2
- In `PV = nRT`, `n` = **N / Nₐ** (number of particles divided by Avogadro's number).
- Same moles of H₂, N₂ and O₂ in the same volume at STP → the **number of molecules are equal**.
- 10 g glucose in 100 cm³ solution → **0.55 M**.
- **Molarity** = moles of solute per **dm³ of solution** (not per dm³ of water).
- **Mole fraction** = moles of one component over moles of **all components in a solution**.
- **Boiling point constants** are used to determine **molar masses** (ebullioscopy).
- Raoult's law: relative lowering of vapour pressure = **mole fraction of solute**.

**Molar-mass arithmetic**
- **60 g NaOH = 1.5 mol** (60 ÷ 40). The option list in the papers is damaged — work the
 division, not the letters.
- **0.1 g of sodium** = 0.1 ÷ 23 = **4.3 × 10⁻³ mol**. ×2
- **1.75 kg CaCO₃** = 1750 ÷ 100 = **17.5 mol**.
- **2.8 g ethylene (C₂H₄, M = 28)** = **0.1 mol**.
- **10.6 g Na₂CO₃** contains **0.3 mol of oxygen** (0.1 mol salt × 3).
- **16.5 g HCl** gives **0.452 mol NaCl** in the neutralisation.
- **3 mol calcium** = 3 × 40 = **120 g**.
- Oxygens in **278 g FeSO₄·7H₂O** (M = 278) = 1 mol salt × 11 O = **6.62 × 10²⁴ oxygen atoms**.

**Balanced equations and limiting reagent**
- Combustion of **2 mol propane** needs **10 mol O₂** (C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O). ×6
- **N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃**: 2.5 mol N₂ gives **5 mol NH₃**.
- **2A + B → product** with equal moles of A and B → **A is limiting**.
- For **2A + B₂ → 2AB**, A is limiting in the **2 mol A + 1 mol B₂** mixture.
- The reactant consumed **first** is the **limiting reactant** — the one **not in excess**. ×3
- 4 mol H₂ + 2 mol O₂ react exactly (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: 4 mol H₂ needs 2 mol O₂) → **4 mol water**.
 ×2
- **CS₂ + 3O₂ → CO₂ + 2SO₂**: 7.6 g CS₂ with 12.8 g O₂ → **O₂ is limiting**.

**Yield**
- **Percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100**. ×12
- Theoretical 25 g, actual 20 g → **80%**. ×9
- Expected 28 g, obtained 18 g, 4 g lost to spillage → real yield **14 g, 50%**. ×2
- % yield 80, actual 20 g → theoretical = **25 g**.
- Actual yield reaches the theoretical value at **100% (1.0)**.
- Reaction efficiency is checked with **percentage yield**, not actual or theoretical alone.
- Actual yield is **less than** theoretical because of **mechanical losses**, reaction
 **reversibility**, and not every molecule having activation energy — "**the substance is pure**"
 is the incorrect reason.

**Empirical and molecular formula**
- **Empirical formula** = simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. ×2
- Empirical formula of **glucose C₆H₁₂O₆** = **CH₂O**.
- Empirical **C₃H₃O** with molar mass 110.15 → multiply by 2 → **C₆H₆O₂**. ×2
- Polymer with empirical **CH₂** and molar mass **28000** → **2000 times** the empirical formula. ×2
- Combustion analysis: C = 40%, H = 6.67%, O = 53.3% → **CH₃O**... work the ratios: C:H:O = 3.33 :
 6.67 : 3.33 → **CH₂O** as keyed by the papers.
- Phosphorus oxide with 43.6% oxygen → **P₂O₅**.
- Combustion of 0.5439 g giving 1.039 g CO₂ and 0.6369 g H₂O → **CH₄O**.

**Isotopes and atomic mass**
- Isotopes: **same atomic number, different mass number** — differ in **neutron** number. ×3
- Standard for relative atomic mass = **Carbon-12**. ×2
- Atomic masses depend on the **number of isotopes and their abundance**.
- **Mass spectrometry** gives the type and relative amount of each isotope; the detector measures
 **relative abundances**.
- **Boron** average mass 10.8 with isotopes 10 and 11 → **20%** of mass 10.
- Neon (²⁰Ne 90.92%, ²¹Ne 8.82%, ²²Ne 0.26%) → **Aᵣ = 20.18**; no single Ne atom is 20.18 u
 because that is the **average atomic mass**. ×2
- Isotopes are separated after converting the substance to a **charged state**.
- Same electrons and protons but different neutrons = **isotopes**.

**Miscellaneous**
- Stoichiometry is the **quantitative** study of reactants and products via a balanced equation.
- "**Active mass**" in the law of mass action = **moles per dm³** (concentration).
- The equilibrium constant is **unitless when the moles of products equal the moles of
 reactants**.
- 'n' in the **Nernst equation** = number of electrons transferred in the redox reaction.
- Oxidation numbers X +6, Y −2, Z −1 combine as **XY₂Z₆**-type — the keyed option is **XY₂Z**
 (check charge balance: 6 + 2(−2) + n(−1) = 0 → n = 2).
- Air molecules do not settle because of the **elastic collisions** of gas molecules.
- RMS speed of a gas of N molecules = √(v₁² + v₂² + … + vₙ²)/N.
- Gas density in a cube of side `a`, N molecules each of mass `m` = **Nm/a³**.
- Two gases at the same T and P with V₁:V₂ = 2:3 → mole ratio **2:3**.
- **2XeF₆ + SiO₂ → 2XeOF₄ + SiF₄**: 122.6 g XeF6 with 60 g SiO₂ — identify the limiting
 reagent by molar ratio.

## u2 — Atomic Structure (quantum numbers, configurations, spectra)

**The four quantum numbers**
- **n (principal)** → the shell, and it decides the orbital's **size**; s orbitals keep the same
 shape but grow as n rises. ×3
- **l (azimuthal)** → the subshell and the **shape** of the orbital. ×7
- **m (magnetic)** → the number of **orientations** in space.
- **s (spin)** → +½ or −½, the **direction of spin**; the one quantum number **not** from the
 Schrödinger equation.
- An electron is completely described by **four** quantum numbers.
- Maximum value of l is **l = n − 1** (l runs 0 → n−1). ×4
- **m ranges from −l to +l**: for l = 1, m = **−1 to 1**. ×3
- Orbitals in a subshell = **2l + 1**; for l = 2 (d) that is **5 orbitals** holding **10
 electrons**. ×3
- **f (l = 3) has 7 orientations**; **p has 3**; **d has 5**; **s has 1** — the only single-
 orientation orbital. ×3
- Maximum electrons in shell n = **2n²**.
- The **N shell (n = 4)** holds **s, p, d and f**.

**Shapes and overlap**
- **p orbitals** are dumb-bell shaped with **2 lobes**. ×2
- The π bond of **propene** is a sideward overlap of unhybridised **p orbitals**, not of the sp²
 hybrids. ×3
- In **sp²** hybridisation the unhybridised p orbital is **perpendicular** to the plane of the three
 sp² orbitals — at **90°**. ×2
- **Benzene's** π molecular orbitals are **delocalised**.
- Ethene: **2 unhybridised p orbitals** overlap to form the π bond.
- Among (CH₃)₃COH / CO₂ / C₂H₄ / CH₃COOH, the one where **every** carbon uses **sp³** is
 **(CH₃)₃COH**.

**Filling rules**
- **Aufbau** = build up in order of increasing energy, ordered by the **(n + l) rule**.
- **Hund's rule** = orbitals of a subshell are singly occupied before any pairing. ×3
- **Pauli exclusion** = no two electrons share the same four quantum numbers. ×2
- After **3d** fills, the next electron enters **4p** (4s is already full). ×2
- After **6d** fills, the next electron enters **7s**. ×3
- Energy order: **4s < 3d < 4p**; and **4s < 4p < 4d < 4f**.

**Configurations that repeat**
- **[Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵** = chromium (Z 24). ×4
- **Fe²⁺** = **[Ar] 4s⁰ 3d⁶** — the 4s electrons leave first.
- **Carbon** = 1s² 2s² 2p². ×2
- **Argon** = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ (18 e⁻). ×3
- Outermost **ns² np⁵** = **chlorine**; **ns²np⁶** = a halide ion / noble gas. ×2
- **Alkaline earth metals** = **ns²**.
- **[Kr] 5s² 4d⁶** = **ruthenium (Ru)**.
- **Au (Z 79)** = [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d¹⁰ **6s¹**.
- **3d⁶ 4s²** in the 3d series = **nickel**.
- The two **anomalous** 3d configurations are **chromium and copper**, stabilised by half-filled /
 fully-filled d subshells. ×2
- The unpaired electron of a **chlorine atom** = **(3, 1, 1)**.
- **M²⁺** with 2,8,14 and atomic weight 56 → Z = 26 (Fe), so **30 neutrons**.
- **Be > B in first ionisation energy** because Be has the **stable filled 2s²** configuration.
- Allowed set of quantum numbers: **n=3, l=2, m=0, s=+½** (l must be < n, |m| ≤ l).
- **Not allowed**: n=2, l=2 (l cannot equal n).
- The element with **1s²2s²2p¹** belongs to **group IIIA**.
- **4s²4p¹** valence shell = **group IIIA**.
- **[Ne] 3s²3p⁵** = a **non-metal of group VII-A**.
- Fully occupying the first two spherical (s) orbitals = **beryllium** (1s²2s²).
- Pair with **1 electron in the outermost s**: **Na & Cr** (Na 3s¹, Cr 4s¹).

**Magnetism**
- **F₂ is diamagnetic** because all its electrons are **paired in p orbitals**. ×6
- Diamagnetic = no unpaired electrons; paramagnetic = at least one.
- Transition-metal compounds with unpaired electrons are **attracted by a magnet**.

**Spectra of hydrogen**
- Rydberg: **ν̄ = R(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²)**, n₂ > n₁. ×2
- **Balmer series** = the **visible** region. ×3
- **Lyman** = ultraviolet, and the **highest-frequency** series; **Paschen, Brackett, Pfund** =
 infrared. ×2
- Infrared transition = **n = 5 → 3** (3→2 and 4→2 are Balmer; 2→1 is Lyman).
- The last Lyman line (n₂ → ∞) has **λ = 1/R**.
- ν̄ = R(1/2² − 1/n²) with n > 2 is the **Balmer** equation.
- Orbital **energy** is decided by the **(n + l) rule**.

**Historical particles and effects**
- **J. J. Thomson** measured the **e/m ratio** of the electron.
- **Chadwick** discovered the **neutron**.
- **Cathode rays** are material particles with definite mass and velocity but they do **not**
 travel at the speed of light — that is the false statement.
- Cathode-ray nature is **independent of the gas** in the discharge tube.
- **Canal (positive) rays** = the other name for positive rays; proton mass **1.6726 × 10⁻²⁷ kg**,
 charge/mass **9.54 × 10⁷ C/kg**, deflected by electric and magnetic fields — the false claim is
 that they **do not cause mechanical motion**.
- Lightest canal-ray particles come from **H₂**.
- **Electric current in solution** is carried by **ions**, not free electrons. ×2
- **Zeeman effect** = splitting of spectral lines in a **magnetic** field; **Stark** = electric.
- **Bohr** introduced **energy levels** (stationary orbits).
- Same-orbital electrons spin opposite to **reduce repulsion** and cancel magnetic fields.
- Spontaneous electron emission from solids under radiation = the **photoelectric effect**.
- Of the three radioactive paths in an electric field, path 1 (bent to the negative plate) is
 **alpha**.
- **Planck's constant h is unchanged** when photon frequency doubles (E = hf scales with f).
- **Cu²⁺** solution colour comes from **d → d** transitions.
- Racemic mixture in **Sₙ1** arises from the carbocation's **empty p orbital**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Alkanes** have only **single** bonds between carbons. →u14
- **Fischer esterification** is a **condensation** reaction. →u18
- **760 torr = 101325 Pa**; **10 Pa = 0.000098 atm**. →u3

## u3 — Gases (gas laws, kinetic theory, real gases)

**The laws**
- **Boyle**: at constant T, **V ∝ 1/P** (PV = constant); double the pressure and volume becomes
 **one-half**. Sea-level air being dense is Boyle's law in practice. ×2
- **Charles**: at constant P, **V ∝ absolute temperature** — the law used to calculate **absolute
 temperature**. ×2
- **Avogadro**: equal volumes of gases at the same T and P hold equal numbers of molecules.
- **Dalton**: partial pressure **Pᵢ = Xᵢ Pₜ** (mole fraction × total pressure). ×6
- The SI unit of pressure is the **pascal**.
- **R = 0.0821 atm dm³ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹**; **R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹**. ×3
- STP in the papers = **14.7 psi and 0 °C** (1 atm, 273 K).
- **1 atm = 760 torr = 101325 Pa**; **10 Pa = 0.000098 atm**.

**Absolute zero and kinetic theory**
- **Absolute zero = −273.16 °C** (= 0 K): molecular motion **ceases**, K.E. is **zero**, and an
 ideal gas's pressure is **0 atm**. ×3
- The postulates explaining ideal-gas behaviour = the **kinetic molecular theory of gases**.
- Ideal-gas molecules have **zero** intermolecular forces. ×6
- K.E. per molecule = **3RT / 2Nₐ**.
- Real gases deviate most at **low temperature and high pressure**. ×2
- Real gases show **positive** deviation as pressure rises because of **repulsive forces**.
- Both ideal and real gases have molecules in **constant random motion** (elastic collisions and
 zero size are ideal-only).
- The false KMT postulate: molecules "do not exert pressure when colliding with the wall" — they
 do.
- Density in a rigid sealed container **stays the same** when T rises.
- Compressibility factor **Z = PV/RT > 1** at STP → molar volume **greater than 22.4 L**.

**Gas calculations that repeat**
- Burning **500 dm³ of ethene** at STP needs **1500 dm³ of O₂** (C₂H₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O).
- **CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂**: 48.8 L of CO₂ at STP (≈2.18 mol) needs about **200 g CaCO₃**... work
 moles × 100; the keyed answer is **200 g**.
- **H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O**: making **1 mol water** needs **11.2 dm³ of H₂** at STP (½ mol).
- A gas at 10 dm³ and 10 °C halves its volume at **141.5 K** (Charles: T₂ = 283/2).
- 1 atm N₂ at 25 °C raised to 2 atm at 50 °C: 1 dm³ → **0.542 dm³**.
- **X mL of H₂** effuses in 5 s; the same volume of **O₂** takes **20 s** (rate ∝ 1/√M, O₂ is
 16× denser → 4× slower).
- Boiling water at **110 °C** needs an external pressure of **760–1200 torr**.
- Jar at 830 mmHg with water drops, cooled 1% (vapour pressure 30 → 25 mmHg): new pressure
 **805 mmHg** — the keyed option is **792 mm of Hg**.
- **KClO₃ → KCl + O₂**: the volume of solid KCl cannot be determined from gas data — answer
 "**cannot be determined from the given information**".
- Two ice cubes pressed into one = **H-bonding** (recrystallisation), not van der Waals.
- **Le Chatelier's principle**: a stress on an equilibrium is partly nullified by the system. ×2
- **Kc** for PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂ has units **mol/dm³**.

**More gas facts**
- One mole of any gas at STP occupies **22.414 dm³**.
- **PV/nRT** is the **compressibility factor**.
- The general gas equation **PV = nRT** is the **ideal gas equation**.
- **R's dimensions** = **[ML²T⁻²K⁻¹]**; raising 1 mol of ideal gas by 1 K costs **8.314 J**
 (the papers key **0.0821 kJ**-scale options — use 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹).
- **Cp = Cv + R**, so with Cv = 3/2 R, **Cp = 5/2 R**; for a monatomic gas **Cp/Cv ≈ 1.67**.
- **Isothermal expansion**: temperature stays constant (internal energy of an ideal gas depends
 only on **temperature**).
- Absorbs 1000 J and expands against 20 kPa from 25 to 50 cm³: ΔU = 1000 − 500 = **+500 J**.
- Work done expanding 15 → 20 dm³ against 2 atm = **−5 atm dm³** (W = −PΔV).
- Melting 1 mol ice at 0 °C absorbing 6025 J, ΔV = −0.002 dm³: **ΔE ≈ 6025.20 J**.
- Bread + peanut butter (20 g CHO, 10 g protein, 9 g fat) = **201 kcal** total.
- **ΔH is measured indirectly by Hess's law**.
- Collecting gas **over water** is an application of **Dalton's law**.
- Stoichiometry must obey the **law of conservation of mass**.
- **RMS velocity ∝ 1/√(molar mass)**; v_rms = √(3P/ρ).
- H₂ vs O₂ at 300 K (O₂ 16× heavier): H₂'s rms speed is **4×** oxygen's.
- Boyle's law graph = **V vs 1/P, a straight line through the origin**.
- At absolute zero, hydrogen molecules have **all motion ceased**.
- Gas density depends on **pressure, temperature and molar mass** — all of the above.
- Partial pressure of H₂ in 9.6% NH₃ / 22.6% N₂ / 67.8% H₂ at 50 atm = **67.8 × 50/100**.
- Charles's law: gas volume would reach zero at **−273.15 °C**.
- **212 °F = 373 K**... 212 °F = 100 °C = **373 K**.
- Collisions must be **elastic** for total K.E. to stay constant.
- **Hydrogen bonding** stabilises α-helices and β-sheets of proteins and holds the two DNA
 strands together. ×2
- Mg + ½O₂ → MgO, ΔH = −692 kJ/mol: that is a standard enthalpy of **formation (ΔH°f)**.
- 125 g Mg + 32 g O₂ → MgO: **excess Mg left = 105 g** (O₂ is limiting; the keyed option list is

## u4 — Liquids (intermolecular forces, vapour pressure, boiling point)

**Intermolecular forces, strongest to weakest**
- **Hydrogen bonding** is the strongest of the usual intermolecular set (London / dipole-dipole /
 H-bond — H-bond wins). ×2
- The **weakest** is the **instantaneous dipole–induced dipole (London dispersion)** force.
- Forces arising from **temporary dipoles** = **dispersion forces**.
- **H-bonding is strongest in HF**, but **H₂O boils higher** because water forms **more** hydrogen
 bonds. ×2
- **Alcohols boil higher than alkanes** of similar mass because of **hydrogen bonding**. ×6
- **Carboxylic acids** boil high because of **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** (dimerisation).
- **Ether boils lower than alcohols/phenols** — weaker intermolecular forces (no O–H to donate).
- **Diethyl ether's** low boiling point vs water = **weak intermolecular forces**.
- **Water > petrol** in boiling point because water's intermolecular forces are **stronger**.
- **H-bonding holds the two DNA strands together** and stabilises α-helix / β-sheet proteins.
- **H-bonding does NOT exist in SiH₄**; it does in proteins, alcohols and liquid HF.
- The polar part of soap dissolves in water via **dipole-dipole / ion-dipole** attraction.
- **I₂ molecules** attract by **instantaneous dipole–induced dipole** forces; iodine is the soft,
 weak-force molecular solid among H₂O / I₂ / sugar / graphite.
- **Molecular crystals** are held by **van der Waals forces**.
- **Sulphur melts higher than phosphorus** because S₈ has **stronger van der Waals forces**.
- **Group VIIA hydride with the lowest boiling point = HCl**. ×6
- Boiling-point order of the hydrides: **H₂O > HF > NH₃ > HCl**. ×2
- Alkali metals: **K has the least boiling point** of Li / K / Na / Cs.

**Vapour pressure and boiling**
- Vapour pressure does **not** depend on the **amount of liquid** (it depends on T, intermolecular
 forces and the type of bond). ×6
- Vapour pressure is **independent of the surface area** of the liquid.
- **Strong dipole-dipole forces → high heat of vaporisation**. ×2
- Heat absorbed when 1 mol of liquid becomes gas at its boiling point = **heat of
 vaporisation**. ×2
- Heat to turn 1 mol of solid **directly** into vapour = **standard heat of sublimation**;
 solid → gas directly = **sublimation**. ×2
- **Lowest vapour pressure at 20 °C = water** (strongest H-bonding of ether / chloroform / CCl₄ /
 water). ×2
- **Highest vapour pressure** of acetone / acetaldehyde / isopentane / benzene = **isopentane**
 (weakest forces, lowest mass).
- **Lower vapour pressure** of CHCl₃ / CCl₄ / H₂O / acetone = **water**. ×2
- If intermolecular forces between gas molecules suddenly vanished, **pressure would remain
 unchanged**. ×2
- **Vacuum distillation lowers the boiling point**: glycerin (290 °C at 1 atm) boils near
 **210 °C at 50 torr**.
- **Branching decreases boiling point** because it **reduces the surface area** available for
 attractive forces.
- Rate of evaporation is **not** affected by the **amount of liquid** (surface area, temperature
 and intermolecular forces do affect it).
- **Static equilibrium** is the equilibrium stage not affected by temperature. ×2
- Decomposition (vs evaporation) of 1 mol needs **high energy**.

**Exothermic and endothermic**
- **Oxidation of sulphur** is exothermic (melting ice, evaporating water and photosynthesis are
 endothermic).
- **Respiration** is the exothermic process among evaporation / sublimation / boiling /
 respiration.

**Boiling point defined, and pressure effects**
- Boiling point = the temperature at which **vapour pressure equals the atmospheric pressure**.
- Halving the external pressure makes ethanol boil **below 78 °C**.
- At **high altitude** water boils below 100 °C because of **lower atmospheric pressure**.
- **Steam burns worse than boiling water** because of its **high latent heat of vaporisation**.
- **Molar heat of vaporisation of water = 40.7 kJ/mol**.
- Highest boiling point of the alcohols = **n-propyl alcohol** (straight chain beats branched;
 tert-butyl is the most branched).
- Highest boiling point of the alkanes = **C₁₀H₂₂** (longest chain, strongest dispersion).
- **Lowest boiling point** of water / ethanol / H₂S / acetic acid = **hydrogen sulphide** (no
 H-bonding).
- **SO₂ has a higher heat of vaporisation than CO₂** because **SO₂ is polar and CO₂ is
 non-polar**.
- **HF's acidity is exceptionally low** because of its **strong H–F hydrogen bonding**.
- **Weakest London dispersion = He** (smallest, fewest electrons).
- **London dispersion is the only force among helium atoms** in the gas phase.
- **Hydrogen bonding is exhibited by NH₃** (not H₂S, HI or SiH₄).
- **Hydrocarbons do NOT form hydrogen bonds** (ammonia, ethyl alcohol and carboxylic acids do).
- **Highest surface tension = mercury** overall; among water / benzene / alcohols / ether it is
 **water**. ×2
- **Vapour pressure is measured with a manometer**.
- Vapour pressure does **not** depend on the **amount of liquid**.
- Dipole-dipole strength does **not** affect **moles** (it affects B.pt, heat of sublimation and
 heat of vaporisation).
- **Ice at 0 °C occupies more volume than water** because of the **empty spaces** in its open
 lattice; ice floats for the same reason. ×2
- **Freezing point** = the temperature where solid and liquid have the **same vapour pressure**.
- **Transition temperature** = where one crystalline form changes into another.
- 18 g glucose in 1 kg water boils at **100.52 °C** (ΔTb = Kb·m = 0.52 × 1).
- Benzene as solvent has the **largest molal boiling-point constant (2.70)** of the four listed.
- The line for **pure water** on a vapour-pressure plot is the **highest** one (lowest curve
 number).
- Across the short periods, melting and boiling points **first increase then decrease** left to
 right.
- **Polyamide (nylon)** forms by condensing hexane-dioic acid with **hexane-1,6-diamine**.
- Most fluid flow is **turbulent rather than laminar because of high velocities**.
- Heat to change 1 kg solid → liquid at the melting point = **latent heat of fusion**.
- Water circulates in living organisms because of **cohesion and adhesion**.
- Newton's original formula **underestimated the speed of sound in air because he assumed
 isothermal** conditions.
- A sphere of radius r in a fluid of viscosity η: retarding force / speed = **6πηr** (Stokes).
- An oil film's colour pattern on water = **interference**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**

## u5 — Solids (crystal lattices, lattice energy, amorphous vs crystalline)

**Unit cells and lattice types**
- The **unit cell** = the smallest repeating part of a crystal lattice that still has all the
 properties of the whole crystal. ×3
- **Cubic** unit cell: **a = b = c, α = β = γ = 90°**.
- In a unit cell the angle **β is between faces b and c**.
- **NaCl** = **octahedral** arrangement of its crystal lattice. ×6
- **Iodine's** unit cell is **orthorhombic**; I₂ forms **molecular crystals**.
- The crystal's shape depends on the **arrangement of particles**.

**Crystalline vs amorphous**
- **Glass, rubber, plastics and charcoal are amorphous** — not crystalline. ×3
- **Copper, alum, sugar, NaCl, KCl, Fe, rhombic sulphur, copper sulphate** are crystalline.
- Amorphous solids **can possess small regions of orderly atomic arrangement**; they have no sharp
 melting point and do not cleave cleanly.
- Crystalline solids break along definite **cleavage planes**.
- **Copper crystals have no cleavage plane** (metallic, deforms rather than cleaves).
- **Graphite conducts better in one direction than another** because of **anisotropy**; properties
 that change with direction = **anisotropic** properties. ×2
- **Graphite is both anisotropic and a good conductor**.
- An element in different physical forms = **allotropy**; a compound in different crystal forms =
 **polymorphism**.

**Lattice energy**
- The **Born–Haber cycle** is used to evaluate **lattice energy**.
- **Mg²⁺(g) + O²⁻(g) → MgO(s)** represents **lattice energy**.
- Lattice energy is the enthalpy of **formation** of the crystal from its gaseous ions.
- Lattice energy tells you about the **structure of ionic compounds**. ×2
- Lattice energy rises as ions get **smaller** and charges get **higher**:
 - **NaF > NaCl > NaBr > NaI** (F⁻ smallest → **NaF greatest**). ×2
 - **LiF** beats NaCl / KCl / CsI; **LiCl** beats NaCl / KCl / KBr.
 - **C₆H₁₂O₆** (a molecular solid) has the **lowest** lattice energy of the ionic list.
- Born–Haber steps: atomise the metal, ionise the metal, ionise the non-metal — "**delonize the
 metal**" is not a real step.

**Conductivity in carbon allotropes**
- **Graphite conducts** because **one valence electron per carbon is free to move** (delocalised
 between the layers); diamond does not.
- **Diamond is the covalent crystal** (NaCl, KI, MgBr₂ are ionic).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- In the ice lattice each O is attached to **four H atoms**. →u3
- **Polymorphism** in biology = *Obelia* (Coelenterata/Cnidaria) — the same word, different
 subject. →biology

## u6 — Chemical Equilibrium (Le Chatelier, Kc/Kp, buffers, solubility)

**Equilibrium basics**
- At equilibrium the **forward and backward rates are equal** — no net concentration change. ×2
- The ultimate fate of a reversible reaction = **a state with no net concentration change**.
- **Kc changes only with temperature** (not pressure or concentration). ×2
- A reaction proceeds **forward** when **Kc is large** (Kc = 10 beats 1, 1/10, 1/100); it goes
 nearly **to completion when Kc is large but positive**.
- Kc = 1 means neither side is favoured; Kc is never negative.
- Equilibrium expression **[C]²/[A][B]** ↔ the equation **A + B ⇌ 2C**.
- **Kp = Kc when the moles of gas are equal on both sides** — e.g. **H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI**. ×2
- **Kp > Kc when volume increases on the product side** (more gas moles made). ×6
- Direction of shift is predicted by **Le Chatelier's principle**. ×2
- Le Chatelier predicts the effect of **concentration, pressure and temperature** — it does **not**
 predict a change in **reaction rate**.
- Removing the **product** shifts equilibrium to the **product side**; raising pressure shifts to
 the **side with lower volume** (fewer gas moles). ×2
- Raising temperature in an **exothermic** reaction shifts equilibrium to the **reactant side**.
- **Kc increases with temperature only for endothermic reactions** — of the four listed, the
 papers key **H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI**.
- Ozone decomposition at 25 °C has a huge **K ≈ 10⁵⁵**.

**Haber and Contact processes**
- **Haber process** maximum ammonia yield = **high pressure, low temperature, continual removal of
 ammonia**. ×2
- The **NOT-true** way to raise NH₃ yield is **continuous addition of NH₃** (low temperature, high
 pressure and removal are all correct).
- **Decreasing temperature** raises ammonia yield (the reaction is exothermic).
- **200 atm** high pressure in Haber's process is for **better yield**.
- Haber's **catalyst = iron (Fe)**; nitrogen comes from **air**. ×2
- **Contact process** catalyst for sulfuric acid = **V₂O₅**.

**Acids, bases and buffers**
- Diluting a **weak acid with water increases [H⁺]** (ionisation rises). ×6
- A **buffer resists change in pH** — e.g. **CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa**. ×2
- Heat of neutralisation of a strong acid + strong base ≈ **−57.4 kJ/mol**.
- A reaction in a **single step** = an **elementary reaction**.
- Rate rises with temperature because the **number of effective collisions increases**.

**Solubility and Ksp**
- Adding a common ion **decreases solubility**: KClO₃ solubility falls when **KCl** is added.
- **Ksp of PbSO₄ at 25 °C = 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ mol² dm⁻⁶**.
- Salt AB (Ksp = 9 × 10⁻⁶): molar solubility = √Ksp = **3 × 10⁻³**.
- PbS (Ksp = 4.0 × 10⁻²⁸): ionic concentration = √Ksp = **2 × 10⁻¹⁴**.
- N₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2NO, Kc = 0.1 at 2000 °C, 0.1 M each: **[NO] at equilibrium = 0.028 M**.
- Ca(OH)₂ (Ksp = 6.5 × 10⁻⁶): solubility ≈ **1.17 × 10⁻²**.
- **Ksp** = the product of ion concentrations in a saturated solution, each raised to its
 coefficient; for AgCl, **Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]**.
- **Precipitation occurs when the ionic product is MORE than Ksp**; if it is **less than Ksp, no
 precipitation** occurs. ×2
- **Common ion effect** = the drop in solubility when a common ion is added — purifying NaCl by
 passing HCl gas, and precipitating AgCl by adding NaCl, are both common-ion effects. ×2

**pH, pKa and buffers**
- **pH + pOH = 14** for pure water at 25 °C.
- **Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([base]/[acid])**.
- The buffer with the **highest pH** = the one with the **most conjugate base** (0.1 M CH₃COOH +
 **0.15 M** CH₃COO⁻).
- **Higher Ka = stronger acid; lower pKa = stronger acid** (and higher pKa = weaker acid).
- The **weaker** acid of HClO₄ (−10) / HBr (−9) / H₂SO₄ (−3) / HCl (−7) = **H₂SO₄ (−3)** (highest
 pKa).
- **Kc has no units** when gas moles balance: **H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI** and **CO + H₂O ⇌ CO₂ + H₂**.
- **2HF ⇌ H₂ + F₂**, Kc = 10⁻¹³: **Kp = 10⁻¹³** (equal gas moles, so Kp = Kc).

**Haber and Contact detail**
- Haber's process is **exothermic** and its equation is **N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)**.
- Haber optimum temperature = **400–450 °C** (modern plants 670–770 K, i.e. 400–500 °C).
- Haber catalyst = **iron crystals with metal-oxide promoters** (not MgO, Al₂O₃ or SiO₂).
- To raise NH₃ yield: **increase the pressure** (and remove ammonia, add excess N₂).
- **Contact process** SO₂ → SO₃ catalyst = **vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅)**.
- SO₃ is **not** absorbed directly in water because the reaction is **highly exothermic** (a mist
 forms); instead it goes to oleum.
- To raise SO₃ yield in **2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (ΔH = −96)**: use **excess air** to drive the
 equilibrium right (and remove SO₃).
- **2NO + O₂ ⇌ 2NO₂ (ΔH = −114)** is favoured at **low temperature**.
- Le Chatelier gives **no information about time**.
- A **catalyst does not affect the equilibrium position** (only concentration, temperature and
 pressure do).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Esterification**: warming ethanol with ethanoic acid breaks the **O–H bond in ethanol** (the
 alcohol loses its hydroxyl hydrogen). →u18
- **Spring time period** (4 kg, k = 400 N/m): T = 2π√(m/k) = **0.628 s** — a physics item.
- **Highest lattice energy = LiF** — a u5 solids fact. →u5

## u7 — Reaction Kinetics (rate laws, order, catalysts, collision theory)

**Rate and its units**
- **Rate of reaction = change in concentration ÷ time taken**. ×6
- Unit of rate = **mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** (i.e. mol·dm⁻³·s⁻¹). ×6
- Rate **decreases** as the reaction proceeds (reactants are used up). ×2
- The rate between two time intervals = the **average rate**; the rate at one moment =
 **instantaneous rate**.
- A **greater slope near the start** means a **greater** initial rate.
- Rate = k[A]²[B]: halving [A] drops the rate by a factor of **4**.

**Order and molecularity**
- First order in A, second order in B → **Rate = k[A][B]²**. ×5
- **Sₙ1 (unimolecular nucleophilic substitution) = first-order kinetics**. ×6
- **Sₙ2 = second-order kinetics**.
- When molar concentration of every reactant is **unity**, rate = the **rate constant** (they are
 equal). ×2
- The rate equals the **specific rate constant when concentrations are unity**.
- **Order of reaction from k's unit**: k in **s⁻¹** → **first order**; k in **L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹** →
 **second order**. ×2
- Unit of the rate constant depends on the **order of reaction**. ×2
- **Rate constant depends on temperature** (not concentration, volume or pressure). ×2
- **Photochemical reactions** whose rate depends only on light intensity are **zero order**.
- **NO + O₃ → NO₂ + O₂** is **second order** (first in each reactant).
- The **rate-determining step** of a multi-step reaction is always the **slowest step**.
- The **Lucas test** is based on the **rate of reaction** (how fast the alkyl chloride appears).

**Activation energy and collision theory**
- **Activation energy** = the minimum energy molecules need to react on collision.
- For a reaction to occur, colliding molecules need **sufficient kinetic energy AND proper
 orientation** (collision theory).
- The unstable high-energy arrangement at the top of the barrier = the **activated complex /
 transition state**.
- Activation energy is **different for the forward and backward reaction**, and is **unaffected by
 a catalyst**.
- **Endothermic reactions have ΔH positive**.

**Catalysts and enzymes**
- A catalyst speeds a reaction by **lowering the activation energy** / providing an **alternative
 pathway** — it does **not** change ΔH, concentration of products, or shift equilibrium. ×2
- The **rate constant is independent of the initial concentration** of reactants.
- A substance that **retards** a reaction = an **inhibitor**.
- **Hydrogenation of oils** to solid fat uses a **nickel** catalyst.
- **Enzymes** are the biological catalysts; they are **organic, protein** catalysts that act
 **independently of the cell** — the false claim is that one enzyme catalyses **all** metabolic
 reactions.
- Enzymes lower activation energy by **providing an alternative pathway**.
- **Ligands act as Lewis bases** (they donate an electron pair).
- Raising **pressure** increases the rate only when **one or more reactants are gases**.
- The **Arrhenius equation** describes the effect of **temperature** on the rate constant.

**Order — more definitions**
- **Order of reaction** = the power to which a reactant's concentration is raised in the rate
 equation.
- The final equation expressing rate in terms of concentration = the **rate law**.
- **Zero-order** reactions: rate is **independent of the reactant concentration**. ×2
- A reaction whose rate doesn't depend on the concentration terms = a **zero-order reaction**.
- **Specific rate constant k is independent of concentration**; it is "specific" because it is the
 **rate per unit concentration**.
- Doubling [A] and quadrupling the product → **second order**.
- **2A + B → product** with B in large excess behaves as a **pseudo first-order** reaction.
- Photosynthesis **6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂** is a **zero-order** reaction (light-limited).
- Rate = k[A][B] with the slow step **2NO + H₂ → N₂ + H₂O₂** matches the rate law
 **k[NO]²[H₂]**.

**Rate calculations**
- First order, k = 0.0458 s⁻¹, [A] = 0.35 M → rate = **0.016 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹**.
- First-order unit of k = **s⁻¹**.
- ΔC = 6 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ over 10 s → rate = **6 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹**.
- k = 0.693 min⁻¹, first order → half-life = 0.693/k = **1 min**.
- Raising temperature increases rate mainly because **collision frequency increases**.
- Forward rate falls with time because **reactant concentration decreases**.
- **Zn powder** gives the most H₂ with 1 M HCl (largest surface area).
- Rate of ionic reactions is studied by the **electrical conductivity method**.
- The **SN2 transition state** carries an overall **negative** charge.
- **Autocatalysis** = a product of the reaction acts as its own catalyst.
- **Activated-complex formation is endothermic** (energy is absorbed to reach the peak).
- **Enzymes increase rate by decreasing activation energy**; more substrate raises the rate
 **until all active sites are saturated**.
- The **Arrhenius plot** of log k vs 1/T is a **straight line with negative slope**.
- Pepsin's rate-vs-pH curve peaks near **pH 2** (a low-pH bell).

## u8 — Thermochemistry (first law, enthalpy, Hess's law, entropy, free energy)

**First law and internal energy**
- **First law = conservation of energy**; energy can be **converted from one form to another**,
 never created or destroyed. ×2
- Mathematical statement: **dE = q + w**. ×4
- Work done **by** the system and heat **absorbed** by it → **ΔE = q + (−w)** (the papers key B;
 note option A `q − w` is algebraically the same expression). ×6
- At **constant volume**, heat supplied = the **internal energy change**, **ΔE = qv**. ×2
- In an **isothermal** process the change in internal energy is **zero**.
- Raising internal energy can change state, allow a reaction, or raise temperature — **all of
 these**.

**State vs path functions**
- **State functions** depend only on the **initial and final states**: enthalpy, internal energy,
 pressure, volume, temperature, Gibbs energy.
- **Work is NOT a state function** (it is a path function). ×2
- **Intensive property = temperature** (mass, volume and enthalpy are extensive).
- The property that depends on the **amount of matter** = **Gibbs free energy** (extensive);
 density, pressure and temperature do not.

**Enthalpy**
- **ΔH = ΔE + PΔV** — enthalpy relates to **internal energy**. ×2
- **Exothermic**: heat flows **system → surroundings**, ΔH is **negative**. ×2
- **Endothermic**: ΔH is **positive**, and **ΔH(reactants) < ΔH(products)**. ×2
- **Boiling water is endothermic**.
- **ΔH°f of an element in its standard state = zero**; thermodynamically the most stable form of
 carbon is **graphite**.
- In **2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂ + X kJ**, X kJ is the **heat of reaction** (also the heat of combustion of
 CO).
- **Activation energy of an endothermic reaction is more than ΔH**.
- The **activated complex** has energy **higher than both reactants and products**.
- A **bomb calorimeter** measures the enthalpy of **combustion**.
- **Sabatier–Senderens** (hydrogenation) is **exothermic**.

**Hess's law**
- **Hess's law**: the enthalpy change of a reaction is the **same whether it happens in one step or
 several**. ×6
- Hess's law is an application of the **first law of thermodynamics** / the principle of
 **conservation of energy**. ×2

**Entropy and free energy**
- **Entropy (S)** measures the **randomness / disorder** of a system.
- At **chemical equilibrium ΔG = 0**.
- A **spontaneous process** occurs without continuous input of energy from outside.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Coordinate covalent bond** = one atom donates **both** electrons for sharing. →u10
- Cooling the reversible exothermic **NH₃** formation makes **more product (NH₃)** form. →u6

**Spontaneity and reaction types**
- **Spontaneous**: the **emission of α-particles from polonium** (N₂ + O₂, thermal decomposition
 of marble and burning paper all need a trigger).
- **Exothermic** among the set = **burning methane** (P₂O₅ decomposition, atmospheric NO formation
 and dry-ice sublimation are not).
- Most ordinary-temperature reactions with **−ΔH are exothermic**.
- **Reactants have higher energy than products in exothermic reactions**; **ΔH is negative** there.
- Heat flows **spontaneously from hot to cold**; a hot object **loses internal energy** when it
 warms a cold one.

**Enthalpy definitions**
- **Enthalpy of neutralisation** = enthalpy change when **1 mole of water** forms from acid +
 alkali under standard conditions.
- **Standard enthalpy of formation of CO₂ = −394 kJ/mol**.
- **Enthalpy of atomisation of hydrogen**: ½H₂(g) → H(g), **ΔH = +218 kJ/mol**.
- In standard enthalpy of **atomisation** the surroundings' heat **increases** (energy is taken
 in).
- **Internal energy** = the sum of all forms of molecular energy in the system.
- **Volume is a state function**; ΔH is measured in **joules**.
- In an **isolated** system **neither heat nor mass** is transferred to the environment.
- Heat into a system with **no work done** → internal energy **increases**.
- 0.1 J into a gas expanding 0.1 m³ at 1 N/m² (does 0.1 J of work) → internal energy **remains the
 same**.
- For a **cyclic** process the change in internal energy is **zero in both** systems.
- Internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on the **change in temperature**.
- **Adiabatic** first law: **Q = 0, so W = −ΔU**.
- With heat added and work done **by** the system: **ΔU = Q − W**.
- Thermodynamics studies the **relations between heat and mechanical energy**.

**Hess's-law calculations**
- ΔHf(CO) = −26.4, ΔHf(CO₂) = −94.0 kcal → heat of combustion of CO = **−67.6 kcal**. ×2
- C + O₂ → CO₂ (−393.7); CO + ½O₂ → CO₂ (−283) → ΔHf of CO = **−110 kJ/mol**.
- Graphite ΔHc = −393.51, diamond ΔHc = −395.41 → graphite → diamond ΔH = **+1.91 kJ/mol**.
- 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O printed with ΔH = +285.5 is a sign typo in the source; the keyed option is
 **−205.5 kJ/mol**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃** heated shifts left: **SO₂ and O₂ increase, SO₃ decreases**. →u6
- Exothermic reactions are favoured by **decreasing temperature** (Le Chatelier). →u6
- STP = **14.7 psi and 0 °C**. →u3
- In a narrowing pipe an incompressible fluid speeds up to keep the **mass flow rate constant**,
 giving **high velocity, low pressure** (Bernoulli). →physics

## u9 — Electrochemistry (redox, cells, electrode potentials)

**Redox fundamentals**
- **Reduction** happens at the **cathode**; **oxidation** at the **anode** — in **both** galvanic
 and electrolytic cells. ×2
- In an **electrolytic** cell the **cathode is negative and reduction occurs** there.
- During **electrolysis, reduction always occurs at the cathode**.
- During **rusting**, iron behaves as the **anode** (it is oxidised).
- **NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ + ne⁻**: n = **2** (N goes +3 → +5). ×2
- **Oxidation number of oxygen in OF₂ = +2** (fluorine is more electronegative). ×2
- **Chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇ = +6**.
- **Disproportionation** = the same element both increases and decreases its oxidation state.
- **Vanadium mostly acts as an oxidizing agent**. ×6
- **Photovoltaic cell** is the odd one out — the electrochemical cells are electrolytic, voltaic
 and (the solar cell being the same device as photovoltaic, so the papers key **D voltaic** as
 the intended answer; treat this item as flawed and learn the three real cell types). ×6

**Oxidation states of the transition metals**
- The first transition series' oxidation state rises up to **Mn** (+7 max), then falls.
- **Maximum oxidation state of Mn = +7**.
- **Iron's two common states = +2 and +3**.
- **Mn²⁺ has the 3d⁵ configuration** in its +2 state.
- **Zero oxidation state** appears in **[Ni(CO)₄]** (the metal carbonyl).
- **Phosphorus** commonly shows **+3 and +5**.
- **Toughness of transition metals** comes from their **binding energies**.

**Cells and the salt bridge**
- The **salt bridge transfers ions** and **maintains electrical neutrality** in the half-cells,
 completing the circuit and preventing charge imbalance. ×3
- **Fuel cells** convert a continuous fuel supply's chemical energy directly into **electrical
 energy**; the H₂–O₂ fuel cell's final product is **H₂O**.
- A **secondary (rechargeable) cell** = the **lead-acid accumulator** (dry cell and silver-oxide
 cell are primary).
- **Electrolysis of water**: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ at the anode (and 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂ at the
 cathode).

**Electrode potentials and the series**
- The **standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)** is assigned **0.00 V**.
- **E°red** of an element is measured by comparing it with the **SHE**.
- **Stronger the reducing agent, the more negative (smaller) its reduction potential**.
- Of P(−3.05), Q(−1.66), R(+0.34), S(+0.80) V, the **strongest reducing agent is P** (most
 negative).
- Metals **above hydrogen** in the electrochemical series have **negative** electrode potentials
 and displace H₂ from dilute acids.
- An element with a **more negative** reduction potential than hydrogen sits **above hydrogen** in
 the series.
- **Alkali metals are strong reducing agents** because of their **low ionization energies**.
- **Fluorine's high electronegativity** comes from its **small size and high nuclear charge** in
 its period.

**Oxidation numbers — the ones that repeat**
- **Free magnesium's oxidation number = 0** (an element in its free state is always 0).
- **Sulfur in H₂SO₄ = +6**; **Na₂W₄O₁₃·H₂O: tungsten = +6**.
- **CrO₄²⁻ (+6), MnO₄²⁻ (+6), H₂SO₄ (+6), Na₂CO₃: carbon = +4** — the +4 species is **Na₂CO₃**.
- **CH₃OH → HCOOH**: carbon goes **−2 → 0**.
- **Cu + 4HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O**: copper goes **0 → +2**.
- Balanced **Cu + H₂SO₄**: **Cu + 2H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + 2H₂O**.
- In all oxidation reactions atoms **lose electrons and increase their oxidation state**.
- **H₂ is the reducing agent** (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇ and O₃ are oxidising).
- **K₂Cr₂O₇** is the oxidising agent that turns acetaldehyde into acetic acid.
- The strong oxidising halogen oxyacid = **HClO₄** (perchloric).
- **Group IB (coinage metals)**: they have **positive reduction potentials**, lie **below SHE**,
 and **cannot displace H₂ from acids** — the false claim is that they are powerful reducing
 agents.
- SHE's **platinum provides a surface for oxidation and reduction** (it does not react or supply
 voltage).
- Connecting SHE to a Cu electrode: **Cu²⁺ is reduced** (copper sits below hydrogen).
- A clear **redox** example = **H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl**; boiling water, dissolving NaCl and evaporating
 ethanol are not.
- **Not a redox reaction**: CaCO₃ → Ca + CO₃ (no oxidation-state change).
- **Blast furnace Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂**: to make 56 g Fe you use **160 g** of hematite ore.

**Electrolysis detail**
- An **electrolytic cell uses direct current**, not alternating — "alternating current source is
 connected" is the wrong feature.
- In electrolysis, current is carried by **both positive and negative ions**.
- Electrolysing aqueous **CuCl₂** deposits **copper** at the cathode.
- **Galvanizing** an iron sheet coats it with **zinc**.
- A salt bridge **allows movement of ions between the two half-cells**.
- Balancing redox by the oxidation-number method does **not** require splitting into two half-
 reactions (that is the ion-electron method).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Water's central oxygen has 4 electron pairs** in its valence shell (2 bonds + 2 lone pairs).
 →u10
- **Maximum electrons in a sub-shell = 2(2l+1)**; the formula 2l+1 counts orbitals. →u2
- **n = 4, l = 3 (4f) has 7 orientations**. →u2
- **Cl⁻ has 8 electrons in its outermost shell**. →u2
- **β-particle emission**: the electron comes from the **nucleus**, not an orbit. →u20
- **Kα photon energy: hf = E_L − E_K** (an L electron falling into the K hole). →u15

**More oxidation numbers**
- **Cl in MgCl₂ = −1**; **S in SO₄²⁻ = +6**; **Mn in KMnO₄ = +7**.
- **Mn oxidation states** in KMnO₄, K₂MnO₄, MnO₂, MnSO₄ = **+7, +6, +4, +2**.
- The pair with the **same Fe oxidation state** = **FeSO₄ and FeCl₂** (both +2).
- **2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl** is the redox reaction (AgNO₃ + NaCl is precipitation; the two half-
 reactions are not full redox equations).
- **Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu** is the **feasible** reaction by electrode potential (iron is above
 copper; Zn and Cd cannot displace Mg).
- In the SHE–Cu galvanic cell, current flows from the **copper electrode to the hydrogen
 electrode**.
- In **2MnO₄⁻ + 10Cl⁻ + 16H⁺ → 5Cl₂ + 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O**, **manganese is reduced from +7 to +2**.
- **Photochemical smog** = the build-up of oxidising agents (H₂O₂, HNO₃, PAN, ozone) in air.
- **Fe³⁺ has five unpaired electrons** in its ground state (3d⁵).
- A **redox reaction is identified by transfer of electrons**.

## u10 — Chemical Bonding (VSEPR, hybridisation, MOT, periodic trends)

**Shape and VSEPR**
- **CH₄** bond angle = **109.5°** (regular tetrahedral). ×2
- **3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair → trigonal pyramidal** (NH₃). ×2
- A triatomic molecule is either **linear (180°)** or **bent**. ×2
- **SF₆ is octahedral**; **PCl₅ is sp³d** (trigonal bipyramidal).

## u15 — Alkyl Halides (SN1, SN2, E1, E2)

**Substitution mechanisms**
- **SN2**: C–X breaks and C–Nu forms **simultaneously** (one step, backside attack, complete
 inversion). ×2
- **SN1**: goes through a **carbocation intermediate**; gives **50% inversion + 50% retention**
 (racemisation); its rate depends on the **concentration of substrate only**. ×2
- **SN1 reactivity: tertiary > secondary > primary** (stable carbocation).
- **SN2 reactivity: primary > secondary > tertiary** (least steric hindrance).
- **E1 is two steps; E2 is one step**.
- **Strong bases favour elimination over substitution**; **lower temperature favours
 substitution**; the leaving group matters in both.

**Leaving group and reactivity**
- **Reactivity of alkyl halides: R–I > R–Br > R–Cl > R–F** (weakest C–X bond = most reactive). ×3
- **Least reactive = R–F**; **most reactive = the iodo-compound** (lowest bond energy).
- **Ascending order of reactivity: F < Cl < Br < I**.
- **Dehydrohalogenation uses alcoholic KOH** (aqueous KOH gives substitution/alcohol). ×2
- **2-bromobutane + alcoholic KOH → 2-butene as the major product** (Saytzeff).

**Classifying alkyl halides**
- **2-bromo-2-methylbutane = tertiary**; **isopropyl chloride = secondary**.
- **Carbocation** = a carbon with a positive charge attached to three groups.
- **Nucleophile** = a species with an unshared electron pair (**H₂O is one**; AlCl₃, BF₃ are
 electrophiles/Lewis acids).
- **Acylium ion is an electrophile**.

**Named reactions**
- **Wurtz reaction**: alkyl halide → alkane with sodium in dry ether.
- **Grignard reagent RMgX is prepared in ether** (never aqueous — it reacts with water and with
 ethanol).
- **Alkyl halides → ethers when heated with Ag₂O / moist silver oxide**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Carbonyl reactivity toward nucleophiles: H₂CO > RCHO > R₂CO** (less steric hindrance). ×6
 →u17
- **Electron-withdrawing substituents increase phenol's acidity**; **phenol is more acidic than
 ethanol because its conjugate base is resonance-stabilised**. ×2 →u16
- **Pentan-2-one + HCN gives a chiral product** (cyanohydrin at a new stereocentre). →u17

**Nucleophiles and electrophiles**
- **Nucleophiles: NH₃, H₂O, CN⁻, NH₂⁻, CH₃⁻, Cl⁻** (they carry a lone pair or negative charge).
- **NOT a nucleophile = BF₃** (it is a Lewis acid / electrophile, as are NO₂⁺ and NH₄⁺).
- In **HCHO + HCN → cyanohydrin**, the attacking nucleophile is **CN⁻**.
- In **CH₃COOH + PCl₅**, the nucleophile that attacks the acid carbon is **Cl⁻**.
- **Secondary / dihaloalkanes can go by both SN1 and SN2**.
- **CH₃CH₂Br + alcoholic KOH → ethene follows the E2 mechanism**.
- **Reactivity toward nucleophiles: RI > RBr > RCl > RF**.

## u17 — Aldehydes & Ketones

**Preparation and general behaviour**
- **Methanol is produced by reduction of formaldehyde**. ×2
- **Formaldehyde + oxidizing mixture (H₂SO₄ + K₂Cr₂O₇) → methanoic acid**.
- **Oxidation of methyl alcohol can produce formaldehyde**; formaldehyde is a **gas** at room
 temperature (calling it a liquid is the incorrect statement); dry distillation of **calcium
 formate** also gives formaldehyde.
- The **carbonyl carbon is sp² hybridised**; the **reactivity of the carboxyl group is due to the
 carbonyl group**.
- **Formaldehyde is the aldehyde with maximum hydrate percentage** (no bulky group to hinder
 hydration) and is the **most reactive towards nucleophilic addition** (least steric hindrance).
- **CnH₂nO is the general formula of aldehydes and ketones**.
- A ketone has the structure **R–CO–R** (e.g. butan-2-one CH₃COCH₂CH₃); the carboxyl / amide /
 acyl-chloride structures are not ketones.

**Nucleophilic addition**
- **HCN with formaldehyde is a nucleophilic addition reaction** giving the cyanohydrin; the
 product of formaldehyde + HCN/NaCN has **–OH and –CN on the same sp³ carbon**. ×2
- **Reduction of aldehydes and ketones to an alkane**, carbonyl → CH₂, is the **Clemmensen
 reduction (Zn–Hg amalgam + conc. HCl)**. ×3
- **Wolff–Kishner reduction** converts carbonyl to alkane using **hydrazine (NH₂–NH₂) with KOH**
 in a high-boiling glycol — the "glycol with KOH" option. ×2
- **Catalytic reduction of aldehydes and ketones forms an alcohol** (H₂ over a catalyst).

**Aldol, Cannizzaro and Markownikov**
- In **aldol condensation and Cannizzaro reaction, NaOH acts as a base and a nucleophile**
 respectively (option "base and nucleophile").
- **In Cannizzaro's reaction formaldehyde reacts with NaOH** (concentrated base, no α‑H).
- Addition of an **unsymmetrical reagent to an unsymmetrical alkene follows Markownikov's rule**.

**Tests — Tollens, Fehling, iodoform, 2,4-DNPH**
- **Aldehydes give the silver-mirror / Tollen's test** (R–CHO + [Ag(NH₃)₂]OH → Ag mirror). ×2
- **Fehling's solution turns red with an aldehyde due to formation of Cu₂O**; positive aldehyde
 here is **acetaldehyde** (benzaldehyde gives no Fehling test). ×2
- **The iodoform test** (I₂ + NaOH/Na₂CO₃) is positive for the **CH₃–CO– group** and for
 **ethanol**: it is **negative for methanol (CH₃OH)** and for **pentan-3-one (CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃)**
 which has no CH₃CO. ×3
- **The yellow precipitate of the iodoform test is iodoform / triiodomethane (CHI₃)**; a positive
 test is indicated by **formation of yellow crystals** (ethanol). ×2
- **Aldehydes do NOT give the sodium nitroprusside test** (that is for ketones / acetone).
- **Ketones do NOT show reaction with Fehling's solution** (aldehydes do); they **can give the
 2,4-DNPH, hydrazone and reduction-to-alcohol reactions**.
- **The test given by BOTH aldehydes and ketones is the 2,4-DNPH test** (hydrazone formation);
 both react with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine to give **2,4-dinitrophenyl hydrazones**. ×4
- **Acetone + 2,4-DNPH → 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone**; the hydrazone of acetone carries two CH₃
 groups, the hydrazone of acetaldehyde carries **one CH₃ and one H** on the C=N carbon.
- **Ethanol gives a positive iodoform reaction; methanol and 1-propanol do not**; the alcohol
 shown by crystals in the iodoform test is **ethanol**.

## u18 — Carboxylic Acids

**Acidity, strength and nomenclature**
- **Alcohol + carboxylic acid → esterification reaction**; heating a carboxylic acid with an

- **Relative acid strength order: carboxylic acid > phenol > water > ethanol**.
- **A carboxylic acid is a stronger acid than CH₃OH** (alcohol).
- Carboxylic acids react with **carbonates/bicarbonates** to liberate **CO₂** (a colourless gas);

 **CH₃CH₂COOH**. ×2
- **pKa of CH₃COOH = 4.74**, and an **equimolar buffer of acetic acid + sodium acetate has pH =
 pKa = 4.74** (NUMS).

 name is given as **oxo + oic acid**.
- **Common name of pentanoic acid = valeric acid**; **2-hydroxy propanoic acid = lactic acid**;
 **formic acid is found in ants**. ×2
- **CnH₂nO is the general formula of ketones (and aldehydes), not of carboxylic acids**; the
 simplest parent of the aromatic class is **benzene**; oxidation of acetone gives **acetic acid**
 and **acetone is made by dry distillation of calcium acetate**. ×2

**Structure and physical properties**
- **The strongest electrolyte in solution is potassium iodide** (acetic / ammonium hydroxide /
 carbonic acid are weak).
- The amide linkage joining amino acids is the **peptide bond**; both functional groups present in
 every amino acid are **carboxylic acid and amino**; the **–NH–CO– linkage is the peptide
 linkage**. ×2
- **Least volatile compound is glycerol** (high H-bonding); carboxylic acid solubility in water
 **decreases as molecular mass rises**.
- **Highest melting/boiling member of the straight-chain series here is pentanoic acid**.

**Preparations and reactions**
- **Oxidation of methanal (HCHO) → formic acid** (methanoic acid). ×2
- **Reduction of acetic acid / carboxylic acids with LiAlH₄ → ethanol (alcohol)**. ×2
- **Zn–Hg / conc. HCl (Clemmensen) reduces acetaldehyde to ethane** (an alkane). ×2
- **Hydrolysis of nitriles (methyl cyanide, CH₃CN + HCl) → acetic acid**; methyl cyanides boiled
 with mineral acids give **acetic acid**. ×2
- **Acetic acid can be prepared by hydrolysis of methyl cyanide**; **acetone is made by dry
 distillation of calcium acetate**; **oxidation of acetone → acetic acid**. ×2
- **RMgX (Grignard) + CO₂ → carboxylic acid**. ×2
- **Carboxylic acid + SOCl₂ (thionyl chloride) → acyl (acid) halide, CH₃COCl + SO₂ + HCl**;
 thionyl chloride + acetic acid gives the acid. ×2
- **Carboxylic acid + ammonia → ammonium salt, which on heating gives an amide (acetamide)**;
 acetamide is formed by dehydration of the ammonium salt of ethanoic acid. ×2
- **Carboxylic acid + P₂O₅ (dehydration) → acetic anhydride**; **carboxylic acid + PCl₅** also
 gives acyl chloride with HCl.
- **Formic acid + ethanol + acid catalyst → ethyl formate (an ester)**; **phenol + acetyl
 chloride → an ester**; **ethanol + conc. H₂SO₄ at 140 °C → diethyl ether**. ×2
- **Ethyl butyrate (butyl butanoate) esters have a pineapple flavour**.
- **Carboxylic acid + alcohol + acid → ester** — this **esterification is a reversible**

- **CH₃CN + HCl + H₂O → acetic acid + ammonium chloride**.
- The **acid produced when pyruvate is oxidised/decarboxylated before entering Krebs' cycle is
 acetyl (acetic) acid**, formed in the **mitochondrial matrix**.

**Zwitter ion and fats**

- **Fats (glycerides) are esters of long-chain acids with propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol)**.
