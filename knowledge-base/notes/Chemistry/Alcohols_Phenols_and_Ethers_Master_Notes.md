## 18.1 Alcohols ($R-OH$)

### 18.1.1 & 18.1.2 Nomenclature and Physical Properties
- **Nomenclature:** Suffix "-ol" (IUPAC) or "Alkyl alcohol" (Trivial). e.g., $CH_3CH_2OH$ is Ethanol or Ethyl alcohol.
- **Physical Properties:** 
 - Extremely high boiling points compared to alkanes of similar mass because they form strong **intermolecular Hydrogen bonds**.
 - Lower alcohols (1-3 carbons) are completely soluble in water because they form H-bonds with water molecules. Solubility decreases as the hydrophobic alkyl chain gets longer.

### 18.1.3 The Lucas Test (Distinguishing $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ Alcohols)
- **Reagent:** Anhydrous $ZnCl_2$ dissolved in concentrated $HCl$.
- **Reaction:** Converts soluble alcohols into insoluble alkyl chlorides, which appear as a cloudy/milky suspension.
 $R-OH + HCl \xrightarrow{ZnCl_2} R-Cl \text{ (insoluble)} + H_2O$
- **Diagnostic Results:**
 - **$3^\circ$ Alcohols:** Cloudiness appears **immediately** (reaction is very fast via $S_N1$).
 - **$2^\circ$ Alcohols:** Cloudiness appears after **5 to 10 minutes**.
 - **$1^\circ$ Alcohols:** **No cloudiness** at room temperature (reaction only occurs upon heating).

### 18.1.4 The Iodoform Test (Haloform Reaction)
- **Purpose:** Used to detect the presence of the specific $CH_3-CH(OH)-$ group.
- **Reagents:** Iodine ($I_2$) and Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$).
- **Distinguishing Methanol vs Ethanol:**
 - **Ethanol ($CH_3CH_2OH$):** Contains the required grouping. Gives a **POSITIVE** test, forming a distinct **yellow precipitate** of Iodoform ($CHI_3$).
 - **Methanol ($CH_3OH$):** Lacks the required grouping. Gives a **NEGATIVE** test (solution remains clear).

### 18.1.5 Preparation by Reduction (Using Equations)
1. **From Aldehydes:** Yields a $1^\circ$ Alcohol.
 $R-CHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{NaBH_4 \text{ or } LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH$
2. **From Ketones:** Yields a $2^\circ$ Alcohol.
 $R-CO-R' + 2[H] \xrightarrow{NaBH_4 \text{ or } LiAlH_4} R-CH(OH)-R'$
3. **From Carboxylic Acids:** Yields a $1^\circ$ Alcohol. (Requires strong $LiAlH_4$).
 $R-COOH + 4[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH + H_2O$
4. **From Esters:** Cleaves the ester to yield a mixture of *two* alcohols.
 $R-COOR' + 4[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} R-CH_2OH + R'-OH$

### 18.1.6 & 18.1.7 Acidity and Reactions
- **Acidic Character:** Alcohols are **extremely weak acids** (even weaker than water). However, they *will* react with active metals to release Hydrogen gas.
 $2ROH + 2Na \to 2RONa \text{ (Sodium alkoxide)} + H_2\uparrow$
- **a. Preparation of Ethers (Intermolecular Dehydration):**
 $2C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. } H_2SO_4, \mathbf{140^\circ C}} C_2H_5-O-C_2H_5 + H_2O$
 *(Note the specific temperature: $140^\circ\text{C}$ gives the ether, while $170^\circ\text{C}$ gives the alkene via elimination).*
- **b. Preparation of Esters (Esterification):**
 $R-COOH + R'-OH \xrightleftharpoons{\text{Conc. } H_2SO_4 \text{ catalyst}} R-COOR' + H_2O$
- **c. Oxidative Cleavage of 1,2-diols:**
 Periodic acid ($HIO_4$) specifically cleaves the $C-C$ bond lying between two adjacent $-OH$ groups, splitting the molecule into two carbonyl compounds (aldehydes or ketones).

### 18.1.8 & 18.1.9 Thiols and Uses
- **Define Thiols ($R-SH$):** The sulfur analogues of alcohols. Also known as "mercaptans". They are infamous for having extremely strong, foul, skunk-like odors (used as an additive in natural gas to detect leaks).
- **Uses:** 70% ethanol or isopropanol is heavily used in medicine as a topical **antiseptic and disinfectant**, working by rapidly denaturing bacterial proteins and dissolving their lipid membranes.

## 18.2 Phenols ($C_6H_5OH$)

### 18.2.2 Acidic Characteristics (Resonance Stabilization)
- **Crucial Concept:** Phenol is a **MUCH stronger acid** than aliphatic alcohols (though weaker than carboxylic acids).
- **Reason:** When an alcohol loses a proton ($H^+$), the resulting alkoxide ion ($RO^-$) concentrates the negative charge heavily on the oxygen. When Phenol loses a proton, it forms the **Phenoxide ion ($C_6H_5O^-$)**. The lone pair on the oxygen delocalizes completely into the benzene ring via **Resonance**. This immense stabilization of the conjugate base makes it much easier for phenol to drop the proton in the first place.

### 18.2.3 Preparation of Phenols (Using Equations)
1. **From Benzene Sulphonic Acid:**
 $C_6H_5SO_3Na + 2NaOH \xrightarrow{\text{Fuse at } 300^\circ\text{C}} C_6H_5ONa + Na_2SO_3 + H_2O$
 $C_6H_5ONa + HCl \to C_6H_5OH + NaCl$
2. **From Chlorobenzene (Dow Process):**
 $C_6H_5Cl + 2NaOH \xrightarrow{300^\circ\text{C}, 200 \text{ atm}} C_6H_5ONa + NaCl + H_2O$
 $C_6H_5ONa + HCl \to C_6H_5OH + NaCl$
3. **Acidic Oxidation of Cumene (Industrial Method):**
 $C_6H_5CH(CH_3)_2 \text{ (Cumene)} + O_2 \to \text{Cumene Hydroperoxide}$
 $\text{Cumene Hydroperoxide} \xrightarrow{H_2SO_4, H_2O} C_6H_5OH \text{ (Phenol)} + CH_3COCH_3 \text{ (Acetone byproduct)}$
4. **Hydrolysis of Diazonium Salts:**
 $C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{Warm to } 50^\circ\text{C}} C_6H_5OH + N_2\uparrow + HCl$

### 18.2.4 Reactivity of Phenol
- **Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution:** The $-OH$ group is a massive **Ring Activator** and **Ortho/Para director** (due to lone pair resonance putting negative charge on the ring).
 - *Bromination:* Reacts instantly with Bromine Water (no catalyst needed) to form a **white precipitate** of 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
 - *Nitration:* Reacts with concentrated $HNO_3$ to form 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (**Picric Acid**, a powerful explosive).
- **With Sodium Metal:** $2C_6H_5OH + 2Na \to 2C_6H_5ONa + H_2\uparrow$
- **Oxidation:** Phenol is highly susceptible to oxidation. Reacting it with Chromic acid ($Na_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4$) oxidizes it to a conjugated diketone called **benzoquinone**.

### 18.2.5 Differentiate Between Alcohols and Phenols
*High-Yield distinction tests:*
1. **Solubility in Base:** Phenol is acidic enough to dissolve completely in aqueous $NaOH$. Alcohols do NOT dissolve in $NaOH$.
2. **Ferric Chloride ($FeCl_3$) Test:** Phenol reacts with $FeCl_3$ to form an intensely colored **deep violet/purple complex**. Alcohols show no color change.
3. **Bromine Water Test:** Phenol instantly decolorizes bromine water, generating a **white precipitate**. Alcohols do not react.

## 18.3 Ethers ($R-O-R'$)

### 18.3.1 & 18.3.2 Nomenclature and Properties
- **Nomenclature:** "Alkoxyalkane" (e.g., Methoxyethane) or "Dialkyl ether" (e.g., Ethyl methyl ether).
- **Physical/Chemical Properties:** Ethers are highly volatile, incredibly flammable liquids. Because they lack any hydrogen attached to oxygen (no active hydrogen), they do **not** form hydrogen bonds with themselves (low boiling points) and are chemically **inert** to bases, active metals, and oxidizing agents. This makes them superb organic solvents.

### 18.3.3 Preparation of Ethers (Using Equations)
**a. Williamson Synthesis (The Best Method):**
- *Reaction:* An alkoxide ion attacks an alkyl halide via $S_N2$. Perfect for making unsymmetrical ethers.
- *Equation:* $RONa + R'X \to R-O-R' + NaX$
- * Trap:* The alkyl halide ($R'X$) **MUST** be primary ($1^\circ$). If a $3^\circ$ alkyl halide is used, the alkoxide acts as a base and causes an Elimination reaction (forming an alkene) instead of Substitution.

**b. Reaction of Alkyl Halides with Dry Silver Oxide:**
- *Equation:* $2RX + Ag_2O \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-O-R + 2AgX$

**c. Reaction of Alcohols with Excess $H_2SO_4$:**
- *Equation:* $2ROH \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. } H_2SO_4, 140^\circ\text{C}} R-O-R + H_2O$

### 18.3.4 Medical Use of Ether
Historically, **Diethyl ether** was revolutionary as one of the first widely used **volatile general anesthetics** in surgery (inhaled to induce unconsciousness). It is rarely used today in modern medicine due to its extreme flammability (explosive hazard in operating rooms) and tendency to cause severe post-operative nausea.
