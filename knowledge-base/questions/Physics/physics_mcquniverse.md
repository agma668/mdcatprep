## Force & Motion

### Q1. A body moves 6 m north, 8 m east, and 10 m vertically upwards. What is the magnitude of the resultant displacement from the initial position?
A.) 10 m
B.) 12 m
C.) 24 m
D.) 10√2 m **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The resultant displacement is found using the 3D Pythagorean theorem: d = √(x² + y² + z²) = √(8² + 6² + 10²) = √(64 + 36 + 100) = √200 = 10√2 m.

### Q2. The area under a velocity-time graph represents:
A.) Acceleration
B.) Instantaneous velocity
C.) Jerk
D.) Displacement **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Since velocity is displacement per unit time, the product of velocity and time (the area under the graph) gives the total displacement.

### Q3. A projectile is thrown with an initial velocity of 10 m/s at an angle of 60° with the horizontal. What is the horizontal component of its velocity at the highest point?
A.) 0 m/s
B.) 8.66 m/s
C.) 10 m/s
D.) 5 m/s **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The horizontal component of velocity (v_x = v cosθ) remains constant throughout projectile motion. So, v_x = 10 * cos(60°) = 10 * 0.5 = 5 m/s.

### Q4. According to Newton's first law of motion, an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless:
A.) It is very heavy.
B.) It has momentum.
C.) Its energy is conserved.
D.) An external force acts on it. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Newton's first law, the law of inertia, states that an object's state of motion will not change unless acted upon by a net external force.

### Q5. A force of 10 N acts on a body of mass 2 kg. What is the acceleration produced?
A.) 20 m/s²
B.) 0.2 m/s²
C.) 8 m/s²
D.) 5 m/s² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — According to Newton's second law, F = ma. Therefore, acceleration a = F/m = 10 N / 2 kg = 5 m/s².

### Q6. Impulse is defined as:
A.) The rate of change of momentum.
B.) The product of mass and acceleration.
C.) The product of force and velocity.
D.) The change in momentum. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The impulse-momentum theorem states that the impulse (FΔt) applied to an object is equal to the change in its momentum (Δp).

### Q7. In an elastic collision between two bodies:
A.) Only momentum is conserved.
B.) Only kinetic energy is conserved.
C.) Neither momentum nor kinetic energy is conserved.
D.) Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The defining characteristic of a perfectly elastic collision is the conservation of both total momentum and total kinetic energy of the system.

### Q8. A bullet of mass 20 g is fired from a gun of mass 10 kg with a velocity of 150 m/s. What is the recoil velocity of the gun?
A.) -3 m/s
B.) 0.3 m/s
C.) 3 m/s
D.) -0.3 m/s **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — By conservation of momentum, m_gun * v_gun + m_bullet * v_bullet = 0. So, v_gun = -(m_bullet * v_bullet) / m_gun = -(0.02 kg * 150 m/s) / 10 kg = -0.3 m/s.

### Q9. The work done is zero if the angle between the force and displacement is:
A.) 0°
B.) 45°
C.) 180°
D.) 90° **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Work is calculated as W = Fd cosθ. Since cos(90°) = 0, the work done is zero when the force is perpendicular to the displacement.

### Q10. The kinetic energy of a body of mass 'm' moving with velocity 'v' is:
A.) mv²
B.) ½mv
C.) mgh
D.) ½mv² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The standard formula for kinetic energy, the energy of motion, is KE = ½mv².

### Q11. The slope of a displacement-time graph gives:
A.) Acceleration
B.) Distance
C.) Force
D.) Velocity **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Velocity is the rate of change of displacement (v = Δd/Δt), which corresponds to the slope of the displacement-time graph.

### Q12. Which of the following is a vector quantity?
A.) Speed
B.) Distance
C.) Mass
D.) Momentum **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Momentum (p = mv) has both magnitude and direction, making it a vector quantity. Speed, distance, and mass are scalars.

### Q13. A car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 10 seconds. The distance it covers in this time is:
A.) 50 m
B.) 200 m
C.) 2 m
D.) 100 m **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — First, find acceleration: a = Δv/t = 20/10 = 2 m/s². Then use s = v_i*t + ½at² = 0*10 + ½(2)(10)² = 100 m.

### Q14. Action and reaction forces described by Newton's third law:
A.) Act on the same body.
B.) Cancel each other out.
C.) Are always perpendicular to each other.
D.) Are always equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Newton's third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. These forces act on different bodies.

### Q15. The rate of doing work is called:
A.) Energy
B.) Momentum
C.) Impulse
D.) Power **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Power is defined as the work done per unit of time (P = W/t), measured in Watts (Joules/second).

### Q16. For a projectile, the angle of projection for maximum horizontal range is:
A.) 30°
B.) 60°
C.) 90°
D.) 45° **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The range formula R = (v_i² sin(2θ))/g is maximized when sin(2θ) = 1, which occurs when 2θ = 90°, so θ = 45°.

### Q17. A body of mass 5 kg is lifted vertically to a height of 2 m. The work done against gravity is (take g = 9.8 m/s²):
A.) 10 J
B.) 49 J
C.) 19.6 J
D.) 98 J **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Work done against gravity is equal to the gain in potential energy: W = PE = mgh = 5 kg * 9.8 m/s² * 2 m = 98 J.

### Q18. Which of the following remains constant for a body in uniform circular motion?
A.) Velocity
B.) Acceleration
C.) Momentum
D.) Speed **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — In uniform circular motion, the direction of velocity is constantly changing, but its magnitude (speed) remains constant.

### Q19. The S.I. unit of impulse is:
A.) N m
B.) N/s
C.) kg m/s²
D.) N s **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Impulse is Force × time, so its unit is the Newton-second (N s). It is equivalent to the unit of momentum, kg m/s.

### Q20. An object of mass 'm' falls from rest from a height 'h'. Its velocity just before striking the ground is:
A.) mgh
B.) 2gh
C.) √(gh)
D.) √(2gh) **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — By conservation of energy, initial PE = final KE. So, mgh = ½mv². Solving for v gives v = √(2gh).

### Q21. The time rate of change of linear momentum of a body is equal to:
A.) Its acceleration
B.) Its impulse
C.) Its kinetic energy
D.) The applied force **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Newton's second law can be stated as F = Δp/Δt, meaning the net force is equal to the rate of change of momentum.

### Q22. If the velocity of a body is doubled, its kinetic energy will be:
A.) Halved
B.) Doubled
C.) Unchanged
D.) Four times the original value **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Kinetic energy (KE = ½mv²) is proportional to the square of the velocity. If v becomes 2v, KE becomes ½m(2v)² = 4(½mv²).

### Q23. In an inelastic collision:
A.) Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.
B.) Kinetic energy is conserved but momentum is not.
C.) Neither momentum nor kinetic energy is conserved.
D.) Momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — In any collision within an isolated system, momentum is conserved. In an inelastic collision, some kinetic energy is converted to other forms (heat, sound).

### Q24. A body is moving with constant velocity. The net force on it is:
A.) In the direction of motion
B.) Opposite to the direction of motion
C.) Perpendicular to the direction of motion
D.) Zero **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Constant velocity means acceleration is zero. According to F=ma, if acceleration is zero, the net force must also be zero.

### Q25. The trajectory of a projectile (in the absence of air resistance) is a:
A.) Straight line
B.) Circle
C.) Hyperbola
D.) Parabola **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The combination of constant horizontal velocity and constant vertical acceleration due to gravity results in a parabolic path.

### Q26. What is the work done by the centripetal force on a body moving in a circle?
A.) Positive
B.) Negative
C.) Infinite
D.) Zero **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The centripetal force is always perpendicular to the direction of motion (the velocity vector). Since W = Fd cos(90°), the work done is zero.

### Q27. A 1 kg object has a potential energy of 1 J relative to the ground. What is its height? (take g = 9.8 m/s²)
A.) 1 m
B.) 9.8 m
C.) 10 m
D.) 0.102 m **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — From PE = mgh, we get h = PE / (mg) = 1 J / (1 kg * 9.8 m/s²) ≈ 0.102 m.

### Q28. The law of conservation of momentum applies to a system of colliding objects only if:
A.) The objects are of equal mass.
B.) The collision is elastic.
C.) The objects stick together after collision.
D.) There is no net external force on the system. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The conservation of linear momentum is a fundamental principle that holds for any isolated system, meaning a system with no net external force acting on it.

### Q29. If the momentum of a body is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes:
A.) Double
B.) Half
C.) Constant
D.) Four times **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Using the relationship KE = p²/(2m), if momentum 'p' is doubled to '2p', the new kinetic energy becomes (2p)²/(2m) = 4p²/(2m), which is four times the original.

### Q30. Instantaneous velocity is the limiting value of the average velocity when the time interval approaches:
A.) Infinity
B.) A constant value
C.) One second
D.) Zero **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Instantaneous velocity is the derivative of displacement with respect to time, which is defined as the limit of the average velocity as Δt → 0.

### Q31. Which of the following effects cannot be produced by an applied force?
A.) Change the speed
B.) Change the direction of motion
C.) Change the shape of the body
D.) Change the mass of the body **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — In classical mechanics, mass is an intrinsic, constant property of an object. A force can change its motion or shape, but not its mass.

### Q32. A force of 5 N acts on a body for 0.1 s. The change in its momentum is:
A.) 50 kg m/s
B.) 5 kg m/s
C.) 0.02 kg m/s
D.) 0.5 kg m/s **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The change in momentum is equal to the impulse, which is Force × time. Δp = 5 N * 0.1 s = 0.5 Ns or 0.5 kg m/s.

### Q33. A stone is dropped from a tower. It will reach the ground in 4 s. The height of the tower is (take g = 9.8 m/s²):

### Q34. The range of a projectile is the same for angles of projection:
A.) 30° and 45°
B.) 45° and 90°

**Answer: D** — The horizontal range of a projectile is the same for complementary angles of projection (angles that add up to 90°), such as 30° and 60°.

### Q35. The work-energy theorem states that the work done on an object is equal to the change in its:
A.) Potential energy
B.) Momentum
C.) Total energy
D.) Kinetic energy **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The work-energy theorem provides a direct link between the net work done on an object and the change in its kinetic energy (W_net = ΔKE).

### Q36. Horsepower is a unit of:

**Answer: D** — Horsepower is a non-SI unit of power, where 1 hp is approximately equal to 746 watts.

### Q37. If a lighter and a heavier body have the same momentum, which one has greater kinetic energy?

C.) It cannot be determined
D.) The lighter body **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Using KE = p²/(2m), for a constant momentum 'p', kinetic energy is inversely proportional to mass. Thus, the lighter body (smaller m) has more kinetic energy.

### Q38. A ball is thrown vertically upward. At the maximum height:

### Q39. Which law of motion gives the measure of force?
A.) First law

**Answer: D** — Newton's second law (F=ma) provides a quantitative relationship to measure force based on the mass and acceleration of an object.

### Q40. A body starts from rest and moves with uniform acceleration. The ratio of the distance covered in the 1st second to the distance covered in the 2nd second is:
A.) 1:1
B.) 1:2
C.) 1:4

**Answer: D** — The distance covered in the nth second is proportional to (2n-1). For n=1, it's 1. For n=2, it's 3. Thus, the ratio is 1:3.

### Q41. A force can be completely described by its:
A.) Magnitude only
B.) Direction only
C.) Magnitude and point of application

**Answer: D** — Force is a vector quantity, and as such, it requires both a magnitude (how strong it is) and a direction to be fully described.

### Q42. The 'reaction' force does not cancel the 'action' force because:

B.) They are in the same direction.
C.) The reaction force exists only after the action force is removed.

**Answer: D** — Action-reaction pairs act on the two different interacting bodies. Forces can only cancel out if they act on the same body.

### Q43. An explosion breaks a rock into three pieces. Which of the following is conserved?
A.) Kinetic energy
B.) Velocity
C.) Mass
D.) Momentum **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — An explosion is caused by internal forces. In the absence of external forces, the total momentum of the system (the rock pieces) is conserved.

### Q44. At what angle must two forces of equal magnitude act so that the resultant is equal to either of them?
A.) 45°
B.) 60°
C.) 90°
D.) 120° **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Using the law of cosines for vector addition, R² = A² + B² + 2ABcosθ. If R=A=B, then A² = A² + A² + 2A²cosθ, which simplifies to cosθ = -1/2. Thus, θ = 120°.

### Q45. A lift is moving up with an acceleration 'a'. The apparent weight of a person of mass 'm' will be:
A.) mg
B.) m(g-a)
C.) 0
D.) m(g+a) **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The normal force (apparent weight) must support the true weight (mg) and also provide the upward acceleration (ma). Thus, N = mg + ma = m(g+a).

### Q46. When a body moves from the equator to the poles, its weight:
A.) Decreases
B.) Remains the same

D.) Increases **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The value of 'g' is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator due to the Earth's shape and rotation. Since Weight = mg, the weight increases.

### Q47. The time of flight of a projectile is given by:

### Q48. Work done by a variable force is determined by:
A.) Multiplying force by distance
B.) The slope of the force-displacement graph
C.) The product of force and time
D.) The area under the force-displacement graph **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — For a variable force, the work done is the integral of F with respect to displacement, which is geometrically represented by the area under the Force-Displacement graph.

### Q49. A conservative force is one where the work done:
A.) Depends on the path taken.
B.) Is always zero.
C.) Is always positive.
D.) Is independent of the path taken. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The work done by a conservative force (like gravity) between two points depends only on the initial and final positions, not on the path followed between them.

### Q50. The escape velocity from the Earth's surface is approximately:

### Q51. If the displacement of a particle is zero, the distance covered:

**Answer: D** — Displacement is a vector (start to end point), while distance is a scalar (total path). An object can travel a non-zero distance and return to its starting point, making its displacement zero while distance non zero.

### Q56. Which of Newton's laws of motion is also called the law of inertia?

### Q57. A force of 100 N acts on a body of mass 2 kg for 10 s. The change in momentum is:

### Q58. In a perfectly inelastic collision, the coefficient of restitution 'e' is:

B.) Between 0 and 1

**Answer: D** — In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects stick together after impact, meaning their relative velocity of separation is zero. The coefficient of restitution e is the ratio of relative separation velocity to relative approach velocity, so e=0.

### Q59. The maximum height of a projectile is given by:

### Q60. A person is holding a bucket of water and walking on a level road with a uniform velocity. The work done by the person on the bucket is:

**Answer: D** — The force exerted by the person is vertically upwards to counteract gravity, while the displacement is horizontal. The angle between them is 90°, and since W = Fd cos(90°), the work done is zero.

### Q61. Power can be defined as the dot product of:
A.) Force and displacement

D.) Force and velocity **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Since Power = Work/time = (Force ⋅ displacement)/time, and velocity = displacement/time, Power = Force ⋅ velocity.

### Q62. Which of the following is a non-conservative force?

B.) Elastic spring force

**Answer: D** — A non-conservative force is one where the work done depends on the path taken. Friction is a classic example as it dissipates mechanical energy as heat.

### Q63. Two bodies of masses m1 and m2 have equal kinetic energies. The ratio of their linear momenta is:

### Q64. The horizontal range of a projectile is maximum when the angle of projection is 45°. The maximum height at this angle is:

### Q65. A bomb of mass 9 kg explodes into two pieces of masses 3 kg and 6 kg. The velocity of the 3 kg mass is 16 m/s. The kinetic energy of the 6 kg mass is:

### Q67. The unit of weight in the S.I. system is:

### Q69. A cricketer catches a ball of mass 150 g moving at 20 m/s. If the catching process takes 0.1 s, the average force exerted is:
A.) 3 N

**Answer: D** — Force is the rate of change of momentum. F = Δp/Δt = (mΔv)/Δt = (0.15 kg * 20 m/s) / 0.1 s = 30 N.

### Q70. A car travels the first half of a distance at 30 km/hr and the second half at 50 km/hr. The average speed is:

**Answer: D** — Average speed for equal distances is the harmonic mean: V_avg = 2v1v2 / (v1 + v2) = 2(30)(50) / (30 + 50) = 3000 / 80 = 37.5 km/hr.

### Q71. A body under the action of several forces will have zero acceleration if:

**Answer: D** — According to Newton's Second Law (F_net = ma), if the net force (the vector sum of all forces) is zero, the acceleration must be zero. This is the condition for equilibrium.

### Q72. If an object is moving at a constant velocity, which statement must be true?

**Answer: D** — Constant velocity means zero acceleration. From F=ma, if acceleration is zero, the net force on the object must also be zero.

### Q73. A 10 N force is required to keep an object moving at a constant velocity of 2 m/s. The work done in 5 seconds is:
A.) 20 J
B.) 50 J
C.) 25 J
D.) 100 J **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — First, find the distance: d = v × t = 2 m/s × 5 s = 10 m. Then, work done W = F × d = 10 N × 10 m = 100 J.

### Q74. Which of the following pairs of physical quantities have the same dimensions?

**Answer: D** — Momentum (p = mv) and Impulse (I = FΔt) both have the dimensions [MLT⁻¹]. The impulse-momentum theorem states that they are equivalent.

### Q76. What is the angle between the velocity and acceleration vectors of a body in uniform circular motion?

### Q78. Two projectiles are fired from the same point with the same speed at angles of 60° and 30°. Which will be the same for both?

**Answer: D** — The kinetic energy due to rotation is given by K.E_rot = ½Iω², which is analogous to the linear kinetic energy formula K.E = ½mv².

### Q161. An object rolling down an incline without slipping has:
A.) Only rotational kinetic energy
B.) Only translational kinetic energy
C.) Only potential energy
D.) Both translational and rotational kinetic energy **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — A rolling object is both moving from one place to another (translation) and spinning (rotation), so its total kinetic energy is the sum of both forms.

### Q162. Which object will reach the bottom of an incline first if released from rest, a solid sphere or a hollow sphere of the same mass and radius?

**Answer: D** — The solid sphere has a smaller moment of inertia than the hollow sphere. This means less of its potential energy is converted to rotational KE and more to translational KE, making it faster.

### Q163. The critical velocity of a satellite in a low Earth orbit is also known as:

**Answer: D** — The specific speed required to maintain a stable orbit at a certain altitude is called orbital velocity. For low-Earth orbit, this is about 7.9 km/s.

### Q164. In a geostationary orbit, a satellite's orbital period is:
A.) 12 hours
B.) 90 minutes
C.) 365 days

**Answer: D** — A geostationary satellite orbits above the equator with an orbital period of exactly 24 hours, causing it to appear stationary from the ground.

### Q165. The sensation of weightlessness in a satellite is due to:
A.) The absence of gravity
B.) The balancing of gravitational and centrifugal forces
C.) The high speed of the satellite
D.) The satellite being in a constant state of free-fall **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Objects in orbit are continuously falling towards the central body but have enough tangential velocity to miss it. This state of constant free-fall results in the feeling of weightlessness.

### Q166. The concept of 'artificial gravity' in a space station can be created by:

**Answer: D** — By spinning a ring-shaped space station, the normal force exerted by the inner floor on an astronaut provides the necessary centripetal force, simulating the sensation of gravity.

A.) Mass
B.) Color
C.) Altitude

**Answer: D** — The simulated gravity is the centripetal acceleration (a_c = rω²). To achieve a_c = g, the required angular velocity (ω), and thus frequency, depends on the radius 'r' of the station.

### Q168. What happens to the weight of a person in a lift accelerating downwards?
A.) It increases
B.) It remains the same
C.) It becomes zero

**Answer: D** — The apparent weight (normal force) is N = m(g-a). When accelerating down, the apparent weight is less than the true weight.

### Q169. A car is moving on a banked road. The necessary centripetal force is provided by:
A.) Friction only

**Answer: D** — On a banked road, the road is tilted. The normal force is perpendicular to the road surface, and its horizontal component points towards the center of the curve, providing the centripetal force.

### Q170. A flywheel is used in machines to:
A.) Increase the speed
B.) Decrease the speed
C.) Reduce the machine's weight

**Answer: D** — A flywheel has a large moment of inertia and is used to store rotational kinetic energy, resisting changes in speed and ensuring smoother operation.

### Q171. The unit of angular acceleration is:
A.) rad/s
B.) rev/s
C.) m/s²
D.) rad/s² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Angular acceleration (α) is the rate of change of angular velocity, so its unit is radians per second per second, or rad/s².

### Q172. If the angular velocity of an object changes from 2 rad/s to 10 rad/s in 4 seconds, its angular acceleration is:
A.) 8 rad/s²
B.) 4 rad/s²
C.) 3 rad/s²
D.) 2 rad/s² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Angular acceleration α = Δω/Δt = (10 - 2) rad/s / 4 s = 8/4 = 2 rad/s².

### Q173. Tangential acceleration in circular motion is responsible for:
A.) Changing the direction of motion
B.) Keeping the object in a circle
C.) Both A and C
D.) Changing the speed of motion **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — While centripetal acceleration changes the direction, tangential acceleration (a_t = rα) acts along the tangent to the path and is responsible for changing the object's speed.

### Q174. A body in uniform circular motion has:
A.) Constant velocity
B.) Constant acceleration
C.) Zero acceleration
D.) Constant speed **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — In uniform circular motion, the magnitude of the velocity (speed) is constant, but the direction of the velocity vector is continuously changing, resulting in a non-zero centripetal acceleration.

### Q175. The moment of inertia of a thin hoop of mass M and radius R about its center is:
A.) ½MR²
B.) ⅖MR²
C.) ⅔MR²
D.) MR² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — For a thin hoop or ring, all the mass (M) is located at the same distance (R) from the axis of rotation, so its moment of inertia is I = MR².

### Q176. The term 'centrifugal force' is often described as a:
A.) Real force directed inwards
B.) Real force directed outwards
C.) Gravitational force
D.) Fictitious or pseudo force **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Centrifugal force is an apparent outward force experienced by an object in a rotating frame of reference. It is not a real force but rather the effect of the object's inertia.

### Q177. Which of the following has the largest moment of inertia for the same mass M and radius R?
A.) A solid sphere
B.) A solid disk
C.) A solid cylinder
D.) A thin hoop **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The moment of inertia is largest when the mass is distributed farthest from the axis of rotation. In a thin hoop, all the mass is at the maximum radius R.

### Q178. The time period of a geostationary satellite is:
A.) Equal to the time period of the Sun
B.) Equal to the time period of the Moon

D.) Equal to the rotational period of the Earth **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — A geostationary satellite remains above the same point on Earth because its orbital period is exactly the same as Earth's rotational period (24 hours).

### Q179. If a car makes a turn on a flat road, the centripetal force is provided by:
A.) The car's engine
B.) The normal force

**Answer: D** — The static friction between the tires and the road surface provides the necessary inward force to make the car change direction and follow the curve.

### Q180. The angular momentum of a particle moving in a straight line is:
A.) Always zero
B.) Always infinite
C.) Constant
D.) Zero only if the line passes through the origin **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Angular momentum is L = r x p. If the particle's path passes through the origin (the reference point), the position vector 'r' is always parallel to the momentum vector 'p', making their cross product zero.

### Q181. The units of angular momentum are:
A.) kg m/s
B.) kg m/s²
C.) kg m²/s²
D.) kg m²/s **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Angular momentum L = Iω. The unit for I is kg m² and for ω is rad/s (or s⁻¹). Therefore, the unit for L is kg m²/s, which is also equivalent to Joule-second (J·s).

### Q182. A solid cylinder and a hollow cylinder have the same mass and radius. Which has a greater moment of inertia?
A.) The solid cylinder
B.) They have the same moment of inertia
C.) It depends on their length
D.) The hollow cylinder **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The hollow cylinder has its mass distributed further from the central axis compared to the solid cylinder, giving it a greater resistance to rotation and thus a greater moment of inertia.

### Q183. If the Earth were to shrink to half its present radius with its mass remaining constant, the length of the day would:
A.) Increase
B.) Remain the same
C.) Become zero
D.) Decrease **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — By conservation of angular momentum (L=Iω), if the Earth's radius decreases, its moment of inertia (I ≈ ⅖MR²) would decrease. To conserve L, its angular velocity (ω) would have to increase, making the day shorter.

### Q184. A body is moving in a circle at a constant speed. Which statement is TRUE?
A.) There is no force acting on the body.
B.) There is no acceleration.
C.) Work is being done on the body.

**Answer: D** — Even at a constant speed, the direction of motion is continuously changing in a circle. Since velocity is a vector (speed + direction), the velocity is changing, which implies there is acceleration (centripetal acceleration).

### Q185. The SI unit of torque is:

B.) Joule (J)

**Answer: D** — Torque is calculated as the cross product of the position vector and the force vector (τ = r x F), so its unit is the Newton-meter (N·m).

### Q186. To unscrew a tight nut, a mechanic should use a wrench that is:

**Answer: D** — Torque equals the lever arm (length of the wrench) times the force. A longer wrench increases the lever arm, allowing the same amount of force to produce a greater torque.

### Q187. An object's angular momentum is conserved if the net external _____ acting on it is zero.
A.) Force
B.) Impulse
C.) Energy
D.) Torque **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The law of conservation of angular momentum is the rotational equivalent of the law of conservation of linear momentum. Angular momentum is conserved when the net external torque is zero.

### Q188. A satellite in a circular orbit around the Earth is an example where:
A.) Linear momentum is conserved.
B.) Kinetic energy is conserved.
C.) Potential energy is conserved.

**Answer: D** — The gravitational force on a satellite in a circular orbit provides a torque that is zero with respect to the center of the Earth. Therefore, its angular momentum is conserved.

### Q189. Real satellites orbit the Earth in paths that are slightly:
A.) Parabolic
B.) Hyperbolic
C.) Circular
D.) Elliptical **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — While we often model orbits as perfectly circular for simplicity, real-world orbits are slightly elliptical due to various perturbations and the nature of gravitational fields.

### Q190. The total kinetic energy of a rolling hoop is:
A.) Equal to its translational KE
B.) Equal to its rotational KE
C.) Zero
D.) The sum of its translational and rotational KE **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — A rolling object is simultaneously translating and rotating. Its total kinetic energy is the sum of the kinetic energy of its center of mass (translational) and the kinetic energy of its rotation about the center of mass (rotational).

### Q191. For a point mass M at a distance R from the axis of rotation, the moment of inertia is:
A.) ½MR²
B.) ⅖MR²
C.) MR
D.) MR² **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The formula for the moment of inertia of a single point mass is I = mr², where 'r' is the perpendicular distance from the mass to the axis of rotation.

A.) Start it rotating
B.) Stop it from rotating

**Answer: D** — A large moment of inertia indicates a high resistance to changes in rotational motion. This makes the object difficult to start, stop, or change its speed of rotation.

## Waves

### Q194. Which of a following is a primary characteristic of a mechanical wave?
A.) It can travel through a vacuum.
B.) It transports energy and matter.
C.) Its speed is constant in all media.
D.) It requires a medium. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the defining feature of a mechanical wave; it needs a substance (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel through.

### Q195. In a transverse wave, the particles of the medium oscillate in which direction relative to the wave's direction of propagation?
A.) Parallel to propagation.
B.) In a circular motion.
C.) They do not oscillate.
D.) Perpendicular to propagation. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the definition of a transverse wave. Imagine the up-and-down motion of a rope as a wave passes along its length.

### Q196. The distance between two consecutive crests or troughs of a wave is known as the:
A.) Amplitude
B.) Frequency
C.) Period
D.) Wavelength **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the spatial period of the wave, the distance over which the wave's shape repeats.

### Q197. The relationship between wave speed (v), frequency (f), and wavelength (λ) is given by:
A.) v = f / λ
B.) v = λ / f
C.) f = v × λ
D.) v = f × λ **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This fundamental wave equation correctly states that wave speed is the product of how often a wave cycle passes (frequency) and the length of that cycle (wavelength).

### Q198. Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas assumed the process of compressions and rarefactions was:
A.) Adiabatic
B.) Isobaric
C.) Isochoric
D.) Isothermal **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Newton proposed that the temperature of the gas remains constant as the sound wave passes, which means heat exchange has time to occur.

### Q199. Laplace corrected Newton's formula by suggesting the process is adiabatic. This correction involves multiplying the pressure by a factor of:
A.) γ
B.) 1/γ
C.) √(1/γ)
D.) √γ **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Newton assumed sound propagation was isothermal, giving v = √(P/ρ). Laplace corrected this by considering the process adiabatic, replacing P with γP, which gives v = √(γP/ρ). Thus, the correction is a factor of √γ.

### Q200. The speed of sound in a gas is independent of:
A.) Temperature
B.) Density
C.) None
D.) Pressure **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — According to the ideal gas law, if the pressure of a gas changes, its density changes proportionally. Since speed depends on the ratio of elastic modulus (related to pressure) to density, the effect cancels out, making speed independent of pressure.

### Q201. When two waves meet at a point in a medium simultaneously, the resultant displacement is the vector sum of the individual displacements. This is known as the:
A.) Huygens' Principle
B.) Doppler Effect
C.) Principle of Interference
D.) Principle of Superposition **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This fundamental principle states that the net response at a given place and time caused by two or more stimuli is the sum of the responses which would have been caused by each stimulus individually.

### Q202. Constructive interference occurs when two waves meet in phase, meaning their path difference is:
A.) (n + 1/2)λ
B.) Odd multiple of λ/4
C.) Zero only
D.) nλ **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This condition ensures that crests align with crests and troughs align with troughs, leading to a resultant wave with maximum amplitude.

### Q203. The phenomenon of beats is produced by the superposition of two waves of slightly different:
A.) Amplitudes
B.) Speeds
C.) Wavelengths
D.) Frequencies **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The slight difference in frequency causes the waves to periodically drift in and out of phase, producing the characteristic waxing and waning of loudness known as beats.

### Q204. If two tuning forks of frequencies 256 Hz and 260 Hz are sounded together, the number of beats heard per second is:
A.) 258
B.) 516
C.) 2
D.) 4 **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The beat frequency is equal to the absolute difference between the frequencies of the two sources: |f₁ - f₂| = |260 - 256| = 4 Hz.

### Q205. When a wave is reflected from a denser medium, what is the phase change it undergoes?
A.) 0 degrees
B.) 90 degrees
C.) 270 degrees
D.) 180 degrees **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Reflection from a denser (or fixed) boundary causes the wave to be inverted. This inversion corresponds to a phase shift of 180 degrees.

### Q206. Stationary waves are formed by the superposition of two identical waves traveling:
A.) In same direction, phase diff π.
B.) In same direction, same phase.
C.) Perpendicular to each other.
D.) In opposite directions. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The interference of an incident wave and its reflection traveling in the opposite direction creates points of no displacement (nodes) and maximum displacement (antinodes), forming a standing wave.

### Q207. In a stationary wave, the points of zero amplitude are called:
A.) Antinodes
B.) Crests
C.) Troughs
D.) Nodes **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — These are the points that remain stationary at all times due to complete destructive interference.

### Q208. The distance between two consecutive nodes in a stationary wave is:
A.) λ
B.) λ/4
C.) 2λ
D.) λ/2 **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — A single 'loop' of a standing wave, from one node to the next, spans exactly half a wavelength.

### Q209. For a stretched string of length L fixed at both ends, the wavelength of the fundamental mode of vibration (first harmonic) is:
A.) L
B.) L/2
C.) 4L
D.) 2L **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The simplest standing wave pattern on a string fixed at both ends has nodes at the ends and an antinode in the middle. This shape represents half of a full wavelength, so the full wavelength is twice the length of the string.

### Q210. For an air column in a pipe closed at one end, which of the following is true for the resonant modes?
A.) All harmonics are present.
B.) Only even harmonics are present.
C.) No harmonics are present.
D.) Only odd harmonics are present. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The requirement of a node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end means that only frequencies that are odd multiples of the fundamental frequency can create standing waves.

### Q211. The Doppler effect is the apparent change in a wave's ________ due to relative motion between the source and the observer.
A.) Speed
B.) Amplitude
C.) Phase
D.) Frequency **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the defining characteristic of the Doppler effect. As the source and observer move closer, wave crests are encountered more often (higher frequency), and as they move apart, less often (lower frequency).

### Q212. When a sound source moves towards a stationary observer, the apparent frequency heard by the observer:
A.) Decreases
B.) Remains the same
C.) Becomes zero
D.) Increases **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The source 'catches up' to the sound waves it emits, effectively compressing them and increasing the frequency at which they reach the observer.

### Q213. Which of the following is an application of the Doppler effect?
A.) Tuning a musical instrument.
B.) Using sunglasses to reduce glare.
C.) Creating an echo.
D.) Radar gun for speed measurement. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Radar guns send out a radio wave, which reflects off the moving car. The change in frequency of the reflected wave is measured and used to calculate the car's speed.

### Q214. The compressions and rarefactions in a longitudinal wave are analogous to what features of a transverse wave?
A.) Amplitude and wavelength
B.) Nodes and antinodes
C.) Frequency and period
D.) Crests and troughs **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — A compression is a region of high density/pressure, similar to a crest (maximum positive displacement). A rarefaction is a region of low density/pressure, similar to a trough (maximum negative displacement).

### Q215. What happens to the wavelength of a wave when it passes from a denser to a rarer medium?
A.) It decreases.
B.) It remains unchanged.
C.) It becomes zero.
D.) It increases. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — When a wave enters a rarer (less dense) medium, its speed generally increases. Since frequency remains constant, and v=fλ, the wavelength must increase.

### Q216. The energy of a wave is directly proportional to the square of its:
A.) Wavelength
B.) Frequency
C.) Speed
D.) Amplitude **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The energy transported by a wave is proportional to the square of its amplitude. A wave with twice the amplitude carries four times the energy.

### Q217. In an open organ pipe (open at both ends), the fundamental frequency corresponds to a standing wave with:
A.) Nodes at both ends.
B.) Node at one end, antinode at other.
C.) Two nodes, three antinodes.
D.) Antinodes at both ends. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the simplest standing wave pattern that can fit in a pipe open at both ends. This pattern (Antinode-Node-Antinode) spans half a wavelength.

### Q218. For a given temperature, the speed of sound is greatest in which state of matter?
A.) Liquids
B.) Gases
C.) Plasma
D.) Solids **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Solids are generally much more elastic and denser than liquids or gases. The increase in the elastic modulus is far more significant than the increase in density, leading to a much higher speed of sound (v=√(E/ρ)).

### Q219. The effect of humidity on the speed of sound in air is that the speed:
A.) Decreases
B.) Remains unchanged
C.) Becomes zero
D.) Increases **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Humid air is less dense than dry air because water molecules (H₂O) have less mass than the nitrogen (N₂) and oxygen (O₂) molecules they replace. Since speed is inversely proportional to the square root of density, lower density means higher speed.

### Q220. Two waves are said to be coherent if they have:
A.) Same amplitude and frequency.
B.) Same speed and amplitude.
C.) Same wavelength and are in phase.
D.) Same frequency, constant phase difference. **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This is the definition of coherence. The phase relationship between the waves does not change over time, which is necessary to observe a stable interference pattern.

### Q221. The loudness of a sound is most closely related to its:
A.) Frequency
B.) Wavelength
C.) Speed
D.) Amplitude **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Loudness is the human perception of sound intensity, which is directly related to the amplitude of the sound wave. A larger amplitude corresponds to a louder sound.

### Q222. Which phenomenon demonstrates that light waves are transverse?
A.) Interference
B.) Diffraction
C.) Reflection
D.) Polarization **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — Polarization involves restricting the orientation of wave oscillations to a single plane. This is only possible if the oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of wave travel, which is the definition of a transverse wave.

### Q223. If the tension in a stretched string is quadrupled, its fundamental frequency of vibration becomes:
A.) Half
B.) Quadrupled
C.) Unchanged
D.) Doubled **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — The frequency of a vibrating string is proportional to the square root of the tension (f ∝ √T). Therefore, if the tension is quadrupled, the frequency becomes √4 = 2 times the original.

### Q224. A wave pulse is a disturbance that is:
A.) Continuous and periodic.

**Answer: D** — A pulse is a single, non-repeated disturbance, like a single flick of a rope.

### Q226. A 'shock wave' or 'sonic boom' is produced when an object travels:

### Q227. The quality or timbre of a musical sound depends on:

### Q228. The bending of waves as they pass through a narrow opening or around an obstacle is called:
A.) Reflection

C.) Interference
D.) Diffraction **(answer)**
**Answer: D** — This phenomenon describes the spreading out of waves as they encounter a barrier or opening. It is most noticeable when the size of the opening is comparable to the wavelength.

**Answer: D** — If two points are separated by an integer number of full wavelengths, they are at the exact same point in their respective cycles and are thus in phase.

### Q233. Which of the following does NOT change when a wave enters a new medium?
