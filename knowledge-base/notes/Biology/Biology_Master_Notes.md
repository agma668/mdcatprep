## u1 — Acellular Life (viruses, prions, viroids)

**What a virus is**
- Virus = non-cellular infectious agent; replicates **only inside** a living host cell.
- A virus cannot replicate on its own — that is the one thing that is *not* true of viruses.
- Virion = the complete, mature, infectious virus particle. ×3
- Capsid = the protein coat surrounding the genome; its subunits are capsomeres.
- Naked virus = non-enveloped virus.
- Envelope is derived from the host membrane, is sunlight-sensitive, and gives the virus a short
 survival time outside the host. Enveloped viruses are **not** tolerant to antibodies.
- HIV's envelope is made of **glycoprotein**.
- The genetic code is enclosed by a lipid membrane in the **flu virus** (enveloped).
- A virus recognises its specific host by its **viral envelope** (spike proteins).
- Viruses **can** crystallize, mutate and infect bacteria; they cannot respire or excrete.
- All viruses are **parasites** (obligate intracellular).
- "Virus" is Latin for **poison**.
- Ivanowski (Russian) showed a disease agent passed through a fine filter — **viruses**.
- W. M. Stanley prepared a crystalline extract of **tobacco mosaic virus** in 1935.
- An **oncolytic** virus kills cancer cells and boosts immunity.

**Shapes and structure**
- Adenovirus — polyhedral capsid with **252 capsomeres**.
- Tobacco mosaic virus — **rod-shaped**. ×2
- Polio virus — **spherical**.
- Bacteriophage — tadpole-shaped; the side of the sheath attached to the head is the **collar**. ×6
- **Lysozyme** acts during the **penetration** stage of bacteriophage infection. ×7
- **Prophage** = phage genome integrated into the host DNA.

**Genome types**
- HIV = **retrovirus**; genome is diploid **single-stranded RNA**. ×3
- **Reverse transcriptase** makes DNA from RNA; the process is **reverse transcription**. ×2
- HIV's replication enzymes are **inside the capsid**, with the RNA.
- HIV **provirus** = double-stranded DNA.
- dsDNA virus — **smallpox**. Herpes is the DNA virus among polio/influenza/herpes/mumps.
- ssDNA virus — **parvovirus** (the mild-rash virus, B19).
- Enveloped RNA virus that spreads in epidemic form — **influenza**.

**Prions and viroids**
- **Prions** = infectious proteins that cause disease in humans and animals.
- Only a **single cellular gene** encodes the protein that makes up a prion.
- **Viroid** = sub-viral particle (naked RNA) that infects plants.

**Viral disease and prevention**
- HIV is transmitted through infected blood and hypodermic syringes. ×2
- The **least** common claimed HIV route is a **mosquito bite** (it does not actually transmit HIV).
- HIV in transfusion is prevented by **screening blood for HIV** before transfusion.
- HIV infection runs in **3** stages.
- CD4 is on helper T cells, suppressor T cells and brain (microglial) cells — **MHC II is not a cell**.
- Viral: hepatitis, polio, AIDS, measles, mumps, influenza. **Syphilis and TB are bacterial.** ×3
- Hepatitis is caused by a **virus**.
- Gonorrhoea ← ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae***, spread by sexual contact.
- Measles ← **Paramyxovirus** (morbilli virus).
- Athlete's foot ← **fungus**.
- *Mycoplasma* is **not** a virus (SARS, FIV, SIV are).
- In AIDS, "D" = **Deficiency**; AIDS is caused by a virus. ×2
- **Vaccination** prevents viral infection; measles and mumps are vaccine-preventable. ×2
- Best way to avoid AIDS = **preventive measures**, not vaccination or medication.
- mRNA vaccines (Pfizer, Moderna) **instruct cells to produce the viral spike protein**.
- Sanitizers work on coronavirus through the envelope's **pH sensitivity**.
- Temporary travel restrictions **do** help control viral spread.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- Plastids occur only in **plants**. →u4
- Restriction endonucleases = the "molecular scissors" used to cut a gene out of DNA; they occur
 naturally in **bacteria**. ×3 →u16
- Helper T cells are required to activate B cells. →u12
- Gestation occurs in the **uterus** (pear-shaped, muscular, distensible sac). →u8
- Labour pains come from oxytocin acting on the **myometrium**. →u8

## u2 — Bioenergetics (photosynthesis + cellular respiration)

**Light reactions**
- Site = **thylakoid** membrane; ATP synthesis during photosynthesis also happens there. ×2
- **Non-cyclic** photophosphorylation is the **Z-scheme**. ×2
- Cyclic photophosphorylation yields **ATP only** (no NADPH, no O₂).
- Cyclic phosphorylation is stimulated by a **low level of NADPH**; C4/cyclic flow gives ATP.
- Non-cyclic flow leaves chlorophyll electron-deficient **twice** (once at PSII, once at PSI).
- PSII gets its replacement electrons from the **photolysis of water**. ×2
- Final acceptor of both electrons **and** protons in the light reactions = **NADP⁺**.
- **NADP reductase** reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH.
- End products of the light reactions = **ATP and NADPH**; these are what the dark reaction uses. ×2
- **Plastocyanin** is the only **copper**-containing protein of the light reactions. ×2
- Plastocyanin is reduced by the **cytochrome complex**.
- Without the cytochrome complex, the **proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane** fails.
- When light hits **P700** it is **oxidised** (loses an electron).
- PSI's chlorophyll a absorbs maximally at **700 nm** (PSII at 680 nm).
- Each **photosystem** = light-gathering antenna complex + reaction centre.
- Energy transfer order: **carotenoids → chlorophyll b → chlorophyll a**.
- **Reduction of CO₂ is not** a light-dependent event.
- **Oxygen is not required** for photosynthesis (it is a product).
- **Van Niel** hypothesised that plants split water and release O₂ as a by-product.

**Chlorophyll and pigments**
- The **porphyrin ring** absorbs the light; **Mg** sits at its centre; the phytol tail anchors it.
- Chlorophyll a carries a **–CH₃ on the 2nd pyrrole ring** (chlorophyll b carries –CHO there). ×2
- Chlorophyll a has **72 hydrogen** atoms (C₅₅H₇₂O₅N₄Mg) and is **bluish green**. ×2
- **Carotenoids** are the pigments common to plants and humans.
- Light **quality, intensity and duration** all affect photosynthesis — intensity is the usual
 limiting factor asked for.
- Photosynthesis is maximal in **red** light.

**Dark reaction / Calvin cycle**
- Chloroplast interior: **grana** embedded in the **stroma** matrix; CO₂ → sugar happens in the
 **stroma**. ×2
- **Rubisco** is the Calvin-cycle enzyme and the **most abundant protein in nature**. ×3
- CO₂ + **RuBP** → an **unstable 6-carbon intermediate**, which splits into two 3-PGA. ×4
- **RuBP** is the CO₂ acceptor.
- **G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate)** is the primary product used for glucose synthesis. ×3
- **Regeneration** = 5 G3P → 3 RuBP using ATP. ×6
- The Calvin cycle runs in **3** stages (fixation, reduction, regeneration).
- **Photorespiration** = sugar oxidised in the chloroplast in daylight with no energy yield.
- **Autotrophic** nutrition depends on **photosynthesis**. ×2
- The ultimate energy source of any ecosystem is the **sun**.

**Glycolysis**
- Site = **cytoplasm**; needs **no oxygen** and runs whether or not O₂ is present. ×2
- Glucose → **2 pyruvate** (3-carbon each). ×3
- Yield = **4 ATP produced, 2 consumed → net 2 ATP** + 2 NADH. ×2
- The **oxidative phase begins with the dehydrogenation of G3P**.
- The isomerisation step interconverts **DHAP ⇌ G3P**.
- Glycerate-1,3-bisphosphate → glycerate-3-phosphate by **losing one phosphate** (to ADP).
- **Kinase** transfers a phosphate from ATP onto glucose (hexokinase step).
- End product of glycolysis under anaerobic conditions = **pyruvate**.

**Fermentation / anaerobic respiration**
- Anaerobic yield = **2 ATP**; only **2%** of glucose's bond energy reaches ATP. ×6
- Anaerobic respiration gives **less ATP but is faster** than aerobic. ×2
- In humans/muscle the end product is **lactic acid**; in yeast it is **ethyl alcohol + CO₂**.
- **Zymase** (yeast) ferments glucose; alcoholic fermentation = glucose broken down by
 microorganisms.
- **Anaerobic respiration is used in baking** (and brewing). ×2

**Link reaction and Krebs cycle**
- Pyruvate → **acetyl CoA** uses **NAD⁺ → NADH** and releases CO₂.
- **Acetyl CoA** is the common entry molecule for fat and protein catabolism too.
- Link reaction, Krebs cycle and ETC all occur in the **mitochondrion**. ×2
- Krebs cycle makes **3 NADH per turn** → **6 NADH per glucose**. ×2
- **FADH₂** is formed at **succinate → fumarate**.
- **Malate** is oxidised by NAD⁺ to **oxaloacetate**.
- Krebs cycle's importance = it feeds **oxidative phosphorylation** to make ATP.

**Electron transport chain**
- Site = **inner mitochondrial membrane / cristae**; ATP synthase sits there. ×3
- **Oxidative phosphorylation** = the phosphorylation when NADH hands electrons to coenzyme Q on
 the inner mitochondrial membrane. ×7
- Oxidative phosphorylation = ATP synthesis by coupled **chemical + osmotic** events
 (chemiosmosis) and occurs in **all aerobic cells**.
- First acceptor from **FADH₂** = **coenzyme Q**; both NADH and FADH₂ feed coenzyme Q. ×6
- Final/ultimate electron acceptor = **oxygen**, which sits at a **low energy level**. ×3
- End product of the ETC = **water**. ×6
- The major event of the ETC is **ATP synthesis**; **34 of the 36 ATP** come from it. ×2
- Aerobic respiration of one glucose = **36 ATP** (38 gross in some texts).
- **Mitochondria are required** for aerobic respiration.
- **Cytochrome c** is the respiratory protein shared across most organisms (evolutionary evidence).
- **Respiration** = biochemical breakdown of complex compounds to release energy.
- **Glyceraldehyde and dihydroxyacetone** are intermediates of both respiration and
 photosynthesis.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Spectrophotometry** measures bacterial growth, rate of photosynthesis and minute DNA
 quantities. →u16
- Polysaccharides are built by **condensation**; **hydrolysis** is its reverse. →u3
- Fat + caustic soda (NaOH) → **saponification**. ×2 →u3
- Ethanol → ethanoic acid by **oxidation**. →u3
- **Fossils** are evidence of past life and of evolutionary history. →u7
- The **Golgi complex** processes and packages cell secretions. →u4

## u3 — Biological Molecules

**Water**
- Water makes up **≈ three-fourths of body weight** and is the one compound present in the **same
 percentage in bacterial and mammalian cells**. ×2
- **Hydrophobic exclusion** is the property of water that keeps lipid membranes intact.
- **Adhesion** = attraction between water and a container / the xylem wall (**cohesion** is
 water-to-water). ×2
- Ice is less dense because hydrogen bonding makes water **expand** on freezing.
- In the ice lattice each O atom is attached to **4 hydrogen** atoms.

**Carbohydrates — general**
- Composed of **C, H and O** only, with H : O ≈ **2 : 1**, hence "**hydrated carbons**". ×4
- General formula of a monosaccharide = **(CH₂O)ₙ**.
- Carbohydrates = compounds that on hydrolysis yield **polyhydroxy aldehyde or ketone** subunits.
- **Lactic acid (C₃H₆O₃) is not** a carbohydrate.
- **Glucose** is the smallest carbohydrate — it cannot be hydrolysed further.
- **Monosaccharides are the sweetest**; **oligosaccharides** (6–10 units) are less sweet and less
 soluble; **polysaccharides** have high molecular weight and are sparingly soluble.
- Lactose, maltose and sucrose are all **disaccharides**.
- The four fundamental biomolecules of protoplasm: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and
 **nucleic acids**; the most abundant element listed for the human body is **carbon**.

**Monosaccharides**
- **Glucose** = hexose in the **aldehyde** form; glucose and fructose are both **hexoses**. ×7
- **Erythrose** = a **tetrose**; ribose and deoxyribose have **5** carbons.
- In solution glucose closes into a 6-cornered **glucopyranose** ring, ribose into a 5-cornered
 **ribofuranose** ring.
- Simplest **keto** monosaccharide = **dihydroxyacetone** (glyceraldehyde is the aldose). ×2
- **Ribose** is the sugar of RNA; **C₅H₁₀O₅** is that ribose.
- Normal glucose content of the human body = **0.08%**.
- Most common respiratory substrate = **glucose**; the **direct** energy source in cells = **ATP**.

**Disaccharides**
- The covalent bridge between two monosaccharides = **glycosidic bond**, formed by **removal of
 water** (condensation). ×5
- **Maltose** = glucose + glucose, **1→4** glycosidic linkage. ×3
- **Sucrose** = glucose + fructose, **α1→β2** linkage; hydrolysis gives glucose + fructose. ×4
- Sucrose is unreactive/non-reducing because its **functional groups are unavailable**, which is
 why plants translocate food as **sucrose**; sugarcane stores sucrose. ×2
- **Lactose** = milk sugar = **glucose + galactose**. ×4

**Polysaccharides**
- **Cellulose** is the most abundant carbohydrate in nature, ~**50%** of woody tissue; its pure
 form is **cotton**; hydrolysis yields the disaccharide **cellobiose**; humans **cannot** digest it.
- **Glycogen** gives a **dark reddish-brown** colour with iodine. ×7
- With **starch**, iodine turns intense **blue-black**.
- Starch = polymer of **α-D-glucose**; **amylose** is unbranched with **1→4** glycosidic links;
 initial hydrolysis of starch yields **maltose**.
- Starch is the polysaccharide of **grains and seeds**; starch and cellulose are **both polymers of
 glucose** (starch is stored in plants only).
- **Glycogen** is the animal storage polysaccharide, most abundant in **liver cells**, and is also
 how **bacteria** store food; its synthesis = **glycogenesis**. ×5
- **Glycogen** is the polysaccharide among sucrose / pentose / lactose.
- **Chitin** is a polysaccharide — the arthropod exoskeleton.
- Deposition of **lignin** kills the cell.
- Bacterial cell wall = **amino acid + sugar** (peptidoglycan).

**Lipids**
- 3 fatty acids + **glycerol** → **triglyceride** + 3 water. ×3
- Fatty acid + glycerol join by an **ester bond**; acylglycerols are esters of **fatty acids and
 alcohols**. ×4
- **Acylglycerol** is the most abundant, richest energy store in plants and animals.
- **Waxes** = fatty acid + **long-chain alcohol**; they waterproof the leaf cuticle and insect
 cuticle. ×4
- **Steroids** are the lipids that contain **no fatty acid**; the steroid skeleton is 4 fused rings
 with **17 carbons**, based on **phenanthrene**.
- **Cholesterol** is the precursor of testosterone, progesterone and oestrogens. ×2
- **Gall stones** are mostly **cholesterol**. ×6
- Saturated fats: commoner in animals, more hydrogen, solid at room temperature — the false claim
 is that they have "multiple double bonds".
- **Oleic acid** is unsaturated; **stearic acid** (C18 saturated) has the highest melting point;
 **butyric acid** has **4** carbons.
- Fatty acids carry the **–COOH** group; the **C–H bonds** of lipids are what make them
 energy-rich.
- Lowest energy per gram among milk / ghee / butter / glucose = **milk**.
- **Tristearin** is a lipid; hydrolysis of a fat gives **glycerol + fatty acids**; **triose +
 glycerol** are used to build lipid.
- Phospholipid: the **phosphate head** is the polar/hydrophilic end, the **fatty acid tails** form
 the hydrophobic zone and sit in the **interior** of the membrane. ×8

**Amino acids**
- **20** kinds of amino acid occur in cells and build most proteins. ×2
- An amino acid = organic compound with **both an amine and a carboxyl group**; in an **α**-amino
 acid both are on the **same carbon**; the backbone is **C–C–N**.
- R = **H** → **glycine**; R = **CH₃** → **alanine**. ×5
- **Aspartic acid** is an acidic amino acid; **proline** is the cyclic one.
- At intermediate pH an amino acid is a **zwitterion**, **–N⁺H₃ … COO⁻**, also called an
 **internal salt**; α-amino acids largely exist in this dipolar form. ×4
- At **low pH** it exists as a **cation**; in **basic** solution as the anion **NH₂–CH₂–COO⁻**.
- When acid is added, the **COO⁻** group acts as the base.
- **Ninhydrin** is the reagent for identifying amino acids.
- Amino acids the body cannot make = **essential** amino acids. ×3

**Proteins**
- The **peptide bond** joins **C and N**, formed between the –OH of one amino acid and the H of
 another's amino group with **release of water**; two amino acids → dipeptide by
 **condensation**. ×9
- Breaking protein back to amino acids = **hydrolysis**. ×3
- A **protein** has >100 residues or MW >1000 (below that, polypeptide).
- **Primary** = number and sequence of amino acids; **secondary** = the α-helix (heat disrupts
 this level); **tertiary** = unique 3-D shape of the fully folded chain; **quaternary** = the
 2α + 2β assembly of haemoglobin. ×6
- **Fibrous** proteins are long strands/fibrils — e.g. **collagen**, the connective-tissue protein;
 **globular** proteins are spherical and water-soluble (they are **not** what hair is made of). ×3
- **Haemoglobin** is a **transport protein** that carries O₂ from lungs to every tissue and binds
 **4** O₂ molecules; **574** amino acids in total, **141** per α-chain. ×5
- Oxygen-carrying capacity = the **ability of haemoglobin to bind oxygen**; as keyed, that binding
 is catalysed by **carbonic anhydrase**.
- **Sickle-cell anaemia** = single substitution, **glutamic acid → valine at position 6**. ×2
- **Disulphide bridges** occur in proteins and are the bond broken/reformed in hair rebonding. ×2
- **Histones** are rich in **basic** amino acids; heavy-metal ions **destabilise proteins**.
- **Insulin and glucagon** are proteins; **enzymes** are proteins; the **active site** is made of a
 few (**3–12**) amino acids. ×4
- Proteins are nitrogenous, are tissue building blocks and are polymers of amino acids — they are
 **not** "hydrated carbons"; the skeleton **H₂N–CH(R)–COOH** marks a protein.
- Every reaction in the body has **at least one enzyme** for it.
- **Coenzymes** are derived from **vitamins**; the coenzyme **NAD** is built from **nucleotides**. ×4

**Nucleic acids**
- **Nucleotide** = pentose sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base; nucleotides differ only in their
 **nitrogenous base**. A sugar + base alone = **nucleoside**. ×3
- Nucleotides are joined into a strand by **phosphodiester bonds**, i.e. **C–O–P–O–C**. ×3
- **Purines** (adenine, guanine) are **double-ringed**; pyrimidines are single-ringed. ×3
- **Uracil** is the base absent from DNA, so the **A–U** pair cannot occur in DNA; A pairs with
 **T**. ×4
- **G–C** pairs stabilise DNA because they make **three hydrogen bonds**.
- One turn of the DNA helix spans **10** base pairs.
- A **chromosome** is **DNA + protein**; the nucleus takes up dye because of its **chromatin**. ×4
- **Bacterial** cells have no histones; prokaryotic DNA is a **single circular double-stranded
 molecule not bound by a membrane**.
- **RNA** contains **uracil in place of thymine**; the **sugar–phosphate backbone** is what mRNA
 and DNA share.
- Genetic information is stored as the **genetic code**; **3** nucleotides give **64** codons. ×2
- Central dogma: **DNA → mRNA → protein**; **RNA polymerase** carries out transcription.
- **rRNA** provides the platform for protein synthesis; ribosomes are **RNA + protein**, need
 **Mg²⁺** to stay structurally stable, and a cluster on one mRNA is a **polysome**. ×4
- The **anticodon** sits on **tRNA**; 20 different amino acids need at least **20** tRNAs.
- Abundance in the cell, high → low: **rRNA > tRNA > mRNA > DNA**.
- Shared DNA/RNA structure means all organisms are **monophyletic**.
- Monosaccharides are components of **DNA, NADP, ATP and ribulose bisphosphate**.
- A sample with C, H, O, N but **no phosphorus** is a **protein**.

**Conjugated molecules and membranes**
- A **conjugated molecule** = one class joined to another: **glycoprotein** = protein +
 carbohydrate, **glycolipid** = lipid + carbohydrate, **lipoprotein** = lipid + protein. A molecule
 of only carbon and hydrogen is **not** conjugated. ×5
- The lung **surfactant** missing in respiratory distress syndrome is a **lipoprotein**. ×6
- **Nucleoproteins** are the ones that do **not** build membranes.
- **Phospholipids** are the basic structural framework of every membrane; the plasma membrane is
 chemically **lipid + protein**. ×3
- **Singer and Nicholson**'s fluid-mosaic model = "a sea of lipids in which proteins float".
- **Cholesterol** is the membrane component absent in prokaryotes.
- Surface **glycolipids and glycoproteins** distinguish one cell type from another.
- **Both lipids and proteins** make the membrane selectively permeable.
- Permeability order: **lipids > water/glucose > sodium**. ×2
- Facilitated-diffusion carriers are **proteins**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Capsomeres** are made of **protein**; an un-enveloped plus-strand RNA virus is characterised by
 **lacking an envelope**; phage sequence = **landing → tail contraction → penetration → DNA
 injection**. →u1
- Light phase: one water molecule → **2H⁺ + 2e⁻ + ½O₂**. →u2
- In glycolysis, ATP is **not** directly involved when **1,3-bisphosphoglycerate →
 3-phosphoglycerate** (that step *makes* ATP). ×6 →u2
- **Kinase** moves the phosphate from ATP to glucose. →u2
- **Nuclear pores** allow RNA and protein to move between nucleus and cytoplasm. ×6 →u4
- **Golgi complex** processes cell secretions; **lysosomes** do intracellular digestion; glycogen
 and lipid droplets are **cell inclusions**; the **cell** is the simplest independent unit of
 life. →u4
- **Tay-Sachs** disease lacks a **lipid-digesting** enzyme. →u4
- **Malonate** is the competitive inhibitor example. →u6
- Insulin **increases membrane permeability** to glucose and converts glucose to **lipids**;
 **glucagon** turns glycogen → glucose; **adrenaline/noradrenaline** are the amino-acid-derived
 hormones. →u5
- Blood glucose concentration is **not** under direct nervous control. →u5
- **Pacinian corpuscles** sense deep pressure; **olfactory receptors are chemoreceptors**; a neuron
 conducts **dendrite → cell body → axon**. →u5
- Mature mammalian RBCs have **no nucleus**; most CO₂ travels as **bicarbonate ions**; **carbon
 monoxide** kills by binding haemoglobin. →u11
- Antibodies have **4** polypeptide chains — 2 light + 2 heavy joined by **disulphide bridges** —
 with variable sequences on **both** heavy and light chains. ×3 →u12
- Digestion of **carbohydrate** starts in the oral cavity: **ptyalin/diastase/amylase acts on
 starch**. ×3 →u14
- **Pepsin**: protein → polypeptides. **Carboxypeptidase** hydrolyses the first peptide bond at the
 **carboxyl** end. **Lipase** splits lipids into fatty acids. →u14
- Protein absorption products = **amino acids and small polypeptides**; **lacteals** carry fats;
 **chylomicrons** = fats + proteins; the large intestine absorbs **water and salts**; low blood
 glucose gives **hunger pangs**. →u14
- **ADH** increases **water** reabsorption; dialysing fluid lacks **nitrogenous waste**;
 **glucose** passes the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule; **ammonotelic** animals excrete ammonia
 and **birds** excrete uric acid. →u15
- **Phosphocreatine** is the secondary energy source for muscle contraction. →u9
- DNA is synthesised in the **S** phase of interphase. →u10
- **Reverse transcription** copies **viral RNA** into DNA, giving **cDNA**; **Taq polymerase** is
 the heat-stable one; **heat** separates DNA strands in PCR; restriction endonucleases cut the
 **phosphodiester bond**; a gene's position is its **locus**; plants carrying foreign DNA are
 **transgenic**; cystic-fibrosis liposomes are coated with the **healthy gene**. →u16
- Bacterial waste products = **alcohol, acetic acid and lactic acid**; carnivorous plants gain
 **nitrates**; developing seeds are rich in **both cytokinins and auxins**; in lichens the
 **fungus** protects its partner from desiccation.
- Chemistry that landed here: 40 g NaOH + 49 g H₂SO₄ → **6.02 × 10²³** water molecules; 10 g
 glucose in 100 cm³ → **0.55 M**; **glucose is not** amorphous; an electron-rich nucleus-seeking
 species is a **nucleophile**; **super glue** bonds broken jewellery; transition-metal compounds
 with unpaired electrons are **attracted by a magnet**.

## u4 — Cell Structure and Function

**Prokaryote vs eukaryote**
- Prokaryotes lack a nucleus and **all membrane-bound organelles**; they have a cell wall, DNA and
 ribosomes.
- The one organelle present in **both** prokaryotes and eukaryotes = **ribosomes** (70S in bacteria,
 80S in eukaryotes). ×3
- The only structure a eukaryote has that a prokaryote never has = the **nucleus** / nuclear
 envelope; absent in prokaryotes too: **chloroplasts**, ER, Golgi, mitochondria, nucleolus.
- **E. coli** is a prokaryote; mitosis is **not** seen in it (or in *Nostoc*, cyanobacteria).
- **Mycoplasma** is devoid of a cell wall; a cell without a wall **bursts in a hypotonic
 solution**.
- Prokaryotes appeared ~**3.5 billion** years ago; **eukaryotes** first evolved ~**1.5 billion**
 years ago.

**Cell wall**
- Bacterial wall = **murein / peptidoglycan** = carbohydrate + protein, unique to bacteria. ×5
- In **Gram-negative** bacteria the peptidoglycan layer is **thin**; **Gram-positive** walls carry
 **teichoic acids** (absent in Gram-negative) and hold **more lipids** by comparison.
- Fungal wall = **chitin**; plant wall = **cellulose**; **deposition of lignin** makes woody tissue
 rigid.
- The "heterotrophic, multicellular, no cell wall" description = **Kingdom Animalia**.
- The **secondary wall** sits **between the two primary walls**; the layer laid down at cytokinesis
 is the **middle lamella**, which also acts as the cementing layer between daughter cells.
- The plant wall is **permeable**; the plasma membrane is **selectively permeable**.
- Bacteria hold their shape through the **cell wall**, and **wall + slime** maintain it.

**Plasma membrane**
- Lipids make up the **lipid bilayer**; **phospholipids (lecithin)** are the main component, with
 **cholesterol** essential for mechanical stability — without it the membrane breaks and cells
 burst. ×3
- **Singer and Nicolson** proposed the fluid-mosaic model: proteins float in a fluid
 **phospholipid** bilayer, heads out / tails in. ×4
- The **carrier protein** controls membrane fluidity; the **proteins** make it differentially
 permeable.
- Asymmetry = the hydrophobic tails face inward and the hydrophilic heads outward; the two halves
 are **not identical**. ×2
- **Membrane proteins** move ions in and out; membrane lipids do **not** do recognition, ligand
 binding or flexibility — those are protein jobs.
- **Adenylate cyclase** in the membrane acts as an **enzyme**; cartilage nutrition arrives by
 **diffusion**.
- A cell's "drinking" = **pinocytosis**; engulfing solid particles = **phagocytosis** (a form of
 endocytosis). The lizard tail-detachment example in the papers is keyed as **autolysis**.
- Passive transport = **facilitated diffusion + osmosis**; **endocytosis** and active transport
 need **energy**. Water across a selective membrane = **osmosis**.

**Organelles — mitochondrion and chloroplast**
- The **powerhouse of the cell**, and the only organelle required for **aerobic respiration**, is
 the **mitochondrion**. ×4
- Mitochondria: **double membrane**, inner folds = **cristae**, small knob-like **F₁ particles =
 ATP synthetase** on the inner membrane; the respiratory-chain carriers sit on the **inner
 membrane**; the **matrix** holds the enzymes. ×7
- **Cristae** are the site of **phosphorylation of flavoproteins / redox reactions**.
- Mitochondria have their **own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes**, make their **own proteins**, and
 are the self-replicating organelle. ×3
- **Mitochondria were first seen as granules in muscle cells**, and they sit in the **mid-piece** of
 the sperm; they are **absent from mature RBCs**.
- Chloroplasts: membrane-bound, containing **pigments, linear ds-DNA, ribosomes and proteins**;
 **photophosphorylation** takes place here, and it is the sugar-factory of autotrophic eukaryotes.
 ×5
- Both chloroplast and mitochondrion **cannot live independently** and share 70S ribosomes.
- Only two organelles are **double-membraned**: mitochondria and chloroplasts.

**Lysosomes, peroxisomes, microbodies**
- **Lysosomes** = single-membrane spherical sacs full of **hydrolytic enzymes**; they do
 **intracellular digestion**, **autolysis** and **autophagy** — the one thing they do *not* do is
 process cell secretions. ×5
- **Tay-Sachs disease** = a malfunctioning **lysosome**. ×4
- Digestive vacuoles and autophagosomes = **secondary lysosomes**.
- **Glyoxysomes** appear only at the **seeding stage of oil-seed plants**; **peroxisomes** convert
 the glycolate of photorespiration into glycine.

**Nucleus**
- **Nucleoplasm** = the colloidal mixture of ions and salts in the nucleus; chromosomes are
 **loosely coiled chromatin** except during division.
- The **nucleolus** synthesises **rRNA** and builds **ribosomes**; it is the structure that
 **disappears during cell division**. ×3
- **Nuclear pores** are the exchange route between nucleus and cytoplasm. ×7
- The nuclear membrane is a **double membrane**, and its **outer membrane is continuous with the
 ER**; the **number of pores varies** from cell to cell.
- **Gestations and cell-division facts**: DNA is made in the **S** phase; when chromosomes uncoil
 and nucleoli reappear at the poles the stage is **telophase**. →u10

**Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi**
- **RER** = rough, makes proteins, is where the **enzymes of lysosomes** are synthesised, and is
 where **proteins and lipids become glycoproteins and glycolipids**.
- **SER** = lipid synthesis and metabolism, **detoxification of drugs and poisons** in liver cells,
 transmission of impulses in muscle, and it makes **cholesterol and phospholipids** in liver
 cells. ×4
- Secreted-protein route: **RER → SER → Golgi complex → secretory vesicles**.
- The **Golgi complex** (1888, Camillo Golgi) = processing and packaging, cell secretions, and
 **synthesis of the plant cell wall**; it is made of **dictyosomes**, is most abundant in
 **secretory cells**, and makes **neurotransmitters** in the nervous pathway. ×6
- Golgi orientation: the **cis (forming) face** takes up young cisternae, the **trans (maturing)
 face** sheds the old ones; cisternae break into vesicles at the **convex, maturing face**.
- **Bacterial cells** have no Golgi apparatus.
- Ribosomes are **proteins + RNA**, come in **two subunits**, exist either on RER or free in the
 **cytoplasm**, and a cluster on one mRNA is a **polysome** whose assembly needs **Mg²⁺**. ×3
- Ribosomes synthesise **proteins**; a **neuron's** RER + Golgi granule clusters are **Nissl's
 granules** (groups of ribosomes). ×5

**Other organelles and inclusions**
- **Vacuoles** are bounded by the **tonoplast**; the large central vacuole is characteristic of
 **plants**, and rupturing the tonoplast is how a plant cell commits suicide.
- **Plastids** are plant-only: **chloroplasts**, the colourless **leucoplasts** (starch-storing
 **amyloplasts**), and the pigmented **chromoplasts** that aid seed dispersal and pollination.
- **Peroxisomes**, **cristae**, **ribosomes**, **cytoskeleton**: found in both plant and animal
 cells, absent in prokaryotes. **Centrioles** mark animal cells; plastids mark plant cells.
- **Mesosomes** are invaginations of the **bacterial cell membrane** and stand in for
 mitochondria — they initiate **DNA replication** and aid cell division. ×5
- **Cytoplasmic streaming (cyclosis)** drives the active movement of mitochondria.
- **Heterocysts** are found **only in cyanobacteria**.

**Cell motility**
- **Flagella** originate from **basal bodies** and are made of **microfilaments** (they are
 definitely motile); **pili** are the thin appendages used in **conjugation** to exchange genetic
 material and to **increase pathogenicity**.
- A centriole has **nine triplets** of microtubules, with **nine outer pairs plus two central
 single tubules** in cilia and flagella too; two pairs migrating to opposite poles give **18**
 triplets.
- Spindle fibres attach to the **centromere**, and animal spindles form from **centrioles**; the
 chromosome tip = **telomere**. ×3

**Cell division and meiosis**
- **Crossing over** = exchange of segments between **non-sister chromatids of homologous
 chromosomes** in meiosis I, occurring in **pachytene**; chiasmata form at the **pairing** stage.
 ×7
- Its outcome: recombinant chromatids and increased **genetic variation**.
- The **centromere** holds sister chromatids together; **centromeres divide in anaphase II**.
- When genes swap and rejoin, the event is **crossing over** — not autosomal linkage.
- A single DNA-protein strand of a chromosome = **chromatid**; the whole thread = **chromosome**.
- Mitosis handles **wound healing and repair**; **mutation** is the main cause of cancer, and only
 **mitosis** creates new genetic variation in the asexual sense. ×2
- Nucleus is metabolically most active in **interphase**; ER's flattened sacs = **cisternae**.
- Ultracentrifugation separates **nuclei → mitochondria → ribosomes** (largest to smallest).
- Chromosome number is **fixed for each species**; chromosomes were first observed by **Walther
 Flemming**.

**Chromosomal abnormalities**
- **Down's syndrome** = non-disjunction of the **21st** pair → 47 chromosomes, **trisomy 21**. ×4
- **Klinefelter's** = 44 + **XXY** (fertile male in humans); XXY in **Drosophila** is a sterile
 male.
- Egg with 24 chromosomes from autosomal non-disjunction = **Klinefelter's syndrome**.
- **Meiosis II of the secondary oocyte proceeds to metaphase**; the ovulated oocyte is arrested in
 **metaphase II**.
- **Histones** arrange DNA into chromosomes; the **locus** = position of a gene on a chromosome. ×4
- Mendel's **independent assortment** is explained by **chromosome behaviour in meiosis**; genes
 assort independently only when on **non-homologous chromosomes**.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- The intake of O₂-rich air and release of CO₂-rich air = **gaseous exchange**; not a liquid
 property: **osmotic pressure** (that is a solution property). →u13
- Photosynthesis happens in the **chloroplast**, not in the nucleus. →u2
- Cytoplasm is where glycolysis runs. →u2
- **Phenotype** = the form or appearance of a trait; haploid cells = **sperm**; Mendelian genetics
 applies to **diploid** organisms; Mendel's seven pea traits sat on **seven chromosomes**; the
 secretor gene is on chromosome **19**, albinism on **11**; **geographic separation** drives
 reproductive isolation; evolution is measured by change in **allele frequencies**. →u7, →u10
- **Dry heat** oxidises the bacterial wall; **UV** and **moist heat** are the other sterilisers; a
 capsule prevents phagocytosis; a bacterial wall is **one layer**; bacteria make **spores** in
 favourable conditions; **plasmids** carry drug-resistance genes and replicate **autonomously** —
 the false claim is that the chromosome depends on them.
- **Viruses** are not classified by cell membrane (they have none). →u1
- Neuron at rest = **inside negative** relative to outside; the **nuclear** membrane pore count
 varies. →u5
- **Apoplast theory** describes water and solute moving through cell walls and intercellular gaps;
 the symplast route is cytoplasmic.
- A cell with chloroplasts and a large fluid-filled vacuole is a **plant cell**; fungal cells lack
 chloroplasts.
- Smooth muscle is spindle-shaped, autonomic, non-striated, **one nucleus per cell**, and it does
 **not** line the heart wall. →u9
- **Phagocytosis** takes up solid food particles; **autophagy** is the cell's self-eating; the
 rapid carrier-mediated uptake of materials is **facilitated diffusion**.

**Leftover definitions and one-offs**
- **Endosymbiosis**: flagella may have arisen from an ingested **spirochete**; mitochondria from an
 aerobic bacterium.
- Organism with **no cell wall at all**: *Mycoplasma* (of the cell types), and of the listed

- **Robert Hooke** saw the **cell wall** (cork, 1665) before anyone saw the protoplast.
- **Gram-positive** wall = many layers of peptidoglycan; **Gram-negative** walls hold more **lipid**

- Cell wall may be **absent in fungi and archaea**; fungal walls = chitin, bacterial = peptidoglycan.
- **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**-type thick walls make bacteria hard to kill; sterilisation of the

- **Adenylate cyclase** = membrane **enzyme**; cartilage nutrition = **diffusion**; passive

- **Phagocytosis** = solid uptake; **pinocytosis** = drinking; **autophagy** = self-eating by

- Chromosome number is **species-specific**; chromosomes were first observed by **Flemming**; DNA is

- Cancer's main cause = **mutation**; wound healing = **mitosis**.

## u5 — Coordination and Control

**Neuron structure**
- Impulse path: **sensory neuron → inter (associative) neuron → motor neuron**; the same as
 **receptors → CNS → effectors**. ×4
- **Sensory** neurons run receptor → CNS; **motor** neurons run CNS → muscle. The thing acted on at
 the motor end is the **effector**.
- The **axon** carries the impulse away from the cell body; **dendrites** receive and relay to the
 cell body. The axon is directly continuous with the cell body.
- Most organelles are in the **cell body**; sensory neuron cell bodies sit in the **dorsal root
 ganglion**; one long dendron = **sensory neuron**; most CNS neurons are **multipolar**.
- **Neuroglia** give nutrition, protection and myelination — they do **not** conduct.
- The butterfly-shaped core of the spinal cord is **grey matter** (cell bodies); the outer rim is
 **white matter**; fibre bundles are a **tract** in the CNS and a **nerve** outside it; a cell-body
 cluster in the CNS is a **nucleus**, outside it a **ganglion**.
- Spinal-cord white matter is **myelinated axons**; matter = mixed fibre; sensory fibres enter the
 dorsal horn.

**Resting and action potential**
- Resting membrane potential ≈ **−70 mV**; outside is positive because of **Na⁺**. ×4
- **Na⁺** sits ~**10× more outside** than inside; at rest the membrane leaks **K⁺** out.
- **Depolarisation = inward Na⁺**; **repolarisation = outward K⁺**; the peak is about **+50 mV**,
 so the active potential is roughly **0.05 V**. ×2
- Sufficient depolarisation of the neurolemma fires an **action potential**; a **sub-threshold**
 stimulus cannot produce a spike.
- An action potential lasts **a few milliseconds** (~1 ms) — never seconds or minutes.
- ATP is needed for the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump**; ions moving through **gates** is passive.
- Speed depends on **axon volume (diameter)** and on myelination; human myelinated fibres carry
 ~**100–120 m/s**.
- **Saltatory conduction** is in **myelinated axons**, jumping node to node, ~**100× faster** than
 unmyelinated; unmyelinated axons conduct **continuously**.
- **Nodes of Ranvier** are gaps in the myelin, formed by **Schwann cells** — they exist in
 myelinated neurons only, and are what allow saltatory conduction.
- A nerve impulse is an **electrochemical wave** along the fibre.
- Hyperpolarisation would not happen if the **potassium gates were not so leaky**.

**Synapse**
- Transmitters are made in the **presynaptic neuron**, stored in **synaptic vesicles** within the
 synaptic knob, and released from the **axon ending**. ×3
- They bind receptors on the **postsynaptic membrane** and change its **permeability to certain
 ions** — that is what triggers the post-synaptic potential. ×7
- **Ca²⁺** opens at the terminal to release acetylcholine.
- An excitatory transmitter opens **Na⁺ gates**; **Na⁺ diffusion** depolarises the post-synaptic
 membrane.
- After transmission the transmitter is **broken down by enzymes** (acetylcholinesterase).
- The CNS's abundant **inhibitory** transmitter is **GABA**; **dopamine** is a neurotransmitter;
 **adrenaline** is hydrolysed by monoamine oxidase.
- Outside the CNS the transmitter is **acetylcholine**; in the sympathetic system it is
 **adrenaline**.
- An **electrical synapse** is defined by the **absence of neurotransmitter**.
- Nicotine is **excitatory on acetylcholine receptors** — it raises heart rate, blood pressure and
 muscular activity.

**Receptors and senses**
- Pain and tissue damage → **nociceptors**. ×3
- Pressure, touch, stretch, motion and sound → **mechanoreceptors**.
- Taste and smell → **chemoreceptors**; light → **photoreceptors**.
- Skin carries at least **5** different sense-receptors; **Pacinian corpuscles** read deep pressure;
 **Messelner's** read light touch.
- A reflex arc involves receptors, a sensory neuron, the spinal cord, an inter-neuron and a motor
 neuron — it does **not** involve the brain.
- A **monosynaptic reflex** uses only a sensory and a motor neuron; the pathway itself is the
 **reflex arc**.
- Impulse route receptor → effector: **sensory → inter → motor**.

**Brain**
- **Forebrain** = cerebrum + hypothalamus + hippocampus + amygdala; the **cerebellum is not**
 forebrain.
- **Hindbrain** = medulla oblongata + pons + cerebellum — the cerebrum is **not** hindbrain.
- **Cerebrum** = thinking, reasoning, problem-solving, conscious activity (the **cortex** is the
 seat of consciousness), behaviour, long-term memory.
- **Cerebellum** = balance, posture, coordination, smooth accurate motion; flying birds have a
 well-developed cerebellum.
- **Medulla** = breathing, heart rate, swallowing; the **respiratory centre** and the central
 CO₂-chemoreceptors are both here. ×2
- **Hypothalamus** = the homeostatic thermostat; holds the **neurosecretory cells** secreting
 releasing/inhibiting hormones that control the anterior pituitary; sets the sleep–wake cycle;
 the infundibulum joins it to the pituitary. ×4
- **Hippocampus** = long-term memory; the one function it shares with the cerebellum is **memory
 formation**.
- **Amygdala** = pleasure, fear, rage, punishment and sexual arousal. ×8
- **Pons** = the sleep↔wakefulness switch; the **midbrain** is the auditory relay and eye-reflex
 centre.
- **Thalamus + cerebrum** = forebrain; the hemispheres join at the **corpus callosum**.
- Shallow groove = **sulcus**, deep groove = **fissure**, raised area = **gyrus**.
- **Dopamine**-producing cells die in Parkinson's disease.

**Spinal cord**
- It leaves the cranium through the **foramen magnum**, wrapped in **3 meningeal layers**, with
 **62 spinal nerves** (31 pairs) branching off.

**Homeostasis**
- The three parts of a homeostatic loop: **receptor → control centre → effector**.
- The control centre is the **hypothalamus**; long-term control (growth, sexual maturity) is by
 **hormones**, not neurons.
- The immune system is the one that fights pathogens — not the nervous or endocrine system.
- A homeostatic thermostat sits in the **hypothalamus**.

**Pituitary**
- The **anterior pituitary** is the **master gland**; it is the one known as "master gland of the
 body" in the papers (the pituitary overall is the master gland).
- **ADH (vasopressin)** is released from the **posterior pituitary**; **oxytocin** also comes from
 the posterior lobe. Both are made in the **hypothalamus** and carried down through the
 infundibulum.
- Anterior-lob e hormones that control other glands are **tropic (trophic)** hormones: FSH, LH,
 TSH, ACTH, GH, prolactin.
- **FSH** and **LH** are made by the **anterior pituitary**, under **hypothalamic** control; FSH is
 secreted by the pituitary, not by the ovary.
- Anterior pituitary gonadotrophins = **FSH + LH + prolactin**.

**Adrenal gland**
- The **adrenal gland** is the gland mainly behind **stress hormones**; its **medulla** makes
 **epinephrine and nor-epinephrine**, the "fight-or-flight" pair. ×2
- The **cortex** makes corticosteroids (cortisol, aldosterone, androgens).
- **Nor-epinephrine** is the hormone with the greater effect on **peripheral vasoconstriction** and
 so raises blood pressure. ×7
- **Epinephrine** is the hormone used clinically as a **bronchodilator and heart stimulant**.
- **Aldosterone** raises **Na⁺ reabsorption** in the nephron and controls blood sodium. ×2
- Cortex destruction → **Addison's disease**; cortical over-activity → **Cushing's disease**.
- The adrenal medulla's chief product = **epinephrine**.

**Pancreas and blood sugar**
- **Insulin** = from the **β-cells** of the islets of Langerhans; it lowers blood glucose and is
 what **diabetic** patients get from **genetically engineered *E. coli***. ×4
- **Glucagon** = from the **α-cells**; it is **antagonistic to insulin** and raises blood glucose. ×4
- **Acromegaly** = over-secretion of **growth hormone (STH)**.
- Long-term control of growth and sexual maturity runs through **hormones**.

**Thyroid**
- **Thyroxine** carries **4 iodine** atoms and is chemically a **hormone** (it is an amino-acid
 derivative), classified functionally as a **hormonal protein**-type messenger in the papers.
- **I-131** treats thyroid cancer; **Na-24** is used to **study blood circulation**.
- Thyroid over-activity → **Graves' disease** and **exophthalmia** (both thyroid); low thyroid
 function with an enlarged gland = **goiter**.
- **Myxoedema** = thyroxine deficiency in adults; **cretinism** = in infants.
- Thyroxine is **not** a steroid; **cortisone/oestrogen** are the steroid hormones.

**Oxytocin and prolactin**
- **Oxytocin** = labour pains (acting on the **myometrium**) and milk ejection/dilating the mammary
 milk ducts. ×5
- **Prolactin** is the hormone raised in breastfeeding mothers (it makes the milk; oxytocin
 releases it).
- Gonadotrophins are lowest at the beginning of the menstrual phase.

**Other hormone facts**
- **Prostaglandins** are the lipophilic hormones found in every tissue, managing inflammation,
 blood flow and injury protection; the hypothalamus also regulates them; semen's prostaglandins
 help sperm transport.
- **Endorphins** = made in the **brain**; they give well-being and inhibit pain.
- **Inhibin** comes from **Sertoli cells** and inhibits FSH; **testosterone** comes from the
 **Leydig (interstitial)** cells.
- Low parathyroid activity drops blood **Ca²⁺**.
- **ADH** rises when blood water content falls; its absence causes **diabetes insipidus** (dilute
 urine in quantity).
- **Vasopressin** is the neurosecretory-cell product.
- Hormones are **chemical messengers**; long-term control is hormonal.
- Water-soluble hormones cross the membrane as: **receptor → 2nd messenger → activation of energy**.
- The **thyroid** is the gland of exophthalmia and Graves' disease.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Kupffer cells** of the liver do phagocytosis. →u12
- **Insulin** is **not** an enzyme (pepsin, sucrase and catalase are). →u6
- **Gastrin** comes from the **pyloric region** of the stomach. →u14
- **Vestigial organs** = structures reduced over evolution with no apparent function. →u7
- The **thymus** is the gland that produces lymphocytes. →u12
- **Golgi** modification of proteins and lipids, and **Nissl's granules** in a neuron, both belong
 to u4. →u4
- **Keratin** is the protein with the α-helix **secondary structure**. →u3
- **The thymus** makes lymphocytes; **oxytocin** acts on the myometrium, not the kidney or ovaries.
- Plant items landing here: **auxin** drives apical dominance; *Euglena*'s plant-like feature is its
 **pyrenoid**. →u8
- The **Casparian strip** blocks water and minerals at the endodermis. →u9
- The reflex arc's link inside the spinal cord is the **associative (inter) neuron**; impulses are
 secreted from the **axon ends** of the neuron.
- Central chemoreceptors reading blood CO₂ sit in the **medulla**.

## u6 — Enzymes

**How enzymes work**
- An enzyme is a natural substance that **speeds up** a chemical reaction by **lowering the
 activation energy** — it does not change the equilibrium, does not alter the reaction's
 direction, and it is **reused** for the next reaction. ×4
- **Activation energy** = the energy needed to weaken bonds enough that they can break on
 collision. High activation energy → a **slow** reaction; activation energy is **different for
 the forward and reverse** reaction.
- The essential structure for enzyme activity is the **tertiary** structure; enzymes are
 **globular proteins** (collagen is not — it is fibrous). ×2
- Enzymes lower activation energy by **stabilising the transition state**; the active site binds
 the substrate and is **itself** where catalysis happens. ×3
- They let metabolism run at room temperature because they **greatly increase reaction rates**.
- Enzymes are inactivated by UV / X / αβγ rays because radiation changes their **shape**.
- Enzymes are highly active at **37 °C**; a catalyst lets equilibrium be reached **earlier**.

**Active site and models**
- The dimple or groove on one side of an enzyme = the **active site**; it binds the substrate and
 takes part in the catalytic reaction.
- **Lock-and-key model** — proposed by **Emil Fischer in 1890**: the active site is **rigid and
 specific**, one key fits one lock, enzymes are **non-regulatory** and **cannot change their active
 site**. ×3
- **Induced-fit model** — proposed by **Koshland in 1959**: the active site is **flexible** and
 reshapes as the substrate binds; such an enzyme can catalyse a **range of related** substrates
 and is a **regulatory enzyme**. ×3
- Substrate binding induces a conformational change so the enzyme can perform its **catalytic**
 function.
- Enzymes that can do one specific reaction are **non-regulatory** and **highly specific**; a
 regulatory enzyme example is **hexokinase** (not urease, maltase or sucrase).

**Temperature and pH**
- Beyond the optimum temperature the enzyme **denatures** — irreversibly, so an inactive enzyme's
 recovery point is the **optimum temperature**, while the **maximum temperature** is where it
 **starts to denature**.
- Human enzymes work best **37–50 °C**; above 50 °C they **permanently lose activity**.
- Optimum pH: **stomach / pepsin ≈ 2.0** (highly acidic; the single-value question keys **2.5**),
 **pancreatic lipase ≈ 8.0**, **catalase ≈ 7.0**. ×3
- **pH** is the factor that changes the ionisation state of the active site.

**Cofactors and coenzymes**
- **Apoenzyme** = the enzyme with its non-protein part missing; **holoenzyme** = enzyme + cofactor.
- A cofactor **covalently / permanently** bound to the protein = a **prosthetic group**. ×4
- A **detachable inorganic ion** cofactor = an **activator** (e.g. **Mg²⁺**); the non-protein
 part of an enzyme is a **cofactor**.
- **Coenzymes** make up about **1%** of the whole enzyme and are derived from **vitamins**;
 **NAD** and **FAD** are coenzymes; **NADP reductase** reduces **NADP⁺**.
- Enzymes and coenzymes in common: both are **reused** and both are **derived from vitamins**.

**Inhibitors**
- **Competitive inhibitors** are **analogous to the substrate** in shape, bind the **active site**,
 have **structural similarity to the substrate**, and block it. ×3
- **Non-competitive inhibitors** do **not** resemble the substrate, bind a **regulatory /
 allosteric site**, and **change the shape of the active site**. ×3
- **Irreversible inhibitors** bind **tightly and permanently**, destroying the globular structure
 and catalytic activity; **cyanide** forms a **covalent bond** at the active site → **irreversible**
 inhibitor. ×7
- **Allosteric activators** bind away from the active site and **increase** its activity.
- **Penicillin** = a **competitive inhibitor** of the enzyme bacteria use to build their cell
 walls. ×2
- **Malonate** is the classic competitive inhibitor against succinate dehydrogenase, which converts
 succinate into **fumarate**.
- Pesticides **DDT and parathion** are inhibitors of key nervous-system enzymes.
- An **uncompetitive** inhibitor binds only the enzyme–substrate complex.

**Enzyme deficiency and one-offs**
- **Lysosomes** in eukaryotes hold **hydrolytic enzymes**; heterotrophic digestion also uses
 **hydrolytic enzymes**.
- **Hyaluronidase** helps the sperm penetrate into the ovum. →u8
- **Pepsinogen** is secreted **inactive** so it does not digest its own cells; trypsin is the
 classic zymogen.
- Missing **adenosine deaminase** → **severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)**. →u12
- Enzymes **do not** reverse a reaction's direction, and a catalyst does **not** change
 equilibrium.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Collagen** is the protein that is *not* globular. →u3
- **Catalase** in the plant protects against the toxic effect of **alcohol metabolism**
 (hydrogen peroxide). →u2
- **PEP carboxylase** in C₄ plants lets them avoid photorespiration because PEP carboxylase acts as
 a **strict carboxylase**. →u2
- **Single-stranded binding proteins (SSBPs)** stabilise the separated DNA strand during
 replication. →u16
- Chemistry that landed here: propyne → propanone by **boiling with water plus HgSO₄/H₂SO₄**;
 methanol from CO + H₂ over **ZnO + CuO**; benzene + 3 H₂ → cyclohexane over nickel with benzene
 at **1/6** of the original volume; vicinal dihalide → alkyne with **KOH/NaNH₂**; acetone + water
 → **propan-2,2-diol**; SO₃ yield rises by **adding more SO₂**; N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ optimum ≈
 **450 °C**; benzene halogenation uses **FeCl₃**; a catalyst lowers **activation energy**;
 **DDT/parathion** as inhibitors.
- **Acquired characteristics** (Lamarck) = traits developed by **use or disuse**. →u7

## u7 — Evolution

**The theorists**
- **Darwin**: evolution by **natural selection**; his core phrase is **descent with modification**;
 he also believed in the **perceived unity of life**, and *On the Origin of Species* is about
 **speciation**. ×8
- **Alfred Russel Wallace** reached the same conclusions independently.
- **Lamarck**: **use and disuse of organs** + **inheritance of acquired characteristics**. His giraffe
 explanation = **stretching of necks over generations**. ×4
- Weismann showed **acquired characters are not inherited** (the mason's children get no strong
 muscles) — this is Lamarck's idea being rejected.
- Lamarck is remembered **for his rejected but appealing theory of heredity**.
- **Aristotle** is credited with the **unity of life** idea.

**Darwin's mechanism**
- Natural selection needs: **overproduction of offspring**, **heritable variation**, **struggle for
 existence**, and the **fit reproducing more**. ×3
- Natural selection acts directly on the **phenotype** and on **heritable variation**; its raw
 material is **variation and mutation**.
- Natural selection is **not** regional, is **not** selected by a breeder, does **not** act only on
 random mutations, and it **does** improve a species' adaptation.
- The primary unit selection acts on is the **individual organism** (the unit of *evolution* is the
 population).
- **Artificial selection** = humans deliberately applying selection pressure — e.g. **dog breeds**;
 breeding evidence for evolution = **domestication**.
- **Genetic drift** = random change in allele frequencies, biggest effect in **small populations**.
- **Gene flow** between populations **reduces** their genetic differences.
- Evolution mechanisms: **mutation, gene flow, genetic drift, natural selection** — photosynthesis
 is not one.
- **Hardy–Weinberg** gives the genotype and allele frequencies at equilibrium. ×2
- **The gene pool** = the total number of genes in a population; allele frequencies are the modern
 measure of evolution.
- **Speciation** = the formation of a new species, driven by **isolation / reproductive isolation**,
 and also by the accumulation of small genetic changes over many generations. Isolation **reduces
 gene flow**.
- **Neo-Darwinism** = Darwin's theory **combined with genetics**, with cases including
 reproductive isolation.
- **Endosymbiont theory**: mitochondria and chloroplasts were once **free-living bacteria** living
 inside larger cells.
- **Catastrophism** = sudden natural disasters; **special creation** = life made by a
 supernatural force; **biogenesis** and **spontaneous generation** are the other old life-origin
 ideas.

**Evidence and comparative anatomy**
- **Fossils** are the strongest evidence of evolution, studied as **palaeontology**; they also record
 **chronology**. ×6
- **Homologous** organs = same origin, **different functions** — evidence of **common ancestry** and
 **divergent evolution**. ×4
- **Analogous** organs = same function, **different origin/structure** — evidence of **convergent
 evolution**. ×4
- **Vestigial** organs = functional in the ancestor, reduced or useless now, with no apparent
 function. ×3
- Classic examples: **whale flippers and human forelimbs = homologous**; **bird wings and insect
 wings = analogous**; the **human appendix = vestigial**; **baleen whale hind-leg bones =
 vestigial**.
- Molecular, biochemical and embryological records all support evolution; **digestion** is not
 evidence.
- Industrial melanism = **natural selection**, first observed in the **peppered moth**, and is the
 best-known textbook example of selection in action. ×3
- **Antibiotic resistance in bacteria** is also **natural selection**.
- Homologous structures prove **common ancestry**, not the same function alone.
- Serially homologous organs = similar structures repeated along one body axis (like the limbs of a
 vertebrate).

## u8 — Reproduction

**Male reproductive system — anatomy**
- **Spermatogenesis** happens in the **seminiferous tubules**, which also hold the mature
 spermatozoa. ×4
- The **epididymis** is where sperm are **stored and matured**, and it opens into the **vas
 deferens**. ×3
- **Vasa efferentia** carry sperm out of the testis; the **vas deferens** connects the epididymis to
 the ejaculatory duct; the **urethra** is the terminal part of the male duct system.
- **Testes** descended from the abdomen into the **scrotum** before birth; the scrotum keeps them
 cooler for spermatogenesis.
- Accessory glands: the **seminal vesicle** and the **prostate** (which encircles the urethra) add
 seminal fluid; the **Cowper's (bulbourethral) gland** neutralises the acidity of urine; the
 **seminal vesicle** joins the sperm duct at the ejaculatory duct.
- The **urethra** is the organ counted as a urogenital organ in males (not the vas deferens).
- The **testes** do *not* produce **FSH** — FSH comes from the pituitary. They do do
 spermatogenesis, secrete inhibin and secrete testosterone.

**Male hormones**
- **Testosterone** is made by the **Leydig (interstitial) cells**, which sit **between** the
 seminiferous tubules. ×2
- Testosterone drives **sperm production** and **male secondary sexual characteristics**; its *not*
 a job is inhibiting ICSH — it does not *limit* spermatogenesis.
- **Sertoli cells** make **inhibin**, which inhibits **FSH**. ×2
- **FSH** stimulates spermatogenesis; **LH/ICSH** stimulates the Leydig cells to make testosterone.
- The **prostate** makes a milky alkaline fluid; the **seminal vesicles** supply fructose;
 **prostaglandins** in semen help sperm transport. →u5

**Female reproductive system — anatomy**
- **Oogenesis** begins in the **ovary**, where oogonia are produced.
- The **ovary** does *not* make **HCG**; it makes **estrogen**, makes **progesterone** and does
 ovulate.
- The **oviduct (fallopian tube)** sweeps the released egg, carries the ovum toward the uterus, and
 is the site of **fertilisation**; its other main job is **transport of the ovum to the uterus**.
- The **uterus** is the small, pear-shaped, muscular, distensible sac where **gestation** happens; it
 receives, retains and nourishes the fertilised ovum, and its outermost layer is the **perimetrium**
 (then myometrium, then endometrium). ×2
- The uterus opens to the vagina through the **cervix**, a narrow muscular-sphincter ring; if
 fertilisation does not happen the **endometrium** is the part that degenerates.
- The female system has ovaries, oviducts and cervix — the **seminiferous tubules** are male.

**Oogenesis**
- **Primary oocytes** give the **first polar body** in the first meiotic division; the **secondary
 oocyte** gives the **second** polar body at fertilisation. ×6
- An oogonium → primary oocyte → secondary oocyte → ovum + polar bodies.
- **Follicular development**: primordial → primary → secondary → **Graafian follicle** (mature);
 most follicles fail by **follicle atresia**, meaning the follicle **degenerates**.
- **FSH** stimulates the **primary follicles** at puberty, and drives the ovary to make
 **oestrogen**.
- The **LH surge** triggers **ovulation**, and **LH** converts the ruptured Graafian follicle into
 the **corpus luteum**, a glandular mass that secretes **progesterone**. ×3
- **Progesterone** is the hormone that makes the uterus receptive to implantation, and the one the
 corpus luteum produces. ×6

**The menstrual cycle**
- **Ovulation** comes **~14 days after the start of menstruation**, so a cycle beginning 10 July
 ovulates around **24 July**, and the LH surge lands in **days 11–14** of a 28-day cycle. ×7
- The **LH surge** is triggered by the **fall in estrogen / FSH** feedback at mid-cycle.
- Phases, in order: **menstrual → follicular (pre-ovulatory, ~9 days, the longest) → ovulatory →
 luteal/secretory (~14 days)**.
- The **secretory (luteal) phase** starts after ovulation, has **raised progesterone**, and its
 endometrium **prepares for implantation**.
- The **proliferative phase** endometrium **regenerates and thickens**.
- Menstruation (endometrial breakdown) is caused by the **fall in progesterone**.
- **Menopause** = the **cessation of ova production** (ovarian degeneration stops).
- Gonadotrophins are **lowest at the start** of the cycle.
- **Oxytocin** causes the labour pains, acting on the **myometrium**, and it also dilates the
 mammary milk ducts. ×5
- **Prolactin** rises in nursing mothers to make milk.
- Across the placenta, **blood** is *not* exchanged — gases, nutrients and wastages are.

**Fertilisation and early development**
- **Fertilisation** of the ovum takes place in the **fallopian (oviduct) tube**, at its **proximal
 part**, in rabbits, humans and other placental mammals. ×2
- **Hyaluronidase** lets the sperm penetrate the ovum. →u6
- **Implantation** of the embryo occurs in the **uterus**, in the endometrium.
- The **corpus callosum** is brain tissue — never a follicle structure.
- **Endosperm** forms through **double fertilisation** in flowering plants.
- Comparative embryology of vertebrates shows development of **gill pouches**; the nervous system
 develops from the **ectoderm**.
- A bacterium's genome becomes **diploid after binary fission** (once the two copies coexist before
 segregation).

**Sexually transmitted infections**

## u9 — Support and Movement (skeleton, joints, muscles)

**Bone tissue and its cells**
- **Osteocytes** = the living cells sitting in the **lacunae** of bone. ×5
- **Chondrocytes** = the living cells of cartilage, sitting in **lacunae** of the cartilage matrix;
 they are what *form* cartilage. ×8
- **Osteoblasts** = bone-forming cells; **osteoclasts** = bone-resorbing cells; **osteogenic cells**
 are the stem cells. ×2
- Bone is wrapped by the **periosteum**; cartilage by the **perichondrium**.
- **Collagen** is the protein that establishes the matrix of bone and cartilage; it is most
 abundant in **tendons**. ×3
- **Hyaline cartilage** is the most abundant cartilage and the type at the ends of long bones;
 cartilage has **no blood vessels** and heals slowly because it lacks vascular supply.
- Bone is a **connective tissue**; it is ~**70% water** by cell weight (bone tissue itself is
 mostly mineral).
- The human skeleton = **axial + appendicular**.

**Skeletal counts**
- **22 bones** in the skull; **33 vertebrae** total; **7 cervical + 12 thoracic = 19**;
 the sacrum is the fusion of **5** vertebrae, the coccyx of **4**.
- **30 bones** per half of the upper limb (humerus, radius, ulna + 30 hand bones); the **femur is
 not** an upper-limb bone and is the **longest bone** of all.
- The pelvic girdle half is **3 bones** (ilium + ischium + pubis fused into the hip bone); the
 **hyoid** sits between the skull and the postcranial skeleton; the inner ear's **incus, malleus
 and stapes** are tiny bones.
- Because of the cranium, animals with a skull are **vertebrates**.
- The **temporal** bone is paired (frontal, occipital, vomer and ethmoid are unpaired).
- The first cervical vertebra is the **atlas**, the second the **axis**.
- The brain is protected inside the **cranium**.

**Joints**
- Joints are studied as **arthrology**; inflammation of a joint = **arthritis**.
- **Synovial joints** have a fluid-filled joint cavity and are the **freely movable** type; the
 synovial cavity holds the oily fluid that cuts friction.
- **Fibrous joints** are immovable — e.g. the **suture** between skull bones.
- **Cartilaginous joints** are slightly movable — **pubic symphysis**, **intervertebral discs**,
 costal cartilage; the pubic symphysis is **fibrocartilage**.
- **Hinge joint** = elbow and knee; the **ulna and humerus** meet there, giving back-and-forth
 movement. Rotational movement at the forearm is **radius and ulna**.
- **Ball-and-socket** = hip and shoulder, the multi-directional joint; the **hip and shoulder**
 joints are both ball-and-socket (some papers key the same pair as synovial).
- **Gliding (plane) joints** are the slightly movable type.
- **Ligaments** hold bones together; **tendons** connect skeletal muscle to bone.
- **Sciatica** = tingling/pain along the leg; **arthritis** = swollen, painful, immovable joint;
 **rheumatoid arthritis** is the autoimmune one; **gout** = hard **uric acid crystals** in the
 joints; **ankylosing spondylitis** fuses the spine; **spondylosis** = degeneration of the
 intervertebral discs.
- **Rickets** is a vitamin-D/bone-softening disorder; the hardening of cartilage by calcium
 deposition is **spondylosis**.
- Rib–vertebra attachments are **slightly movable** joints.

**Muscle types**
- **Skeletal** = voluntary, striated, multinucleate, **uni-nucleate? no** — it is the only one you
 control.
- **Smooth** = involuntary, non-striated, **uni-nucleate**, spindle-shaped; it controls the
 diameter of blood vessels and lines hollow organs; it has **no sarcomeres**.
- **Cardiac** = involuntary, **branching**, **intercalated discs**, striated with a
 **syncytium**-like branching network; it is the **myogenic** muscle (self-exciting); its
 involuntary + branching character is shared with smooth muscle in being involuntary.
- Common to cardiac and smooth: **involuntary**.
- **Myofibrils** are the contractile elements; the **sarcoplasm** is the muscle cytoplasm; the
 **sarcoplemma** is the muscle cell membrane; **endomysium** surrounds each muscle fibre.
- The **neuromuscular junction** is a **chemical synapse** between an **axon and the
 sarcoplemma**.
- **Shivering thermogenesis** = **involuntary muscle contraction**; the rib cage and pleura act
 passively during breathing, the diaphragm actively.

**Sarcomere and the sliding-filament model**
- The **sarcomere** = the unit between **two Z-lines** (one Z to the next); it is the repeating
 pattern of the myofibril. ×4
- Bands: **A band** = whole myosin; **I band** = actin only; the **H zone** sits at the **mid of
 the A band** and is the only myosin region.
- **Thick** filament (16 nm) = **myosin**; **thin** filament = **actin**, with **tropomyosin**
 and **troponin** bound to it. Each myosin is surrounded by **6 actin** filaments. ×3
- In a **relaxed** fibre: **tropomyosin blocks the myosin binding site** on actin, the **H zone is
 broad**, and no cross-bridges form.
- On stimulation: **Ca²⁺ leaves the sarcoplasmic reticulum** (that is its store), binds
 **troponin** — not actin, not tropomyosin, not the sarcoplemma — which pulls tropomyosin aside;
 cross-bridges form; actin **slides over myosin**; the **Z-lines move closer**, the **I band
 shortens** and the **H zone disappears**; the **A band stays the same** and the sarcomere
 shortens. ×8
- **ATP** is used for **cross-bridge breaking** (detachment of myosin), and energy is released when
 a **phosphate is removed** from ATP.
- On **recovery**, calcium is **pumped back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum**.
- **T-tubules** run perpendicular through the fibre and carry the action potential deep in, from
 the sarcoplemma into the sarcoplasmic reticulum; the SR's **Ca²⁺ gates** open as a result.
- **Myoglobin** is the muscle pigment that binds/stores oxygen; **haemoglobin** is the blood one.
 ×2
- Fibrous proteins do **not** carry oxygen in blood (that is haemoglobin's job); they do take part
 in clotting (fibrin), nail formation (keratin) and muscle contraction (actin/myosin).
- **Lactic acid** build-up → **muscle fatigue** (tiredness and pain), not cramp, tetany or
 paralysis; fatigue = **deficiency of glucose/ATP plus excess lactic acid**; **tetany** is an
 ionic imbalance (low Ca²⁺).
- **Myosin is a globular protein** at its head but the filament is described as fibrous in some
 papers — actin, haemoglobin, myoglobin and insulin lack quaternary structure, myosin has it.

**Other connective tissue and cytoskeleton**
- **Cartilage** is a connective tissue that is avascular and heals slowly.
- **Intermediate filaments**: **8–10 nm**, made of **vimentin**, giving **mechanical support**
 (tubulin makes the 7 nm microtubules for cilia, flagella and cyclosis).
- **Actin** and **myosin** do not exhibit quaternary structure, unlike haemoglobin and myosin.
- All cells are genetically identical except **reproductive cells** (gametes).

## u10 — Inheritance (Mendel, dominance types, sex-linked, chromosomal)

**Terminology**
- **Homozygous** = both alleles of a gene pair the same (a true-breeding plant that self-fertilises
 into all-offspring-of-its-own-phenotype has a **homozygous** genotype). ×3
- **Genotype** = the genetic makeup inherited from the parents (your hair-colour makeup);
 **phenotype** = the observable physical characteristics. ×2
- **Karyotype** = the particular array of chromosomes an individual possesses.
- **Allele** occupies the **same locus on the respective homologues**; dominance is one allele's
 physiological effect over its partner at that locus.
- Mendel called his particulate factors **Elementen**; he worked with the **garden pea**
 (*Pisum sativum*), selecting **seven pairs** of contrasting characters.
- Mendel's second step after choosing pure-breeding tall and dwarf plants was to **cross them to
 produce hybrids**.
- **Test cross** = unknown × **tt** (homozygous recessive).
- A **hybridisation** = fusion of two genetically dissimilar parents by artificial pollination.
- **Apoptosis** = programmed cell death; **necrosis** = cell death from tissue damage.

**Mendel's laws and ratios**
- Monohybrid F₂ **genotypic** ratio = **1 : 2 : 1** → shows **segregation of alleles**;
 **phenotypic** ratio = **3 : 1** (e.g. 3 round : 1 wrinkled). ×3
- Dihybrid F₂ phenotypic ratio = **9 : 3 : 3 : 1**, which reflects **independent assortment**;
 the shuffling of alleles happens during **gamete formation**. ×2
- True-breeding round peas in F₂ = **25%**; a 1:1 ratio comes from **Yy × yy**.
- A 45 tall : 14 dwarf offspring means the parents were **Tt × Tt**.
- A heterozygous **AABbCc** plant makes **4** different kinds of gametes.
- Mendel's own RR × rr cross: monohybrid, each parent makes **only one kind** of gamete, F₂ shows
 **3 genotypes** but **2 phenotypes** — the false claim is "three phenotypes in F₂".
- The **law of segregation** says one individual out of four in F₂ has a phenotype that failed to
 show in F₁.
- When F₁ shows only the dominant parent's phenotype, the recessive allele is **dominant? no** — the
 trait is **dominant**, and the F₁ looks like the **dominant parent**.
- **Gene linkage** is the exception to Mendel's laws, and it is a **physical relation between genes**
 on the same chromosome. **Linkage** also distorts the 9:3:3:1 ratio.
- **Morgan** first discovered gene linkage in *Drosophila*, and his experiments showed deviation
 from independent assortment due to **linkage**.
- **Crossing over** is the exchange of segments between **homologous** chromosomes and is the
 phenomenon that increases variation; a higher recombination frequency means **more variation**.

**Types of dominance**
- **Complete dominance**: one allele fully masks the other.
- **Codominance**: both alleles expressed completely and fully → **AB blood group** is the classic
 example; *IAIA and IBIB are co-dominant* statements key as correct in ABO.
- **Incomplete dominance**: the heterozygote is **intermediate** — here the **genotypic and
 phenotypic ratios are the same** in F₁. ×6
- **Over dominance**: the **heterozygote exceeds** both homozygotes (Drosophila *w/w⁺* has a
 brighter eye pigment than *w⁺/w⁺* or *w/w*). ×6
- **Pleiotropy** = one gene affects **two or more** (unrelated) traits.
- **Epistasis** = a gene at one locus suppresses or modifies the effect of a gene at another locus;
 the suppressed gene is **hypostatic**.
- **Polygenic inheritance** = several gene pairs at different loci acting **additively** on one
 trait — the basis of **continuous variation** (human skin colour is the example). ×2
- **Multiple alleles** = the **ABO blood group system**. ×3
- **Jumping genes** are the ones with **no fixed locus**.
- **Pattern baldness** is due to **hormonal differences** between sexes, and a **homozygous female
 is normal**; it is sex-influenced, not sex-linked.
- Two allelic genes at the same locus both fully expressed = **codominance**.

**Sex-linked inheritance**
- **Hemophilia** is **X-linked recessive**; **haemophilia A** = disturbed **Factor VIII**,
 **haemophilia B** = disturbed **Factor IX**. **Haemophilia A** is the more common one. ×2
- A hemophiliac male + carrier female → **50%** of sons affected (all daughters carriers); a
 carrier female × affected male → **50%** of sons; carrier × normal man → **half her sons**
 affected; normal man × heterozygous woman → **0%** chance for their **daughter** being
 hemophiliac (she would need both alleles).
- Hemophilia "zigzags" from the **maternal** grandfather, through a **carrier daughter**, to the
 **grandson** — never straight from father to son. ×6
- An **X-linked recessive** trait never passes father → son; it is equally passed to sons and
 daughters only if it is **autosomal**. ×2
- **X-linked dominant** (e.g. **hypophosphataemic rickets**, vitamin-D-resistant rickets): **all
 female offspring of an affected father are affected**, and an affected mother has a **50%**
 chance of passing it to any child.
- **Y-linked (holandric)** traits: gene on the **Y**, always **more common in males**, passed from
 an affected father **only to his son**.
- Sex-linked recessive traits are **more likely to affect men than women** — colour blindness,
 haemophilia.
- **Colour blindness** = **deuteranopia** in the papers' naming; red colour blindness means red and
 green both look **grey** — the *drosophila* "eyed" trait is sex-linked too (white-eyed female ×
 red-eyed male gives a **0.5** chance of colour-blind male offspring in the keyed version).
- **Hypophosphataemic rickets** is the X-linked **dominant** disorder; **haemophilia, colour
 blindness, Becker muscular dystrophy** are X-linked recessive.
- **Drosophila bobbed** gene is **X-linked**.

**Blood groups**
- **ABO** = **multiple alleles** (IA, IB, i) on an **autosomal** chromosome; IA and IB are
 **codominant**; it **codes for antigens** (the false claim is "doesn't code for any antigen").
- A person with **group O** whose children are AB cannot have a mother **homozygous for IA**
 (the other options can work); group O is ii.
- A **baby girl with haemophilia** must have a **carrier (or affected) mother and a haemophiliac
 father**.

**Mutations**
- A change in a DNA molecule's structure making a **different allele** = **gene (point) mutation**.
- **Phenylketonuria** = a **point mutation**; **sickle-cell anaemia** = **base substitution**
 (glutamic → valine).
- **Chromosome inversion** and other structural changes are **chromosomal** mutations.
- **Mutation** is the **ultimate source of new genetic variation**; **crossing over** and
 **independent assortment** also create variation, but **natural selection** does not create new
 variation — it sorts it.
- A **selectively advantageous** mutation increases in frequency and **spreads through the
 population over generations**; a **bottleneck effect** is the population shrink after a
 natural disaster, and the **founder effect** is from a small colonising group.

**Chromosomal abnormalities**
- **Turner's syndrome = 44 + XO** — sterile female, infertility, short stature, webbed neck,
 low hairline. ×4
- **Klinefelter's = 44 + XXY** — enlarged breasts and small testes in males, a sterile male in
 *Drosophila*. ×4
- **XXY is sterile female in humans but sterile male in Drosophila** — the sex-chromosome
 count behaves differently across species. ×8
- **Down's syndrome = trisomy 21 (autosomal non-disjunction)** — mental retardation, short stature,
 broad face, squint eyes. ×2
- **Edward's = trisomy 18**; **Patau's = trisomy 13**; **Jacob's** is a sex-chromosome
 abnormality.
- **Alzheimer's disease** is the age-related one.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Evolution** = change in allele frequencies in populations over generations; the struggle for
 existence is from **overproduction**; the best adapted leave the most offspring. →u7
- **14 chromosomes** are found in the **garden pea**.
- The **tRNAVal** is the tRNA that inserts the valine of the sickle-cell haemoglobin. →u3
- **Self-fertilisation** in successive generations leads to reduced variation and inbreeding
 effects.
- **Chromosomal abnormalities of Turner = XO, Klinefelter = XXY** also surface in u4 and u7.
- **Down's syndrome is autosomal non-disjunction**; **Jacob's** is the sex-chromosome form.
- Haemophilia patients can bleed to death from small cuts and bruises.

## u11 — Circulation (heart, vessels, blood, lymph)

**Heart chambers and valves**
- The **right atrium** receives **deoxygenated blood** from the superior and inferior vena cava;
 the **left atrium** receives oxygenated blood from the **four pulmonary veins**.
- The **aorta** originates from the **left ventricle**.
- **Tricuspid (right AV) valve** separates the **right atrium from the right ventricle**; the
 **bicuspid/mitral** valve goes from **left atrium to left ventricle**.
- **Semilunar valves** sit at the aorta and pulmonary artery outlets and **prevent backward flow**
 into the ventricles — they are **not** in veins.
- A human heart has **6 valves** in total (2 atrioventricular + 4 semilunar halves), though most
 papers count **3 valve groups**.
- **Chordae tendineae** are the fibrous cords in the **ventricles** attached to the **tricuspid
 valve** and papillary muscles; weakening the papillary muscles lets blood back-flow into the
 **right atrium**. ×2
- The heart wall layers: **endocardium** (inner, and the layer the **valves** are formed from),
 **myocardium** (the **cardiac muscle** wall — the only cardiac-muscle layer), **epicardium**
 (outer). The closed sac around the whole heart is the **pericardium**.
- The **SA (sinoatrial) node** sits in the wall of the **right atrium**, upper dorsal, and is the
 **pacemaker** that normally regulates the heartbeat; impulses go **SA node → AV node → Bundle of
 His → Purkinje fibres**.
- The delay between SA and AV node conduction is about **0.10 s** (the papers' keyed value).
- The ventricle's thick-walled chambers sit on the **apex / lower side** of the heart.

**Cardiac cycle**
- One complete heartbeat = **1 systole + 1 diastole**, lasting about **0.8 s**.
- **Ventricular systole** closes the atrioventricular (bicuspid/tricuspid) valves and opens the
 semilunar ones; ventricular diastole opens the AV valves again.
- The **LUB** sound = AV valves closing at the start of ventricular systole; the **DUB** = semilunar
 valves closing as the ventricles relax. Heart sounds come from **valve closure**, not contraction.
- **Diastole** = the chambers **relax and fill with blood**.
- The **cardiac output** = the volume of blood the left ventricle pumps into the systemic circuit
 per minute.
- Cardiac muscle is the muscle of the **heart walls only** (not the valves, not the aorta).

**Blood vessels**
- **Veins** = **larger bore, thin walls, no pulse**, and **valves present** (except in the upper
 part of the body — that exception is the false statement); wall ~two cells thick; diameter
 40–50 μm; flow is **toward the heart**.
- **Arteries** = **thick, muscular walls**, no valves, pulsatile.
- **Capillaries** = walls of **one cell**, walls are **highly permeable**, and they have the
 **lowest blood velocity because they have the greatest total cross-sectional area**.
- Microcirculation order: **arterioles → metarterioles → thoroughfare channels → capillaries**.
- The **descending aorta** bifurcates into the **iliac arteries**, whose femoral branches feed the
 thigh muscles.
- The **pulmonary vein** carries **oxygenated** blood (the one vein that does); the **pulmonary
 artery** carries deoxygenated.
- A **four-chambered heart with a right systemic arch only** = **birds** (mammals have the left
 systemic arch).
- **Bernoulli's principle**: blood pressure drops when velocity **increases in a narrow vessel**.

**Blood**
- **Fibrinogen** is the plasma protein involved in **clotting**; **prothrombin** also takes part;
 **albumin** maintains **osmotic balance**.
- **Blood group antigens** are **glycoproteins** (and glycolipids); they sit on the RBC surface.
- Haemoglobin transports O₂ and CO₂ and **buffers blood pH** — it does **not** regulate blood
 pressure in stress.
- **Carboxyhaemoglobin (10–20%)** forms when **CO₂** combines with the **haem portion** of
 haemoglobin.
- **Leukaemia** = an increased number of **blood cells** (white cells) in the blood.
- **Hepatitis B**'s major route is **blood transfusion** (and transfusion/blood contact generally).
- **Cholesterol** deposits plaque-like masses in arteries, causing a **heart attack**.
- Blood enters the **glomerulus through the afferent arteriole**; Bowman's capsule continues into
 the **proximal convoluted tubule**.
- **Anti-A and anti-B antibodies** in serum = **blood group O** (the only group with both);
 both antigens present = **group AB**; group A− can be donated to **A positive**.
- **O−ve** is the **universal donor**; **AB+ve** is the universal recipient. ABO gives **4
 phenotypes**; the ABO system was discovered by **Landsteiner**; the Rh factor was named after the
 **Rhesus monkey**, and Rh gives **2 phenotypes**.
- **IAi × IAi** gives **3/4 group A and 1/4 group O** children; an A father with a B child can be
 **IBi**; a group-O child from a group-B father means the father is **IBi**.
- Group A × group B parents can have a child of **any of A, B, AB and O**.
- **Anti-A and anti-B antibodies** appear **in the first few months of life**, not on first
 exposure.

**Lymph**
- Lymphatic tissue fluid = **lymph**.
- The **thoracic duct** is the largest lymph duct and drains into the **subclavian vein**; lymph
 empties into the bloodstream at the **subclavian vein**.
- Loss of the lymphatic system compromises **blood composition and defence**, not oxygen-carrying
 capacity.
- **Lymphatic blockage** first causes **excess fluid accumulation in the tissue** (oedema).

**Homeostasis of the blood**
- **High osmotic pressure of blood** (dehydration) triggers **ADH** secretion.
- **Alcohol, caffeine and atrial natriuretic factor** all favour large quantities of dilute urine —
 **renin** does not.
- A **negative feedback** mechanism example = **control of body temperature** (platelet
 aggregation, labour and milk ejection are positive feedback).
- Vasodilators **relax smooth muscle** in vessels to **reduce blood pressure**.
- Sympathetic over-activity raises **heart rate** and **dilates the pupils**; the parasympathetic
 constricts bronchi and promotes digestion.
- The **autonomic nervous system** does **not** control **limb movement** (that is somatic).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **ABO = multiple alleles**, symbol **I**, on an autosomal chromosome; the character determined by
 three alleles = human blood group. →u10
- **Polygenic** = human skin colour and eye colour; **Rh** is a single-gene system. →u10
- **Nissl's granules** are in **nerve cells** (not WBCs, RBCs or platelets). →u5
- The **knee-jerk** reflex is the exception to polysynaptic transmission (it is monosynaptic);
 heartbeat, breathing and playing games are polysynaptic. →u5
- **Histamine** is produced by **basophils**; **E. coli**-style bacteria destroy red corpuscles and
 cause pernicious anaemia. →u12
- **Necrosis** vs **apoptosis** belongs to u10's cell-death items.
- English items misfiled here: "it is just a **cyclone** in the teacup"; "**resuscitate** him".
- Capillary rise: the tube of **smaller radius shows a higher rise** of water.

## u12 — Immunity

**The four kinds of immunity**
- **Natural active** = you catch the disease and survive, then develop your own immunity
 (exposure to HIV and recovering → natural active). ×6
- **Natural passive** = antibodies handed to a baby in **colostrum / mother's milk**.
- **Artificial active** = a **vaccine** (attenuated culture of bacteria, live or inactivated
 polio) — slow to build, long lasting.
- **Artificial passive** = injecting **antibodies / antiserum / anti-venom / immunoglobulins /
 plasma** from a recovered patient — **immediate** protection, short lived.
- **Passive** immunity is what gives **immediate** protection; injecting antibodies is **passive**,
 and the snake-bite antivenom is **passive** too.
- Recovered-COVID plasma works because of the **antibodies** in it, not interleukins or
 interferons.

**The lymphocytes**
- **B lymphocytes** are made and **matured in the bone marrow** (named for the **bursa of
 Fabricius** in birds). They **secrete antibodies** — plasma cells come from B cells in the
 **humoral** response. ×3
- **T lymphocytes** **mature in the thymus**; the thymus is the gland of T-cell maturation and it
 produces lymphocytes. ×2
- **Cell-mediated immunity** = T cells recognise the antigen and attack the microorganism or a
 transplanted organ; **humoral immunity** = B cells and their antibodies. ×4
- **Helper (T4/CD4) T cells** are required to activate B cells; **cytotoxic T** and **memory T**
 complete the cell-mediated arm.
- Antibodies are made by **B lymphocytes** — not T cells, not RBCs, not platelets. ×2
- **Lymphocytes** (with monocytes/macrophages) are the cells central to immunity; **thrombocytes
 (platelets)** are not.

**Antibody structure**
- An antibody has **2 heavy + 2 light chains**, 4 polypeptide chains total, joined by
 **disulphide bridges**; the **variable region** is at the **upper** end and is what
 **recognises the antigen**; the **constant region** is at the lower end. ×3
- Antibodies **neutralise** antigens, **precipitate** them and **enhance phagocytosis** — they do
 **not** secrete cytokines (that is a living cell's job).
- **Monoclonal antibodies** in diagnosis **detect specific antigens** in clinical samples.

**Innate / non-specific responses**
- Non-specific barriers: **mucus, HCl, spermine** (in semen), phagocytes, inflammation, fever.
- **T cells** are specific, not non-specific — they are the exception in the "which is not
 non-specific" question.
- The **inflammatory** chemical of the upper respiratory tract is **histamine**, produced by
 **basophils** (and mast cells); histamine makes blood vessels **dilate**. ×6
- **Bradykinin** is produced at the site of tissue injury and promotes fluid leakage;
 **interferons** are the cytokines that **activate natural killer cells** and control viral
 infection — they do not "secrete interleukin". ×2
- The **complement** and inflammatory cascade causes the redness and swelling of inflammation.
- **Neutrophils** are the **most abundant WBC** and the first to arrive at an infection;
 **monocytes** become tissue **macrophages**; **macrophages** and neutrophils are the main
 phagocytes. ×2
- **Granulocytes** = **neutrophils, eosinophils and basophils** (monocytes and macrophages are
 agranulocytes).
- Phagocytosis is what lysosomes and macrophages/kupffer cells do.
- The **complement** system opsonises pathogens; **opsonins** mark them for phagocytosis.

**Lymphoid organs and lymph flow**
- **Lymph nodes** filter lymph; **lymph vessels** move it. The **spleen** is a lymphatic organ
 (it also filters blood and removes old red cells); the **thymus**, **tonsils** and **Peyer's
 patches** are lymphoid too.
- Lymph flows **toward the thoracic lymph duct** and empties into the **subclavian vein**; the
 lymph's **final destination** is the subclavian vein.
- Lymph through the lymphatic vessels = **lymph**; the vessel in the intestinal villi is the
 **lacteal**, which carries absorbed fat.
- Swollen lymph nodes after a throat infection = an **active immune response**.
- **Lymph nodes vs vessels**: vessels move lymph, nodes filter it.

**Blood transfusion rules**
- Transfusion matches **the donor's antigen against the recipient's antibodies** — donor antigen
 must not be attacked by the recipient's antibodies.
- **Rh⁺ blood** carries **Rh antigens on the RBCs** (the Rhesus **monkey** is where the factor
 was first studied).
- **Rh antibodies** in Rh-negative people appear only **after exposure to the Rh antigen** — never
 before birth, never naturally after birth.

**Chemotherapeutics, antisepsis and sterilisation**
- **Chemotherapeutics** = chemicals used to kill or inhibit microbes **in living tissue**. ×6
- **Disinfectants** work on **inanimate** surfaces; **antiseptics** on living tissue;
 **antibiotics** are chemicals made by microorganisms that destroy other microbes (from
 **actinomycetes** and some bacteria).
- **Pasteurisation** preserves **milk products**; **vaccination** uses an **attenuated** culture.
- The biggest problem with antibiotic misuse = **increased resistance in pathogens**.
- **BCG vaccine** is used against **pulmonary tuberculosis**.

**Diseases**
- **HIV** targets **T-helper (CD4) lymphocytes** — both B and C options describe the same cells.
- **SCID** = missing **adenosine deaminase**; treatable by putting genetically engineered cells
 into **bone marrow**.
- **Cystic fibrosis** affects **endothelial / epithelial** cells (the sweat and mucus glands);
 liposome gene therapy coats the healthy gene.
- **Arthritis** = inflammation of a joint, an **autoimmune** disease and the leading cause of
 disability over 65 — it is *not* inflammation of a nerve.
- **Leukaemia** = too many white blood cells.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Thymus** produces lymphocytes; the **spleen** filters blood and removes old red cells. →u11
- **Monoclonal antibody** technology and the engineered **malaria vaccine** belong to u16
 biotechnology. →u16
- **Interferons** control viral infection; **bradykinin** and **histamine** drive inflammation.
- **Kupffer cells** of the liver are the resident phagocytes.
- **Cerebral cortex** is the outer grey-matter layer (a CNS item misfiled here). →u5
- The waste excreted by a healthy human is **urea** (not ammonia, uric acid or creatinine). →u15
- Swollen lymph nodes = active immune response; lymph flows to the **subclavian vein** via the
 thoracic duct. →u11

## u13 — Respiration (human)

**Airway anatomy**
- The **pharynx** is the part common to both the digestive and the respiratory systems, and its
 main respiratory role is being the **passage for both food and air**. ×2
- The **epiglottis** is the flap that stops the bolus entering the trachea; it is also what closes
 the nasal opening during swallowing (along with the **soft palate**, which pushes up). ×3
- **Swallowing** is initiated by the **tongue**, which forces the epiglottis into a horizontal
 position and moves the soft palate up.
- The **trachea** lies **ventral** (in front of) the oesophagus; the **pharynx** sits above the
 larynx.
- **Cartilage rings** in the trachea stop it collapsing and keep the air passage open.
- **Ciliated epithelium + goblet cells** line the **trachea and bronchi** — **alveoli have no
 cilia**.
- **Smooth muscles** are in the trachea, bronchi and bronchioles — **alveoli have none**.
- **Bronchioles** end in the **alveoli and alveolar sacs**; alveoli are the actual **gas-exchange
 surface**, and each air sac is made of several microscopic **single-layered** alveoli.
- Alveoli are elastic because of **collagen and elastin** — these fibres expand and recoil.
- The lungs are enclosed in the double-membraned **pleura** (two layers); the area between the two
 lungs is the **mediastinum**; the **hilum** is the lung's root.
- The lungs are closed sacs open to the outside only via the **trachea, nostrils and mouth** —
 never the oesophagus.
- The **diaphragm** separates the thoracic from the abdominal cavity; the stomach sits below it on
 the **left side of the abdomen**.
- The **diaphragm** is the main muscle of breathing, while the **rib cage and pleura act
 passively**.
- Frog breathing: fresh air enters the bucco-pharyngeal cavity through the nostrils and old air is
 held under pressure in the **glottis**.

**Mechanics and volumes**
- On **inhalation** the diaphragm moves **down** and the ribs move **up and out**, so the **chest
 cavity volume increases and the lung pressure decreases**. ×6
- **Tidal volume** at rest ≈ **0.5 L**; **residual volume** (left after forced expiration) ≈
 **0.5–1.5 L**; **total lung capacity** ≈ **5–6 L** (total inside capacity ~5 L). ×2
- The **residual volume** is the air that always remains in the lungs.
- Lung capacities of interest: tidal 0.5 L, expiratory reserve ~1.1 L, inspiratory reserve ~3.1 L,
 residual ~1.2 L, total ≈ 5 L.

**Gas transport**
- About **70%** of CO₂ is carried as **bicarbonate** ions; the **chloride shift (Hamburger's
 phenomenon)** moves Cl⁻ into the RBC while HCO₃⁻ leaves. ×2
- **Formation of oxyhaemoglobin (HHb)** is *not* part of tissue-level gas exchange — that happens
 in the lungs; at the tissues you get **HHb formation, sodium bicarbonate and the chloride
 shift**.
- **Carboxyhaemoglobin** forms when **CO** binds the **iron** part of the haem (10–20% saturates).
- The **pulmonary vein** returns oxygenated blood; the **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated.
- The **carbonic anhydrase** in RBCs speeds CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻.

**Diseases and disorders**
- **Respiratory distress syndrome** in premature babies = **deficiency of surfactant** (surfactant
 is a **lipoprotein**). ×2
- **Tuberculosis** is a respiratory disease (and in one paper keyed as the STD among the options —
 the STD is **syphilis** in the same question set).
- **Lung cancer** is the common respiratory malignancy; **testicular cancer** is the young-men's
 high-cure-rate one.
- The **larynx's** primary function is **voice production** (not humidifying, filtering or
 gaseous exchange).
- **Respiratory surface** must be thin, moist and have a large area with a good **blood** supply.
- Excretory organs include the kidney, skin and lungs — the **appendix** is not excretory.
- **Pseudomonas** is a **spiralled** bacterium; **Staphylococcus pneumoniae**'s non-virulent
 strain is **non-capsulated R-type** (the virulent one is capsulated S-type, the Griffith
 transformation).
- **Nose and trachea** are both supported by **hyaline cartilage** (the epiglottis is elastic
 cartilage).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Surfactant** is a **lipoprotein** — a u3 conjugated-molecule fact. →u3
- **Hyaline cartilage** is the most abundant cartilage (u9). →u9
- **Diaphragm** as a smooth muscle, and the **intercostal muscles** of the chest wall, belong to
 u9. →u9
- **Hamburger's / chloride shift** is a u11 circulation fact. →u11
- **The spleen** filters blood and removes old red cells (u11). →u11
- **Urea** is the healthy human's excretory product (u15). →u15
- Chemistry that landed here: the geometry of **but-2-ene** is its cis/trans isomerism case, and
 the hydrogen needed to make 5 kg of ammonia is ~**15 g** worth of H₂ — an industrial-gas
 calculation. →u16
- **Digestion of protein starts in the stomach** (u14). →u14

## u14 — Digestion

**Mouth and saliva**
- Digestion **begins in the buccal cavity** and ends in the small intestine. ×2
- The **parotid glands** sit **behind the jaws** (the other two are submandibular/submaxillary
 and sublingual). ×8
- Mastication makes the sublingual and back-of-mouth glands secrete **saliva**; the ground food
 mass is the **bolus**.
- **Amylase (ptyalin/diastase)** digests **starch** in the mouth; digestion of **carbohydrate** is
 the only one that starts in the oral cavity. ×3
- **Lingual lipase** comes from **Ebner's gland**.

**Oesophagus**
- Food is forced down the oesophagus by **peristalsis** (swallowing/deglutition initiates it).
- **Pyrosis (heartburn)** results from inefficiency of the **oesophagus / cardia** — acid reflux.

**Stomach**
- The portion right after the oesophagus is the **cardiac portion**, then fundus, body, pyloric.
- Gastric glands have **3 (four)** cell types: **parietal (oxyntic) cells** make **HCl** *and*
 **intrinsic factor**; **chief (zymogen) cells** make **pepsinogen**; **mucus/goblet cells** make
 mucus; endocrine cells make **gastrin**. ×6
- **Protein breakdown** in the stomach is done by **chief cells** (via pepsin).
- **Gastrin** is secreted from the **pyloric region / antrum** of the stomach, and it **stimulates
 gastric juice**.
- **Pepsinogen → pepsin** by **hydrochloric acid** (low pH).
- **Intrinsic factor** (from parietal cells, i.e. the **stomach**) is needed for B₁₂ uptake.
- Stomach wall muscles churn food with gastric juice into **chyme**.
- Acid reaching the duodenum is the **acid in food** that stimulates secretin.

**Small intestine**
- **Digestion is completed in the small intestine**; it is the absorptive organ too.
- **Secretin** is released into the blood from the **duodenal mucosa** by acid; it stimulates
 pancreatic juice (the bicarbonate part).
- **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** **releases bile from the gall bladder**.
- **Enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** is made by the **duodenal lining** and activates
 **trypsinogen → trypsin**. ×3
- **Chymotrypsinogen** is a component of **pancreatic juice**; it is also activated as a zymogen.
- Proteins after digestion are absorbed as **amino acids and small polypeptides**.
- The **ileocolic sphincter** at the end of the ileum lets a little residue into the large
 intestine. ×6
- The **pyloric sphincter** lets chyme from stomach → small intestine; the **cardiac sphincter**
 guards the other end.
- Lipids: **mechanical** digestion = the stomach (churning) / bile emulsification, **chemical** =
 **pancreatic lipase**.
- The carbohydrate-digesting enzyme in pancreatic juice is **amylopsin (pancreatic amylase)** —
 not ptyalin (that is salivary).
- **Pancreatic lipase** works at alkaline pH; **pepsin** is the acidic one.
- **Lactase** is the odd enzyme out among pepsin, trypsin and chymotrypsin (it is a
 carbohydrase, not a protease).

**Pancreas, liver and bile**
- **Bile** = a **watery, enzyme-free** secretion containing the **breakdown products of
 haemoglobin** (bile pigments), **bile salts** and **cholesterol** — **lipase is not** in bile. ×2
- Bile is made in the **liver**, stored in the **gall bladder**, and poured into the duodenum
 through the **common bile duct**.
- **Emulsification of fats** is bile's job.
- **Jaundice** = elevated **bile pigments in the blood**.
- Removing the gall bladder causes **uncontrolled release of bile** → poor fat digestion.
- **Secretin** stimulates pancreatic secretion; the hormone that *inhibits* it in the papers'
 keying is **gastrin** (the intended contrast is that gastrin acts on the stomach, not the
 pancreas).
- **Gastrin + secretin** together drive the pancreas in the historical papers' version.
- **Hepatitis = inflammation of the liver**; **hepatitis A** spreads by the **faecal-oral**
 route, **hepatitis B/C** are blood-borne (hepatitis B also passes **mother → child during
 pregnancy**), and **hepatitis D** is the **viroid** disease.
- **Mesenteric veins** drain the **large intestine**.

**Large intestine**
- Its jobs: **absorb water**, **absorb electrolytes**, and **make vitamins** (large-intestine
 microflora make **vitamin K**, with symbiotic bacteria). ×2
- It does **not** absorb amino acids (that is the small intestine).
- Parts: caecum, colon, sigmoid colon, rectum — the **appendix attaches to the caecum**.
- **Villi are not** in the large intestine (they are small-intestine).

**Goblet cells and mucus**
- **Goblet cells secrete mucus** — never HCl, enzymes or hormones. ×5

**Enzyme zymogens**
- Secreted **inactive**: pepsinogen, trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen (and erypsinogen); the one
 secreted **active** is **amylase** (also lipase in some lists). ×3
- All proteolytic enzymes are released as **zymogens** except **pepsin** itself is active once
 formed by HCl.

**Absorption**
- **Glycogen** is stored in the **muscle and liver**. ×2
- The large intestine absorbs **water and salts**; the **lacteals** carry absorbed **fat**.
- **Chylomicrons** = fat + protein, formed in the gut wall.

**Feeding disorders**
- **Anorexia nervosa** = stopping eating out of fear of obesity (the 18-year-old with
 underdeveloped feminine characteristics and psychological immaturity). ×2
- **Bulimia nervosa** = binge-then-purge; **orthorexia** = obsession with "healthy" food;
 **dyspepsia** = indigestion.

**Parasites and worms**
- **Fasciola** = **liver fluke**, an endoparasite of sheep in the **liver and bile duct**; its
 primary host is the **sheep**, with the **snail** as intermediate host.
- **Schistosoma** lives in the host's **blood**.
- **Taenia** = tapeworm; **Ascaris** = roundworm (endoparasite); **leech** = the **ectoparasite**.
- **Dugesia** is the **free-living carnivorous flatworm**.
- *Coelenterate* colony members that do different jobs are **zooids**.
- **Alternation of generation** = sexual form producing asexual form and vice versa.
- **Vermiform appendix** is the human **vestigial organ** here.

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Cyclic photophosphorylation** does *not* include **photolysis of water** — that is the
 non-cyclic path. →u2
- **Storage (lysosomal storage) diseases** come from defective **lysosomal enzymes** — Tay-Sachs
 and the like. →u4, →u6
- **Symbiotic bacteria** in the large intestine make **vitamin K**. →u7

 →u15

## u15 — Homeostasis (kidney, osmoregulation, excretion)

**Definition**
- **Homeostasis** = maintaining the internal environment against fluctuations of the external
 one. Thermoregulation is one kind of it; evolution is the accumulation of adaptations over
 time.

**Nephron — filtration and reabsorption**
- Filtration happens at the **glomerulus + Bowman's capsule**.
- **Most reabsorption** happens in the **proximal tubule** (the useful constituents too); this is
 also where water and sodium are reclaimed, along with the collecting duct. ×3
- The **countercurrent multiplier** is the **loop of Henle** (in the renal medulla); water is
 reclaimed by that mechanism in the **collecting duct**.
- The **ascending limb of the loop of Henle** actively pumps out **Na⁺**, promoted by
 **aldosterone**, and it does **not** let water out. ×2
- **Hypertonic (concentrated) urine** is made by the **loop of Henle**, chiefly via
 **juxtamedullary nephrons**, which sit at the cortex–medulla border and handle the water-shortage
 case. ×2
- **ADH** acts on the **distal tubule and collecting ducts**, changing their permeability so water
 is reclaimed. ×3
- Under **ADH**, the kidney makes **hypertonic urine with decreased volume** — the keyed
 combination. ×6

**Anatomy of the kidney**
- The **renal pelvis** collects urine before it enters the **ureter** (cortex and medulla do not).
- Urine → renal pelvis → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra.
- The **renal cortex** holds cortical nephrons; the **medulla** holds the juxtamedullary ones.

**Hormonal control**
- **ADH / antidiuretic hormone** → water reabsorption → concentrated, low-volume urine; it rises

## u16 — Biotechnology

**Core idea and cloning**
- **Biotechnology** = using living organisms in industry to make useful products.
- **Cloning** = producing genetically identical copies of an organism by asexual reproduction; the
 identical individuals are **clones**. ×2
- Plants carrying foreign DNA = **transgenic plants**. ×2
- **Genetic engineering** joins DNA from different sources; the result is **recombinant DNA
 (rDNA)**.
- The modified plasmid or phage DNA that carries the new gene is called **recombinant DNA /
 rDNA**.
- **DNA probes** are made of **radioactively labelled nucleotides**.

**Tools and vectors**
- **Enzymes** are the tools for manipulating DNA in recombinant DNA technology.
- **Restriction endonucleases** are the molecular scissors, cutting the **phosphodiester bond** of
 duplex DNA. ×3
- **DNA ligase** is the enzyme that joins two double-stranded DNA fragments.
- **Plasmids** are the common vectors in recombinant DNA technology; a **phage** vector, and the
 classic one is **lambda phage**. ×3
- Bacteria take up recombinant plasmids when treated with **CaCl₂**.
- **Agrobacterium tumefaciens** is the bacterium used to make transgenic plants such as
 **golden rice**.

**PCR**
- **PCR** is the process that makes multiple copies of the desired gene; its sequence is
 **heating → cooling → add primer → copying of the strand**. ×2
- **Taq polymerase** is the heat-stable polymerase; **heat** separates the DNA strands.

**Reverse transcription and gene therapy**
- DNA made from mRNA by reverse transcriptase = **complementary DNA (cDNA)**.
- **Gene therapy** for **cystic fibrosis** uses **liposomes (prosomes)** coated with the healthy
 gene; for **SCID (adenosine deaminase deficiency)** it works by putting genetically engineered
 cells into the **bone marrow**. ×2
- **Insulin** is produced for diabetics by **genetically engineered *E. coli***; growth hormone
 too. ×3
- **Monoclonal antibodies** are used in diagnosis to **detect specific antigens** in clinical
 samples; a **biotech malaria vaccine** works by creating a **targeted immune response** against
 the parasite.

**DNA fingerprinting**
- **DNA fingerprinting** is the technique used to identify criminals, and the **microsatellites**
 are what produce the distinctive pattern on the autoradiograph.
- Age of fossils is determined by **radioactive dating** (not DNA fingerprinting or PCR).

**Plant tissue culture**
- **F. C. Steward** used the **carrot** for his tissue-culture / totipotency experiment.

**Biological control**
- **Biological control** = destroying pests with the same living organisms or natural enemies
 (not pasteurisation, not integrated disease management, not genetic engineering).

**Bled in from other units (same buckets)**
- **Positive feedback** examples: **labour contractions** (body temperature, insulin production
 and thyroxine release are negative feedback). →u5
- **Genetic drift / gene pool / mutation** are evolution mechanisms, not continental drift. →u7
- **Genetic engineering cells** go into bone marrow for **SCID**; **prosom** liposomes for
