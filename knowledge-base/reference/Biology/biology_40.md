# MDCAT Biology MCQs — Source: .com (40 questions with answers & explanations)

## Acellular Life

### Q1. HIV primarily attacks which type of immune cells?
A. B cells
B. Red blood cells
C. Helper T cells
D. Natural killer cells
**Answer: C** — HIV specifically targets and destroys CD4+ helper T-lymphocytes weakening the immune system.

### Q2. Which of the following is NOT a criterion for classifying viruses?
A. They have DNA or RNA.
B. They can mutate.
C. They lack cellular structure.
D. They are obligate intracellular parasites.
**Answer: C** — Viruses are acellular and do not have their own mode of nutrition so this is not a classification criterion.

## Bioenergetics

### Q3. How many ATP molecules are produced from the complete oxidation of one glucose molecule?
A. 36
B. 38
C. 40
D. 42
**Answer: B** — Complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose molecule yields approximately 38 ATP molecules.

## Biological Molecules

### Q4. Water is an excellent solvent because of its:
A. Polar nature
B. High boiling point
C. Low density as ice
D. Transparency
**Answer: A** — Water's polar nature allows it to dissolve many ionic and polar substances effectively.

### Q5. Which of the following elements is found in all organic biological molecules?
A. Nitrogen
B. Carbon
C. Phosphorus
D. Sulfur
**Answer: B** — Carbon is the fundamental element of all organic molecules due to its ability to form four covalent bonds.

### Q6. Glucose is a:
A. Disaccharide
B. Polysaccharide
C. Lipid
D. Monosaccharide
**Answer: D** — Glucose is a monosaccharide (simple sugar) with the formula C6H12O6.

### Q7. Glycoproteins are examples of:
A. Conjugated molecules
B. Simple proteins
C. Polysaccharides
D. Lipids
**Answer: A** — Glycoproteins are conjugated molecules consisting of a protein covalently bonded to carbohydrate groups.

### Q8. Triglycerides are composed of:
A. Two fatty acids and one glycerol
B. Three amino acids
C. Glycerol and phosphate
D. One glycerol molecule esterified with three fatty acid chains
**Answer: D** — A triglyceride consists of one glycerol molecule esterified with three fatty acid chains.

### Q9. How many amino acids are commonly found in proteins?
A. 10
B. 20
C. 30
D. 40
**Answer: B** — There are 20 standard amino acids that are commonly found in proteins.

### Q10. RNA differs from DNA in having:
A. Double-stranded structure
B. Uracil instead of thymine and ribose sugar
C. Deoxyribose sugar
D. Thymine instead of uracil
**Answer: B** — RNA contains uracil instead of thymine and has ribose sugar instead of deoxyribose.

### Q11. The DNA double helix model was proposed by:
A. Gregor Mendel
B. Louis Pasteur
C. James Watson and Francis Crick
D. Charles Darwin
**Answer: C** — James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double helix model of DNA in 1953.

## Biotechnology

### Q12. Recombinant DNA technology involves:
A. Using restriction endonucleases to cut DNA and ligases to join fragments
B. Only PCR amplification
C. Natural cell division
D. Protein synthesis only
**Answer: A** — Recombinant DNA technology uses restriction endonucleases to cut DNA and ligases to join fragments from different sources.

## Cell Structure & Function

### Q13. Which organelle is present in plant cells but absent in animal cells?
A. Mitochondria
B. Cell wall
C. Ribosome
D. Endoplasmic reticulum
**Answer: B** — Plant cells have a rigid cell wall made of cellulose which is absent in animal cells.

### Q14. Chromosomes are composed of:
A. Lipids only
B. DNA wrapped around histone proteins
C. RNA only
D. Carbohydrates
**Answer: B** — Chromosomes consist of DNA wrapped around histone proteins forming chromatin.

### Q15. The rough endoplasmic reticulum is studded with:
A. Lysosomes
B. Golgi bodies
C. Ribosomes
D. Peroxisomes
**Answer: C** — Rough ER has ribosomes attached to its surface giving it a rough appearance and enabling protein synthesis.

### Q16. Prokaryotic cells lack:
A. Cell membrane
B. Membrane-bound nucleus
C. Ribosomes
D. DNA
**Answer: B** — Prokaryotic cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus; their DNA is in the nucleoid region.

## Circulation

### Q17. Arteries differ from veins in that arteries:
A. Carry deoxygenated blood
B. Have thinner walls
C. Have valves
D. Have thicker walls with abundant elastic and smooth muscle layers
**Answer: D** — Arteries must withstand and maintain high pressure; their walls are thicker with abundant elastic and smooth muscle layers.

### Q18. During ventricular systole, the atrioventricular (mitral and tricuspid) valves are:
A. Closed
B. Open
C. Partially open
D. Ruptured
**Answer: A** — During ventricular contraction (systole), the AV valves close (producing the first heart sound, S1).

### Q19. The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle because it:
A. Pumps blood to the lungs
B. Receives blood from the veins
C. Has fewer chordae tendineae
D. Pumps blood into the systemic circulation against much higher peripheral resistance
**Answer: D** — The left ventricle pumps blood into the systemic circulation (aorta) against much higher peripheral resistance.

### Q20. Lacteals in the intestinal villi are lymphatic capillaries responsible for absorbing:
A. Proteins
B. Dietary fat particles and fat-soluble vitamins
C. Glucose
D. Water
**Answer: B** — Lacteals absorb chylomicrons (dietary fat particles) and fat-soluble vitamins from the intestinal epithelium.

### Q21. Lymph coming from the right side of the head, the right arm and the right half of the thorax is drained by the:
A. Right lymphatic duct
B. Thoracic duct
C. Cisterna chyli
D. Left subclavian vein
**Answer: A** — The right lymphatic duct collects lymph from the right upper quadrant of the body.

## Coordination & Control

### Q22. The resting membrane potential of a neuron is approximately:
A. -90 mV
B. -40 mV
C. -70 mV
D. +30 mV
**Answer: C** — The resting membrane potential of a typical neuron is approximately -70 mV (inside negative).

### Q23. The basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system is:
A. Glial cell
B. Neuron
C. Synapse
D. Axon
**Answer: B** — The neuron (nerve cell) is the basic structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

### Q24. The largest part of the human brain is:
A. Cerebellum
B. Cerebrum
C. Brain stem
D. Hypothalamus
**Answer: B** — The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain responsible for higher functions like thinking and voluntary actions.

### Q25. A sensory receptor is best described as a biological:
A. Transducer
B. Amplifier
C. Generator
D. Modulator
**Answer: A** — A receptor detects a stimulus and converts its energy into the electrical energy of a nerve impulse; this energy conversion is exactly what defines a transducer.

## Digestion

### Q26. Chemical digestion of carbohydrates begins in the:
A. Stomach
B. Small intestine
C. Large intestine
D. Oral cavity
**Answer: D** — Salivary amylase (ptyalin) begins the hydrolysis of starch to maltose in the oral cavity.

### Q27. The complete set of permanent teeth in an adult human being consists of:
A. 28 teeth
B. 30 teeth
C. 32 teeth
D. 34 teeth
**Answer: C** — The adult dentition has eight teeth in each half jaw; total of thirty-two teeth.

## Enzymes

### Q28. A competitive inhibitor is able to occupy the active site because it:
A. Changes the enzyme's shape
B. Destroys the enzyme
C. Binds to the allosteric site
D. Is structurally similar to the normal substrate
**Answer: D** — A competitive inhibitor is structurally similar to the normal substrate; so it fits the active site and blocks it.

### Q29. Enzymes are best defined as:
A. Biological catalysts
B. Structural proteins
C. Energy storage molecules
D. Genetic material
**Answer: A** — An enzyme is a catalyst of biological origin; it accelerates a cellular reaction without itself being permanently altered.

### Q30. Raising the temperature well above the optimum reduces enzyme activity mainly because:
A. Substrate decomposes
B. Enzyme concentration decreases
C. The enzyme is denatured
D. pH changes drastically
**Answer: C** — Above the optimum, the extra kinetic energy disrupts weak bonds holding the tertiary structure; the active site loses its shape.

### Q31. The optimum temperature of an enzyme is the temperature at which:
A. The enzyme is destroyed
B. The reaction stops
C. The greatest rate is obtained
D. The enzyme is most stable
**Answer: C** — Below this point the rate is limited by low kinetic energy and above it by denaturation.

### Q32. An enzyme inhibitor is a substance that:
A. Increases enzyme activity
B. Reduces the rate of enzyme-catalyzed reactions
C. Destroys enzymes permanently
D. Creates new enzymes
**Answer: B** — An inhibitor combines with the enzyme in such a way that fewer molecules of substrate can be converted.

### Q33. The correct sequence of events in an enzyme catalysed reaction is:
A. Product → Substrate → Enzyme
B. Enzyme → Product → Substrate
C. Substrate → Product → Enzyme complex
D. Substrate + Enzyme → ES complex → Product + Enzyme
**Answer: D** — The substrate first occupies the active site to give the ES complex; it is then changed into product.

### Q34. Enzymes accelerate a reaction by acting on which thermodynamic quantity?
A. Free energy change (ΔG)
B. Activation energy
C. Equilibrium constant
D. Entropy
**Answer: B** — An enzyme provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy.

## Evolution

### Q35. The age of the earth is estimated to be about:
A. 1 billion years
B. 2.5 billion years
C. 4.6 billion years
D. 10 billion years
**Answer: C** — Radiometric dating places the formation of the earth at roughly 4.6 billion years ago.

### Q36. Darwin published his theory of evolution by natural selection in the book:
A. Philosophie Zoologique
B. On the Origin of Species
C. Principles of Geology
D. The Descent of Man
**Answer: B** — On the Origin of Species appeared in 1859.

### Q37. The central idea of Lamarck's theory of evolution was:
A. Survival of the fittest
B. Mutation
C. Inheritance of acquired characteristics
D. Natural selection
**Answer: C** — Lamarck held that organs strengthened by use and weakened by disuse pass those modifications to offspring.

### Q38. Natural selection acts directly upon which of the following?
A. Genes
B. DNA sequences
C. Phenotypes (expressed characters)
D. Chromosomes
**Answer: C** — Selection can only act on expressed characters; individuals with favourable phenotypes survive and reproduce more.

### Q39. The Miller and Urey experiment demonstrated that:
A. Organic molecules can form from inorganic precursors under primitive Earth conditions
B. DNA replicates semi-conservatively
C. Cells can arise spontaneously
D. Proteins are made of amino acids
**Answer: A** — Sparking a mixture of methane, ammonia, hydrogen and water vapour produced amino acids.

## Homeostasis

### Q40. Urea is produced in the liver by the:
A. Glycogenolysis
B. Gluconeogenesis
C. Krebs cycle
D. Urea cycle (ornithine cycle)
**Answer: D** — The urea cycle in hepatocytes converts toxic ammonium ions into urea for kidney excretion.
