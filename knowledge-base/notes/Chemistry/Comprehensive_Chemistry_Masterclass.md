## 1. Organic Chemistry Deep-Dive

### A. Reactivity & Dissociation Energy of Halocarbons
- **Concept:** Bond dissociation energy decreases as the size of the halogen increases down the group. Therefore, a weaker bond makes the molecule more reactive.
- **Dissociation Energy Trend:** C–F > C–Cl > C–Br > C–I
- **Reactivity Trend:** C–I > C–Br > C–Cl > C–F
- **🔥 Application:** If asked which group has **less dissociation energy and greater reactivity**, the answer is **C–I**.

### B. Alcohols and Carboxylic Acids (Solubility & Reactivity)
- **Solubility Rule (Chain Length):** Smaller carbon chains are more soluble in water because the hydrophobic (non-polar) alkyl chain is small, allowing hydrogen bonding to dominate. As chain length increases, solubility decreases.
- **Solubility Rule (Branching):** Tertiary > Secondary > Primary. Branching makes the molecule more spherical, decreasing the surface area of the hydrophobic portion, thus increasing solubility.
- **Reactivity of Alcohols:**
 - **O–H Bond Cleavage (Acidic Nature):** Primary > Secondary > Tertiary. (Primary alcohols are the least sterically hindered and most acidic).
 - **C–O Bond Cleavage (Nucleophilic Substitution):** Tertiary > Secondary > Primary. (Due to the stability of the tertiary carbocation formed).
- **Boiling Point of Carboxylic Acids:** The boiling point of a dimer of carboxylic acid is relatively higher than its monomer. 
 - **Reason:** Intermolecular **Hydrogen Bonding** causes two molecules to strongly associate.
- **Derivative Reactivity:** Acid Chlorides > Acid Anhydrides > Esters > Amides.

### C. Carbonyl Compounds (Aldehydes & Ketones)
- **Iodoform Test:** Gives a positive test (yellow precipitate) for compounds containing the **CH₃–CO–** (methyl ketone) group or **CH₃–CH(OH)–** group. 
 - **🔥 Application:** Between Acetaldehyde, Formaldehyde, Benzaldehyde, and Methanol... **Acetaldehyde** produces iodoform.
- **Alpha-Hydrogen Reactions:**
 - **Aldol Condensation:** Requires the presence of at least one alpha-hydrogen.
 - **Cannizzaro Reaction:** Occurs when alpha-hydrogens are **NOT** present (e.g., Formaldehyde, Benzaldehyde).
- **Reduction of Carbonyls:** 
 - Reduction of **Aldehydes** always gives **Primary (1°) Alcohols**.
 - Reduction of **Ketones** always gives **Secondary (2°) Alcohols**.

### D. Benzene & Aromatic Compounds
- **Nitration of Benzene:** The most active nitrating agent (the electrophile) during the nitration of benzene is the **Nitronium ion (NO₂⁺)**.
- **Oxidation Resistance:** Benzene does **not** undergo oxidation with alkaline KMnO₄ solution. This statement assumes the exceptional stability of the **delocalized pi electron ring** (aromaticity).
- **Friedel-Crafts Acylation:** Benzene reacts with **Acetyl Chloride (CH₃COCl)** in the presence of a Lewis acid catalyst (like AlCl₃) to produce **Acetophenone**.
- **Homolytic Bond Fission:** Occurs when a bond breaks evenly, giving one electron to each atom (forming free radicals). 
 - **Example:** **Photochlorination of Methane** (requires UV light/photons). *Note: Alkaline hydrolysis, addition of HBr, and nitration of benzene all involve heterolytic fission.*

### E. Polymers
- **Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC):** An important polymer extensively used to form plastics. Its formation (polymerization of vinyl chloride) primarily takes place by a **Radical Mechanism**.

## 2. Physical Chemistry & Calculations

### A. Solutions & Colligative Properties
- **Colligative Properties:** Properties that depend only on the *number* of solute particles, not their nature. They include:
 1. Relative lowering of vapor pressure.
 2. Elevation of boiling point.
 3. Depression of freezing point.
 4. Osmotic pressure.
- **🔥 Application:** What is *not* a colligative property? **Modification of refractive index**.
- **Boiling Point Elevation:** When a non-volatile solute (e.g., 2g of sugar) is added to a solvent (1000ml of water), the boiling point of the solution will be **Increased**.
- **Freezing Point Depression Calculation:**
 - Formula: $\Delta T_f = i \times K_f \times m$
 - For $0.1 m$ NaCl solution: $NaCl$ dissociates into 2 ions ($Na^+$ and $Cl^-$), so the Van't Hoff factor **$i = 2$**.
 - $\Delta T_f = 2 \times 1.86 \times 0.1 = 0.372^\circ C$.
 - Therefore, the expected freezing point is $0 - 0.372 =$ **$-0.372^\circ C$**.

### B. Molarity & Concentration
- **Concentration of NaOH:** 
 - Moles of NaOH = Mass / Molar Mass = $4g / 40(g/mol) = 0.1$ moles.
 - Volume = 1 Liter.
 - Concentration (Molarity) = $0.1 \text{ moles} / 1 \text{ L} =$ **$0.1 M$**.

### C. Buffer Solutions
- A buffer contains equal concentrations of $X^-$ and $HX$.
- If $K_b$ for $X^-$ is $10^{-10}$, we find $K_a$ for $HX$ using $K_w = K_a \times K_b$ ($10^{-14} = K_a \times 10^{-10}$).
- $K_a = 10^{-4}$, so $pK_a = 4$.
- By Henderson-Hasselbalch equation: $pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[X^-]}{[HX]}$.
- Since $[X^-] = [HX]$, the log term is $\log(1) = 0$.
- **Therefore, the pH of the buffer is 4.**

### D. Gas Laws
- **Graham's Law of Diffusion/Effusion:** The rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass ($M$) or density ($d$).
- **Temperature Relation:** The velocity of gas molecules relates directly to temperature (in Kelvin). 
- Formula: $\frac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}}$.

### E. Rate Expressions
- The rate expression (Rate Law) for a reaction cannot be predicted from the balanced chemical equation alone (unless it's an elementary step). It must be determined experimentally.

## 3. Inorganic Chemistry & Bonding

### A. Oxidation and Reduction
- **Oxidizing Agent:** A substance that *oxidizes* something else by *reducing* itself (gaining electrons).
- **Reducing Agent limitations:** The oxide which **cannot** act as a reducing agent is **CO₂**. 
 - **Reason:** In CO₂, Carbon is already in its highest possible oxidation state (+4) for Group 14. It cannot lose any more electrons to reduce another species. (SO₂, NO₂, ClO₂ can be further oxidized).

### B. Group 13 Halides (Lewis Acidity)
- Among the halides of Group 13 ($BF_3, AlCl_3, BCl_3, BBr_3$), the one which is **most acidic** is **BBr₃**.
- **Reason:** While F is the most electronegative, back-bonding ($p\pi-p\pi$) from Fluorine to Boron is highly effective in $BF_3$, satisfying Boron's electron deficiency. In $BBr_3$, the large size of Bromine makes back-bonding ineffective, leaving Boron highly electron-deficient and a stronger Lewis acid.

### C. Oxyacids of Chlorine
- **Trend:** The acidic strength and oxidizing power increase with the oxidation state of the central chlorine atom.
- **🔥 Application:** The strongest oxyacid of chlorine is **HClO₄** (Perchloric acid), where Cl is in the +7 oxidation state.

### D. Alkali Metals and Oxygen
- When burned in the air:
 - **Lithium (Li)** forms only a **monoxide** ($Li_2O$).
 - **Sodium (Na)** forms a **peroxide** ($Na_2O_2$).
 - **Potassium (K), Rubidium (Rb), Cesium (Cs)** form **superoxides** ($KO_2, RbO_2, CsO_2$).

### E. Solutions & Hydration
- When NaCl is dissolved in water, the crystal lattice breaks apart. The $Na^+$ ions (and $Cl^-$ ions) become surrounded by water molecules. This process is called being **hydrated**.

## 4. Mathematics Bonus: Triangles & Sines

### A. Law of Sines
The Law of Sines establishes the relationship between the lengths of the sides of a triangle and the sines of its angles:
**$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$**
- Use this when you know two angles and one side (AAS or ASA) or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA).

### B. Pythagorean Triples
For any right-angled triangle, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Knowing common integer triples saves massive amounts of time on the reasoning section.
- **3 - 4 - 5** (and multiples: 6-8-10, 9-12-15)
- **5 - 12 - 13**
- **8 - 15 - 17**
- **7 - 24 - 25**
