# Most Important Biological Molecules MCQs with Answers

## Biological Molecules MCQs with Answers

What is the primary focus of biochemistry?

Biochemistry is defined as the branch of biology that deals with the chemical compounds of living beings and the various chemical reactions (metabolism) that occur within them.

Which of the following best describes bioelements?

Bioelements are approximately 25 elements out of 92 naturally occurring elements on Earth that are found in living beings.

What percentage of the human body is constituted by the six major bioelements?

The six commonest bioelements (Oxygen, Carbon, Hydrogen, Nitrogen, Calcium, Phosphorus) constitute '99%' of the protoplasm in the human body.

Which of the following is considered a 'major bioelement' in the human body?

The six major bioelements that constitute '99%' of the human body are Oxygen (65%), Carbon (18%), Hydrogen (10%), Nitrogen (3%), Calcium (2%), and Phosphorus (1%). Potassium, Sulphur, and Iron are listed as minor or trace elements.

What distinguishes minor bioelements from major bioelements?

Minor bioelements are defined as those found as less than '1%' of the protoplasm, while trace elements are less than 0.01%.

Which of the following is NOT one of the four fundamental kinds of biological molecules?

The four fundamental kinds of biological molecules are carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. Water is an inorganic biomolecule, not one of the four fundamental organic macromolecules.

What percentage of a mammalian cell is composed of water?

According to Table 2.1, water constitutes '70%' of both bacterial and mammalian cells.

Which of the following organic biomolecules has the highest percentage in a mammalian cell?

In mammalian cells, proteins make up 18%, carbohydrates 4%, lipids 3%, and DNA 0.25%.

What is the approximate number of naturally occurring elements found in living beings?

Approximately 25 elements out of 92 naturally occurring elements on Earth are found in living beings and are called bioelements.

What is the branch of biology that deals with the composition and structure of chemical compounds in living organisms and the chemical processes within them?

Biochemistry is defined as the study of the composition and structure of different chemical compounds found in living organisms and the chemical processes taking place within or related to living organisms.

Which biological molecule is primarily responsible for providing a quick and readily available source of energy for cellular activities?

Carbohydrates, particularly glucose, are readily broken down by cells to release energy for immediate use, serving as a primary fuel source.

The diverse functions of biological membranes, such as transport and cell recognition, are largely attributed to which two types of biological molecules?

Biological membranes are primarily composed of a lipid bilayer, which provides the structural framework, and various proteins embedded within or associated with it, which carry out diverse functions like transport, enzymatic activity, and cell recognition.

Which biological molecule is crucial for carrying genetic information from the nucleus to the ribosomes for protein synthesis?

RNA (Ribonucleic Acid), specifically messenger RNA (mRNA), is responsible for carrying the genetic code from DNA in the nucleus to the ribosomes in the cytoplasm, where it is translated into proteins.

What is a key role of lipids in living organisms, besides energy storage?

Lipids, particularly fats, serve as long-term energy reserves, but also play crucial roles in insulating the body and cushioning vital organs against physical shock. They also form cell membranes.

Which class of biological molecules is responsible for a vast array of functions including enzymatic catalysis, structural support, transport, and defense?

Proteins are highly versatile macromolecules that perform a wide range of functions, acting as enzymes to speed up reactions, providing structural support (e.g., collagen, keratin), transporting substances (e.g., hemoglobin), and defending the body against pathogens (e.g., antibodies).

What is the primary reason for water's high polarity?

Water is highly polar due to the bent shape of the molecule and the unequal sharing of electrons, where oxygen is more electronegative, resulting in a partial negative charge on oxygen and partial positive charges on hydrogen atoms.

The ability of water to form hydrogen bonds is due to:

Hydrogen bonds form between the partially positive hydrogen atom of one water molecule and the partially negative oxygen atom of an adjacent water molecule due to the polar nature of water.

Water is often called the 'universal solvent' due to its ability to dissolve a wide range of substances. This property is primarily linked to its:

Water's high polarity allows it to form hydration shells around charged and polar molecules (hydrophilic substances), effectively dissolving them.

What term describes the tendency of water molecules to stick to other water molecules?

Cohesion is the property of water molecules to stick to each other due to extensive hydrogen bonding, which is responsible for surface tension.

The property of water that allows plants to transport water upwards through xylem vessels is primarily:

Cohesion among water molecules and adhesion of water molecules to the xylem walls create a continuous column of water that can be pulled upwards during transpiration.

Water has a high specific heat capacity. This property is vital for:

Water's high specific heat capacity helps living organisms resist drastic temperature changes, thus maintaining a stable internal environment (homeostasis).

Why does ice float on liquid water?

As water freezes, hydrogen bonds stabilize, forming an open, crystalline lattice that spaces the molecules further apart, making ice less dense than liquid water, allowing it to float.

The high heat of vaporization of water is important because it allows organisms to:

A large amount of energy is required to break the hydrogen bonds and convert liquid water to water vapor. This property allows for effective cooling as heat is removed from the body during evaporation (e.g., sweating).

Hydrolysis reactions, which involve the breaking of a bond by the addition of a water molecule, demonstrate water's role as a:

In hydrolysis, water actively participates in the chemical reaction by splitting into H+ and OH- and adding across a bond, thus acting as a reactant or reagent.

What is the process called when water molecules spontaneously dissociate into hydrogen ions (H+) and hydroxide ions (OH-)?

Water molecules have the ability to spontaneously dissociate or ionize into a hydrogen ion (H+) and a hydroxide ion (OH-).

The surface tension of water, allowing insects to walk on its surface, is a direct result of:

Cohesion, the strong attraction between water molecules due to hydrogen bonding, creates surface tension at the air-water interface.

When a substance like oil separates from water, forming distinct layers, this phenomenon is known as:

Hydrophobic exclusion describes the tendency of nonpolar substances (like oil) to aggregate and separate from water, as water molecules prefer to hydrogen bond with each other rather than with the nonpolar molecules.

Which property of water is crucial for aquatic life in cold climates, preventing entire bodies of water from freezing solid?

The lower density of ice means it floats on the surface, insulating the water below and preventing it from freezing completely, allowing aquatic organisms to survive.

What type of bond is responsible for the unique properties of water, such as its high boiling point compared to similar-sized molecules?

The extensive network of hydrogen bonds between water molecules requires a significant amount of energy to break, leading to its unusually high boiling point.

Water has a pH of 7. This neutrality is a result of water's:

The ionization of water into H+ and OH- ions is fundamental to the pH scale and the concept of neutrality (where [H+] = [OH-]).

Water molecules adhere to the walls of narrow tubes, a phenomenon called capillarity. This is an example of:

Adhesion is the attraction between water molecules and molecules of other substances, such as the cellulose in plant cell walls or glass in a narrow tube.

When a plant wilts due to lack of water, which property of water is directly related to its ability to maintain turgor pressure in plant cells?

Water, acting as a solvent, fills the plant cells, and its cohesive properties help maintain turgor pressure within the cells, which prevents wilting.

Why is water considered an excellent thermal buffer in biological systems?

Water's high specific heat capacity allows it to absorb excess heat when metabolic activity is high and release it when the environment is cold, thus buffering temperature fluctuations.

What is the approximate pH of pure water at 25°C?

Pure water at 25°C is neutral, meaning the concentration of H+ and OH- ions are equal, resulting in a pH of 7.

In what state are water molecules most spread out due to hydrogen bonding?

In ice, hydrogen bonds form a stable, crystalline lattice where molecules are more spread out compared to the constantly breaking and reforming bonds in liquid water, leading to its lower density.

What is the general molecular formula for monosaccharides?

Monosaccharides are true carbohydrates represented by the general molecular formula (CH2O)n, where 'n' is the number of carbon atoms, ranging from 3 to 7.

Which of the following is a characteristic of monosaccharides?

Monosaccharides are the simplest sugars, defined as polyhydroxy aldehydes (aldoses) or polyhydroxy ketones (ketoses).

Glucose is classified as a:

Glucose is a simple sugar, specifically a hexose monosaccharide, as it cannot be hydrolyzed into simpler sugar units.

What type of monosaccharide is glucose, based on its functional group?

Glucose is an aldose because it contains an aldehyde functional group (-CHO).

How many carbon atoms are typically found in monosaccharides?

The number of carbon atoms in monosaccharides ranges from 3 to 7.

Which of the following is an example of an oligosaccharide?

Oligosaccharides are carbohydrates that yield 2 to 10 monosaccharide units upon hydrolysis. Sucrose (cane sugar), maltose, and lactose are common disaccharides, a type of oligosaccharide.

What type of bond links two monosaccharides together to form a disaccharide?

Monosaccharide units are joined together by a covalent bond known as a glycosidic bond, formed by a condensation reaction.

Sucrose is a disaccharide formed by the condensation of which two monosaccharides?

Sucrose is commonly known as cane sugar or table sugar and is formed by the condensation of one molecule of glucose and one molecule of fructose.

Lactose, the sugar found in milk, is composed of which two monosaccharides?

Lactose is formed by the condensation of one molecule of glucose and one molecule of galactose.

Which polysaccharide is the main storage carbohydrate in plants?

Starch is the principal carbohydrate stored in plant cells as an energy reserve.

Which polysaccharide provides structural support in plant cell walls?

Cellulose is a fibrous polysaccharide that forms the major structural component of plant cell walls.

Glycogen is the main storage polysaccharide in:

Glycogen is the primary storage form of glucose in animals, especially abundant in the liver and muscle cells.

What is the key difference between alpha-glucose and beta-glucose?

Alpha-glucose and beta-glucose are anomers, differing in the orientation of the hydroxyl group (-OH) on the anomeric carbon (carbon 1) when in ring form.

Which polysaccharide is a component of the exoskeleton of insects and crustaceans?

Chitin is a structural polysaccharide found in the cell walls of fungi and the exoskeletons of arthropods (insects, crustaceans).

The conversion of a linear monosaccharide into its ring form involves a reaction between the carbonyl group and a hydroxyl group. This ring form is more stable in:

In aqueous solutions, monosaccharides with five or more carbon atoms predominantly exist in cyclic (ring) forms, which are more stable than their open-chain counterparts.

Maltose is a disaccharide formed from two units of:

Maltose is a disaccharide commonly known as malt sugar, composed of two glucose units joined by an alpha-1,4 glycosidic bond.

What type of reaction is involved in the formation of disaccharides from monosaccharides?

Disaccharides are formed from two monosaccharides by a condensation reaction, where a molecule of water is removed.

Which term refers to isomers that are mirror images of each other, like D-glucose and L-glucose?

D-glucose and L-glucose are stereoisomers, specifically enantiomers, meaning they are non-superimposable mirror images of each other.

Why are laboratory-manufactured 'left-handed' sugars not metabolized by human enzymes?

Biological systems, including human enzymes, are highly stereospecific. Most naturally occurring sugars are D-isomers, and the enzymes that process them are structured to recognize and act upon these 'right-handed' forms, making L-isomers non-metabolizable.

What is the primary role of starch in a potato plant?

Starch is the primary storage polysaccharide in plants, allowing them to store excess glucose as a readily available energy reserve.

Which carbohydrate is sometimes referred to as 'animal starch'?

Glycogen is the animal equivalent of starch and is often referred to as 'animal starch' due to its role as the primary glucose storage form in animals.

Which of the following is a characteristic of polysaccharides?

Polysaccharides are complex carbohydrates (macromolecules) formed by the polymerization of hundreds to thousands of monosaccharide units linked by glycosidic bonds. They are generally not sweet and are less soluble or insoluble in water.

The hydrolysis of a disaccharide yields:

Hydrolysis is the reverse of condensation; it involves the addition of a water molecule to break the glycosidic bond, yielding two monosaccharide units from a disaccharide.

Which of these properties is true for monosaccharides?

Monosaccharides are simple sugars, generally sweet in taste, readily soluble in water, and can be oxidized.

In the open-chain structure of a monosaccharide, all carbon atoms except one have a:

In the open-chain form of a monosaccharide, all carbon atoms except one (which is part of an aldehyde or ketone group) have a hydroxyl (-OH) group attached.

What are the basic building blocks of proteins?

Proteins are polymers made up of repeating monomer units called amino acids, linked together by peptide bonds.

Which of the following functional groups are characteristic components of an amino acid?

Every amino acid contains at least one amino group (-NH2) and one carboxyl group (-COOH), both attached to the central carbon atom (alpha-carbon).

The unique characteristic of each amino acid is determined by its:

The R-group, or side chain, differs among the 20 amino acids and gives each its unique properties.

What type of bond links amino acids together to form a polypeptide chain?

Amino acids are linked by peptide bonds, formed during a condensation reaction between the carboxyl and amino groups.

The formation of a peptide bond involves a reaction where:

Peptide bond formation is a dehydration synthesis reaction where a water molecule is removed.

The primary structure of a protein refers to its:

The primary structure is the unique linear sequence of amino acids in a protein.

Sickle cell hemoglobin is an example illustrating the significance of:

A single amino acid change in hemoglobin leads to sickle cell disease, showing the importance of amino acid sequence.

Which level of protein structure involves the folding of the polypeptide chain into alpha-helices and beta-pleated sheets, stabilized by hydrogen bonds?

Secondary structure includes localized folding like alpha-helices and beta-sheets, stabilized by hydrogen bonds.

The overall three-dimensional shape of a single polypeptide chain, including all its side chain interactions, defines its:

Tertiary structure is the full 3D structure formed by the folding and interactions of the polypeptide chain.

What type of protein structure results from the association of two or more separate polypeptide chains?

Quaternary structure describes the interaction and arrangement of multiple polypeptide subunits in a protein.

Enzymes, which catalyze biochemical reactions, are primarily what type of biological molecule?

Most enzymes are proteins that catalyze biological reactions.

Which of the following is a characteristic of fibrous proteins?

Fibrous proteins have elongated shapes and provide structural support (e.g., collagen, keratin).

Globular proteins typically have which of the following characteristics?

Globular proteins are spherical, soluble, and perform functional roles in the body.

The breakdown of a protein into smaller polypeptide fragments or individual amino acids is called:

Hydrolysis is the reaction that breaks peptide bonds by adding water, releasing amino acids.

What type of interaction is crucial for maintaining the tertiary and quaternary structures of proteins, often involving the burying of nonpolar side chains away from water?

Hydrophobic interactions drive protein folding by keeping nonpolar side chains away from the aqueous environment.

What is a defining characteristic of lipids?

Lipids are a diverse group of organic compounds characterized by their insolubility in water and solubility in nonpolar organic solvents due to their nonpolar hydrocarbon nature.

Triglycerides (acylglycerols) are formed by the esterification of glycerol with:

Triglycerides are formed when glycerol reacts with three fatty acids via ester bonds in a condensation reaction.

What is the primary biological role of triglycerides?

Triglycerides serve as the main form of long-term energy storage in many organisms.

Phospholipids are crucial components of cell membranes due to their:

Phospholipids are amphipathic, having both a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails, enabling them to form cell membranes.

Which of the following is an example of a steroid?

Cholesterol is a steroid composed of a four-ring structure and is a precursor for many hormones.

Waxes are lipids primarily known for their role in:

Waxes are hydrophobic lipids that serve as waterproof coatings on plant leaves, fruits, and animal surfaces.

Terpenes are a diverse group of lipids that include which important biological molecules?

Terpenes include biologically important molecules like vitamins A, D, E, K and pigments like carotenoids.

Which part of a phospholipid molecule is hydrophilic?

The phosphate group is polar and hydrophilic, making it the water-attracting portion of a phospholipid.

The conversion of a fatty acid from a saturated to an unsaturated state involves:

Unsaturated fatty acids contain double bonds, unlike saturated fatty acids which have only single bonds.

Prostaglandins are a group of lipid compounds that act as:

Prostaglandins are eicosanoids that function as local hormones, playing roles in inflammation and other processes.

Which of the following is a key structural difference between RNA and DNA?

RNA differs from DNA by having ribose instead of deoxyribose and uracil instead of thymine. RNA is usually single-stranded; both contain phosphate groups.

What is the primary role of messenger RNA?

Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries genetic code from the DNA to ribosomes, where it is translated into proteins.

Which type of RNA plays a crucial role in protein synthesis by catalyzing peptide bond formation?

Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) is part of ribosomes and catalyzes the formation of peptide bonds during protein synthesis.

What defines a conjugated molecule?

Conjugated molecules consist of two or more different biological components, such as protein and carbohydrate.

Glycoproteins are crucial components of cell membranes, often involved in:

Glycoproteins help in cell communication and recognition by acting as molecular identifiers on cell surfaces.

Which conjugated molecule is primarily responsible for the transport of lipids (like cholesterol and triglycerides) in the bloodstream?

Lipoproteins transport hydrophobic lipids in blood. LDL and HDL are examples of lipoproteins.

Glycolipids are found in cell membranes, particularly abundant in nervous tissue, and contribute to:

Glycolipids are involved in cell recognition and communication, especially in neural tissue.

Which type of conjugated molecule forms structures like chromosomes and ribosomes, playing roles in genetic information storage and protein synthesis?

Nucleoproteins are DNA/RNA-protein complexes found in chromosomes and ribosomes, essential for gene expression and protein production.
