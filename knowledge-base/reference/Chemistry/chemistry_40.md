## Alcohols and Phenols

### Q1. Propan-2-ol is classified as which type of alcohol?
A. Primary
B. Tertiary
C. Quaternary
D. Secondary
**Answer: D** — The carbon bearing the hydroxyl group is attached to two other carbon atoms; which defines a secondary alcohol.

### Q2. Phenol is more acidic than ethanol because:
A. Ethanol has a longer carbon chain
B. Phenol has more hydroxyl groups
C. The phenoxide anion is resonance-stabilized
D. Ethanol is a stronger acid
**Answer: C** — The phenoxide anion is resonance-stabilized — negative charge delocalized into the ring.

### Q3. The IUPAC name of CH₃OH is:
A. Ethanol
B. Propanol
C. Dimethyl alcohol
D. Methanol
**Answer: D** — CH₃OH is a one-carbon alcohol. IUPAC name: methanol.

### Q4. Phenol is more reactive than benzene toward electrophilic substitution because:
A. Benzene is too stable
B. The −OH group donates electrons to the ring via resonance (+M effect)
C. Phenol has fewer hydrogen atoms
D. Benzene has a ring structure
**Answer: B** — The −OH group donates electrons to the ring via resonance, increasing electron density.

### Q5. Ethanol is produced industrially from ethene by:
A. Hydration over phosphoric acid catalyst
B. Fermentation only
C. Reduction of acetic acid
D. Hydrolysis of ethyl chloride
**Answer: A** — Ethene and steam pass over a phosphoric acid catalyst at high temperature and pressure.

### Q6. Phenol is more acidic than ethanol because the phenoxide ion is:
A. Resonance stabilized
B. More basic
C. Insoluble in water
D. Larger in size
**Answer: A** — The negative charge is spread over the ring by resonance; this stabilisation makes loss of the proton more favourable.

### Q7. Sodium ethoxide dissolved in water gives a solution that is:
A. Alkaline
B. Acidic
C. Neutral
D. Amphoteric
**Answer: A** — The ethoxide ion is a stronger base than hydroxide; so it removes a proton from water generating hydroxide ions.

### Q8. Benzene requires a halogen carrier for bromination, whereas phenol needs none, because in phenol the:
A. Ring is smaller
B. Electron donation from hydroxyl oxygen raises ring electron density
C. Bromine is more reactive
D. Temperature is higher
**Answer: B** — Electron donation from the hydroxyl oxygen raises the ring's electron density so much that it attacks bromine without a catalyst.

## Aldehydes and Ketones

### Q9. The IUPAC name of CH₃CHO is:
A. Methanal
B. Ethanal
C. Propanal
D. Ethanol
**Answer: B** — CH₃CHO is a 2-carbon aldehyde. IUPAC name: ethanal (acetaldehyde).

### Q10. The characteristic reaction of aldehydes and ketones is:
A. Electrophilic substitution
B. Nucleophilic addition
C. Free radical substitution
D. Elimination
**Answer: B** — The carbonyl group (C=O) is polar; nucleophiles attack the electrophilic carbon.

### Q11. Tollen's test is positive for:
A. Ketones
B. Alcohols
C. Aldehydes
D. Carboxylic acids
**Answer: C** — Tollen's reagent (ammoniacal AgNO₃) is reduced by aldehydes to give a silver mirror.

### Q12. Aldehydes can be prepared by mild oxidation of:
A. Primary alcohols
B. Secondary alcohols
C. Tertiary alcohols
D. Ethers
**Answer: A** — PCC (pyridinium chlorochromate) oxidizes primary alcohols to aldehydes without over-oxidation.

### Q13. Which is more reactive toward nucleophilic addition: formaldehyde or acetone?
A. Acetone
B. Formaldehyde
C. Both equal
D. Neither reacts
**Answer: B** — Formaldehyde is more reactive because it has minimal steric hindrance and lacks electron-donating methyl groups.

### Q14. Reduction of an aldehyde with NaBH₄ gives a:
A. Carboxylic acid
B. Ketone
C. Ester
D. Primary alcohol
**Answer: D** — Reduction of an aldehyde adds two hydrogen atoms across C=O to give a primary alcohol.

## Alkyl Halides

### Q15. 2-bromopropane is classified as which type of alkyl halide?
A. Primary
B. Tertiary
C. Quaternary
D. Secondary
**Answer: D** — The carbon bearing the bromine is attached to two other carbon atoms; a secondary halide.

### Q16. In E2 elimination, the reaction is:
A. One-step bimolecular
B. Two-step unimolecular
C. Three-step
D. Equilibrium
**Answer: A** — E2 is a one-step bimolecular process: Rate = k[substrate][base].

### Q17. The IUPAC name of CH₃CH₂Cl is:
A. Methyl chloride
B. Dichloroethane
C. Chloromethane
D. Chloroethane
**Answer: D** — The parent chain is ethane, and Cl is at position 1. Chloroethane.

### Q18. In a nucleophilic substitution reaction of an alkyl halide, the halogen leaves as:
A. A cation
B. A free radical
C. A neutral atom
D. A halide ion (leaving group)
**Answer: D** — The nucleophile displaces the halogen; which departs as a stable halide ion.

### Q19. SN2 reactions are favored by:
A. Tertiary substrates
B. Weak nucleophiles
C. Polar protic solvents
D. Primary substrates, strong nucleophiles, polar aprotic solvents
**Answer: D** — SN2 is favored by primary substrates, strong nucleophiles, and polar aprotic solvents.

### Q20. Which carbon-halogen bond is the weakest and most easily broken?
A. C-F
B. C-Cl
C. C-I
D. C-Br
**Answer: C** — Bond strength falls down the group; the carbon-iodine bond is the weakest.

## Atomic Structure

### Q21. The proton was discovered by:
A. J.J. Thomson
B. Rutherford
C. Chadwick
D. Goldstein
**Answer: D** — Goldstein discovered canal rays (protons) in 1886.

### Q22. According to Aufbau principle, electrons fill orbitals in order of:
A. Decreasing energy
B. Random order
C. Increasing atomic number only
D. Increasing energy
**Answer: D** — The Aufbau principle states that electrons fill orbitals starting from the lowest energy level.

### Q23. According to Planck's quantum theory, energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called:
A. Waves
B. Atoms
C. Quanta (photons)
D. Electrons
**Answer: C** — Planck proposed that energy is emitted/absorbed in quanta (photons), not continuously.

### Q24. The principal quantum number (n) determines the:
A. Shape of orbital
B. Orientation of orbital
C. Energy level and size of the orbital
D. Spin of electron
**Answer: C** — The principal quantum number n determines the energy level and size of the orbital.

### Q25. The shape of an s orbital is:
A. Dumbbell
B. Cloverleaf
C. Cylindrical
D. Spherical
**Answer: D** — All s orbitals are spherically symmetrical in shape.

### Q26. The Balmer series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A. Infrared region
B. Ultraviolet region
C. X-ray region
D. Visible region
**Answer: D** — The Balmer series corresponds to transitions to n=2 and lies in the visible region.

## Carboxylic Acids

### Q27. Which reagent distinguishes a carboxylic acid from a phenol?
A. Sodium hydrogen carbonate
B. Sodium hydroxide
C. Water
D. Ethanol
**Answer: A** — Only the carboxylic acid is strong enough to displace CO₂ from hydrogen carbonate.

### Q28. Acyl halides are prepared from carboxylic acids using:
A. HCl
B. NaCl
C. Thionyl chloride (SOCl₂)
D. H₂SO₄
**Answer: C** — R−COOH + SOCl₂ → R−COCl + SO₂ + HCl.

### Q29. The linkage present in an ester is formed between a carboxylic acid and:
A. An alcohol
B. An amine
C. An aldehyde
D. A ketone
**Answer: A** — The alkoxy group of the alcohol replaces the hydroxyl of the acid.

### Q30. The IUPAC name of CH₃COOH is:
A. Methanoic acid
B. Ethanoic acid
C. Propanoic acid
D. Butanoic acid
**Answer: B** — CH₃COOH is a 2-carbon carboxylic acid. IUPAC: ethanoic acid.

### Q31. A primary alcohol on vigorous oxidation with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ gives:
A. Aldehyde only
B. Carboxylic acid
C. Ketone
D. Ester
**Answer: B** — Oxidation proceeds through the aldehyde but continues to the carboxylic acid under vigorous conditions.

### Q32. Which pair of reactants would give the ester ethyl ethanoate?
A. Methanol + ethanoic acid
B. Ethanol + ethanoic acid
C. Ethanol + methanoic acid
D. Propanol + ethanoic acid
**Answer: B** — The alkyl part comes from the alcohol (ethanol) and the acyl part from the acid (ethanoic acid).

### Q33. Carboxylic acids are more acidic than alcohols because:
A. The carboxylate ion is resonance stabilized
B. They have longer carbon chains
C. They have more hydrogen atoms
D. They are larger molecules
**Answer: A** — The carboxylate ion (RCOO⁻) is resonance stabilized with negative charge delocalized over two oxygen atoms.

### Q34. The functional group characteristic of a carboxylic acid is:
A. −COOH
B. −CHO
C. −OH
D. −CO−
**Answer: A** — The carboxyl group combines a carbonyl and a hydroxyl on the same carbon.

## Chemical Bonding

### Q35. Bond energy is defined as the energy required to:
A. Form one mole of bonds
B. Break one mole of a particular bond in gaseous molecules
C. Change one mole of solid to gas
D. Heat one mole of substance
**Answer: B** — Bond energy is the energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond in gaseous molecules.

### Q36. Which molecule has zero dipole moment?
A. CO₂
B. H₂O
C. NH₃
D. HCl
**Answer: A** — CO₂ is linear with two equal C=O dipoles pointing in opposite directions, cancelling out.

### Q37. The hybridization of carbon in methane (CH₄) is:
A. sp
B. sp³
C. sp²
D. dsp³
**Answer: B** — Carbon in CH₄ forms 4 equivalent bonds using sp³ hybridization, giving tetrahedral geometry.

### Q38. A sigma (σ) bond is formed by:
A. Lateral overlap of p-orbitals
B. Head-on (axial) overlap of orbitals
C. Overlap of d-orbitals
D. No overlap required
**Answer: B** — Sigma bonds result from head-on (axial) overlap of orbitals along the internuclear axis.

### Q39. According to VSEPR theory, the shape of CH₄ is:
A. Tetrahedral
B. Trigonal planar
C. Linear
D. Bent
**Answer: A** — CH₄ has 4 bond pairs and no lone pairs, giving tetrahedral geometry with 109.5° angles.

## Chemical Equilibrium

### Q40. A buffer solution resists changes in:
A. Temperature
B. pH
C. Volume
D. Mass
**Answer: B** — A buffer solution resists changes in pH upon addition of small amounts of acid or base.
