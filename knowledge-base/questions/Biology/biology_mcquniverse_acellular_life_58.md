# MDCAT Biology — Acellular Life MCQs — Source: MCQUniverse.pk

## Acellular Life

### Q1. Which of the following is a key characteristic that considers viruses non-living?
A. They have DNA or RNA.
B. They can mutate.
C. They lack cellular structure.
D. They are obligate intracellular parasites.
**Answer: C** — Viruses lack cellular structure, a key non-living characteristic.

### Q2. The complete viral particle, consisting of a nucleic acid core and a protein coat, is called a:
A. Gene
B. Capsomere
C. Virion
D. Prion
**Answer: C** — A virion is the complete viral particle (nucleic acid core + protein coat).

### Q3. Which component of a virus protects the viral genome from host nucleases?
A. Tail fibers
B. Envelope
C. Matrix protein
D. Capsid
**Answer: D** — The capsid (protein coat) protects the viral genome.

### Q4. Viruses that attack bacteria are specifically known as:
A. Retroviruses
B. Bacteriophages
C. Viroids
D. Prions
**Answer: B** — Viruses that attack bacteria are bacteriophages.

### Q5. What type of nucleic acid is predominantly found in plant viruses?
A. Double-stranded DNA
B. Single-stranded DNA
C. Double-stranded RNA
D. Single-stranded RNA
**Answer: D** — Plant viruses predominantly contain single-stranded RNA.

### Q6. An additional lipoprotein layer derived from the host cell membrane that surrounds some viruses is called the:
A. Capsid
B. Envelope
C. Matrix protein
D. Spike
**Answer: B** — The envelope is a lipoprotein layer derived from the host cell membrane.

### Q7. The protein subunits that make up the capsid of a virus are called:
A. Glycoproteins
B. Capsomeres
C. Spikes
D. Enzymes
**Answer: B** — Capsomeres are the protein subunits making up the capsid.

### Q8. Viruses that cause diseases like smallpox and polio are categorized as:
A. Bacteriophages
B. Plant viruses
C. Animal viruses
D. Fungal viruses
**Answer: C** — Smallpox and polio are caused by animal viruses.

### Q9. Which of the following viral symmetries is characteristic of Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)?
A. Icosahedral
B. Polyhedral
C. Helical
D. Complex
**Answer: C** — TMV has helical symmetry.

### Q10. David Baltimore's classification system for viruses is primarily based on:
A. Host specificity
B. Mode of replication and genome type
C. Presence or absence of an envelope
D. Size and shape of the virion
**Answer: B** — Baltimore classification is based on mode of replication and genome type.

### Q11. What is the primary function of glycoprotein spikes on the viral envelope?
A. Energy production for the virus
B. Replication of the viral genome
C. Recognition and attachment to host cell receptors
D. Breakdown of host cell DNA
**Answer: C** — Glycoprotein spikes enable recognition and attachment to host cell receptors.

### Q12. Which of the following is NOT typically found in the core of a virion?
A. Viral genome
B. Enzymes for replication
C. Ribosomes
D. Reverse transcriptase
**Answer: C** — Ribosomes are never found in a virion core.

### Q13. A virus with a polyhedral or spherical shape typically exhibits which type of capsid symmetry?
A. Helical
B. Filamentous
C. Icosahedral
D. Complex
**Answer: C** — Spherical/polyhedral viruses exhibit icosahedral symmetry.

### Q14. The term "virus" originates from a Latin word meaning:
A. Small
B. Toxin
C. Poison
D. Invisible
**Answer: C** — "Virus" derives from the Latin word meaning "poison."

### Q15. Which of the following is considered a "living characteristic" of viruses?
A. Can be crystallized and stored
B. Lack cellular structure
C. Behave as inert particles outside host
D. Reproduce using host cell machinery
**Answer: D** — Reproduction using host cell machinery is a living characteristic.

### Q16. Which type of virus is characterized by a dsDNA genome and a "tadpole" shape?
A. Plant virus
B. Bacteriophage
C. Animal virus with envelope
D. Retrovirus
**Answer: B** — Bacteriophages are tadpole-shaped dsDNA viruses.

### Q17. The property of viruses that allows them to be crystallized without losing infectivity is considered a:
A. Living characteristic
B. Non-living characteristic
C. Metabolic characteristic
D. Genetic characteristic
**Answer: B** — Crystallization without losing infectivity is a non-living characteristic.

### Q18. Which of the following is an example of an animal virus that is dsDNA?
A. TMV
B. HIV
C. Adenovirus
D. Poliovirus
**Answer: C** — Adenovirus is a dsDNA animal virus.

### Q19. What is the specific enzyme found in retroviruses that converts their ssRNA genome into dsDNA?
A. DNA polymerase
B. RNA polymerase
C. Reverse transcriptase
D. Ligase
**Answer: C** — Reverse transcriptase converts ssRNA into dsDNA.

### Q20. Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites because they lack:
A. A protein coat
B. A nucleic acid genome
C. Metabolic enzymes
D. Specific host receptors
**Answer: C** — Viruses lack metabolic enzymes, making them obligate intracellular parasites.

### Q21. Which structural component is found in bacteriophages but not in animal viruses like HIV?
A. Capsid
B. Nucleic acid
C. Tail fibers
D. Envelope
**Answer: C** — Tail fibers are found in bacteriophages.

### Q22. The number of capsomeres in a virus's capsid is:
A. Variable and changes with host
B. Always 252
C. Specific to the particular virus
D. Determined by the host cell
**Answer: C** — Capsomere number is specific to each virus.

### Q23. Which classification includes viruses that cause Rous sarcoma and warts?
A. Bacteriophages
B. Plant viruses
C. Animal viruses
D. Fungal viruses
**Answer: C** — Rous sarcoma and warts are caused by animal viruses.

### Q24. Which viral component protects the genome from denaturation by pH and temperature outside the host?
A. Tail fibers
B. Glycoproteins
C. Capsid
D. Reverse transcriptase
**Answer: C** — The capsid protects the viral genome.

### Q25. Why are non-enveloped viruses generally more stable and survive longer outside the host?
A. They have a stronger capsid
B. Their genetic material is more robust
C. Their outer protein coat is less susceptible to degradation than a lipid envelope
D. They do not require a host to replicate
**Answer: C** — Protein coat is less susceptible to degradation than a lipid envelope.

### Q26. TMV is an example of a virus primarily classified by its:
A. Complex structure
B. Enveloped nature
C. Helical symmetry
D. Icosahedral symmetry
**Answer: C** — TMV has helical symmetry.

### Q27. Which enzyme is found within the viral core of retroviruses like HIV?
A. DNA ligase
B. RNA primase
C. Integrase
D. Helicase
**Answer: C** — Integrase is found within the viral core of retroviruses.

### Q28. Iwanowsky's (1892) experiment with tobacco mosaic disease showed the causative agent was:
A. A bacterium that could be cultured
B. A toxin produced by plants
C. A filterable agent smaller than bacteria
D. A fungus visible under a light microscope
**Answer: C** — A filterable agent smaller than bacteria.

### Q29. Viruses are distinct from prions and viroids primarily because viruses possess:
A. A protein coat
B. A nucleic acid genome
C. The ability to cause disease
D. Resistance to heat
**Answer: B** — Viruses possess a nucleic acid genome.

### Q30. The causative agent of AIDS was identified in which year?
A. 1980
B. 1982
C. 1984
D. 1986
**Answer: C** — The AIDS causative agent was identified in 1984.

### Q31. Which of the following describes the shape of HIV?
A. Tadpole-shaped
B. Rod-shaped
C. Spherical
D. Filamentous
**Answer: C** — HIV is spherical.

### Q32. What are the two main glycoproteins embedded in the HIV envelope?
A. gp41 and gp160
B. gp120 and gp41
C. gp24 and gp17
D. gp120 and gp60
**Answer: B** — gp120 and gp41 are the main envelope glycoproteins of HIV.

### Q33. The enzyme responsible for converting ssRNA HIV genome into cDNA is:
A. Integrase
B. Transcriptase
C. Reverse transcriptase
D. RNA polymerase
**Answer: C** — Reverse transcriptase converts the ssRNA genome into cDNA.

### Q34. Which host cell does HIV primarily attack due to CD4 receptors?
A. B cells
B. Red blood cells
C. Helper T cells
D. Natural killer cells
**Answer: C** — HIV attacks Helper T cells via CD4 receptors.

### Q35. In the HIV life cycle, after dsDNA is formed, what is the next step?
A. Reverse transcription
B. Transcription of viral mRNA
C. Integration into host cell DNA
D. Assembly of new virions
**Answer: C** — dsDNA integrates into host cell DNA.

### Q36. What is the term for the integrated viral DNA within the host cell chromosome during HIV life cycle?
A. Provirus
B. Prophage
C. Plasmid
D. Virion
**Answer: A** — The integrated viral DNA is called the provirus.

### Q37. Which early symptom appears during the "asymptomatic carrier" stage of AIDS?
A. Severe weight loss
B. Persistent night sweats
C. Swollen lymph glands
D. Opportunistic infections
**Answer: C** — Swollen lymph glands appear in the asymptomatic carrier stage.

### Q38. The primary reason HIV leads to AIDS is that it:
A. Directly causes skin cancer
B. Produces toxins
C. Destroys T-cells
D. Targets red blood cells
**Answer: C** — HIV destroys T-cells, leading to immunosuppression.

### Q39. Diseases that attack an AIDS victim due to weakened immunity are known as:
A. Autoimmune diseases
B. Genetic diseases
C. Opportunistic diseases
D. Communicable diseases
**Answer: C** — They are opportunistic diseases.

### Q40. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) for HIV is best described as:
A. A complete cure
B. Boosts the immune system directly
C. Controls the virus and prolongs life but is not a cure
D. Prevents attachment of HIV to cells
**Answer: C** — ART controls the virus and prolongs life but is not a cure.

### Q41. Which is a common mode of HIV transmission?
A. Sharing food utensils
B. Mosquito bites
C. Casual contact like hugging
D. Sharing used syringes/needles
**Answer: D** — Sharing used syringes/needles transmits HIV.

### Q42. The HIV matrix proteins form a shell located:
A. Inside the capsid
B. Between the envelope and the capsid
C. Outside the envelope
D. Embedded within the viral RNA
**Answer: B** — Matrix proteins lie between the envelope and capsid.

### Q43. Which stage of AIDS is characterized by persistent swollen lymph glands, night sweats, and persistent diarrhea?
A. Asymptomatic carrier
B. Full Blown AIDS
C. AIDS Related Complex (ARC)
D. Incubation period
**Answer: C** — AIDS Related Complex.

### Q44. Uncoating of the HIV virion after entry results in release of:
A. Only viral proteins
B. Viral RNA and enzymes
C. Only viral DNA
D. Entire intact virion
**Answer: B** — Uncoating releases viral RNA and enzymes.

### Q45. Which is NOT a common control measure against HIV transmission?
A. Screening blood for transfusion
B. Sharing toothbrushes
C. Not using used syringes
D. All of the options
**Answer: B** — Sharing toothbrushes is NOT a control measure.

### Q46. The lipid bilayer of the HIV envelope is primarily derived from what source?
A. Viral biosynthesis
B. Host cell membrane during budding
C. Host cell nucleus
D. Environmental lipids
**Answer: B** — The lipid bilayer comes from the host cell membrane during budding.

### Q47. The enzyme that facilitates incorporation of HIV dsDNA into host DNA is:
A. Reverse transcriptase
B. Protease
C. Integrase
D. RNA polymerase
**Answer: C** — Integrase facilitates integration into host DNA.

### Q48. Which condition is considered an 'opportunistic disease' that may attack an AIDS victim?
A. Common cold
B. Influenza
C. Skin cancer
D. Measles
**Answer: C** — Skin cancer is an opportunistic disease.

### Q49. HIV is primarily named so because it directly leads to:
A. Formation of antibodies against itself
B. A decrease in Helper T cells
C. An increase in red blood cell count
D. Destruction of all white blood cells
**Answer: B** — HIV leads to a decrease in Helper T cells.

### Q50. What happens to the host cell after mature HIV virions bud off?
A. It becomes immortal
B. It immediately undergoes lysis
C. It loses functionality and eventually dies
D. It converts to a different cell type
**Answer: C** — The host cell loses functionality and eventually dies.

### Q51. Which is NOT a mode of HIV transmission?
A. Sexual contact
B. Mosquito bites
C. Mother to newborn during birth
D. Blood transfusion with unscreened blood
**Answer: B** — Mosquito bites do not transmit HIV.

### Q52. Severe weight loss and weakness are characteristic symptoms of which AIDS stage?
A. Asymptomatic carrier
B. AIDS Related Complex (ARC)
C. Full Blown AIDS
D. Initial infection stage
**Answer: C** — Full Blown AIDS.

### Q53. The enzyme that breaks down large structural proteins into smaller functional units during HIV biosynthesis is:
A. Reverse transcriptase
B. Integrase
C. Protease
D. Ribonuclease
**Answer: C** — Protease breaks down large proteins.

### Q54. After HIV's ssDNA is synthesized, what enzyme disintegrates the viral genomic RNA?
A. Reverse transcriptase
B. Ribonuclease
C. Transcriptase
D. Protease
**Answer: B** — Ribonuclease disintegrates the viral genomic RNA.

### Q55. What is the effect of ART on HIV-positive individuals?
A. Eliminates the virus completely
B. Significantly delays progression and can extend lifespan
C. One-time treatment that guarantees cure
D. No side effects and inexpensive
**Answer: B** — ART significantly delays progression and extends lifespan.

### Q56. What is the role of gp120 glycoprotein in the HIV life cycle?
A. Fusion of viral and host membranes
B. Integration of viral DNA
C. Attachment to CD4 proteins on host cells
D. Cleavage of viral proteins
**Answer: C** — gp120 attaches to CD4 proteins on host cells.

### Q57. The process by which HIV enters the host cell after fusion of the viral envelope with the cell membrane is:
A. Exocytosis
B. Direct Fusion
C. Endocytosis
D. Active transport
**Answer: B** — Direct fusion.

### Q58. Which AIDS stage is diagnosed when the individual is highly infectious and HIV antibody test is positive, even if initial symptoms have disappeared?
A. AIDS Related Complex (ARC)
B. Full Blown AIDS
C. Asymptomatic carrier
D. Opportunistic disease stage
**Answer: C** — Asymptomatic carrier stage.
