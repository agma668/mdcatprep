## 1. Kingdom Monera (Prokaryotes)
*The most ancient, abundant, and simplest organisms. Crucial for as it contrasts heavily with Eukaryotes.*

### A. Cellular Structure & Features
- **No Membrane-Bound Organelles:** Prokaryotes lack mitochondria, Golgi, ER, and chloroplasts. 
- **🔥 High-Yield - Non-Membrane Organelles:** **Ribosomes** (70S in bacteria vs 80S in eukaryotes), **Centrioles** (absent in bacteria, present in animals), and the **Cytoskeleton** are the only non-membrane-bound structures.
- **Cell Wall:** Made of **Peptidoglycan (Murein)**. *(Note: Archaebacteria have pseudopeptidoglycan, distinguishing them from true bacteria).*
- **Genetic Material:** A single, circular double-stranded DNA molecule localized in a region called the **Nucleoid**.
- **Plasmids:** Extra-chromosomal circular DNA that often carries antibiotic resistance genes.
- **Pili vs Flagella:** Pili are for attachment and mating (conjugation); Flagella are for motility.

### B. Bacterial Shapes (Morphology)
1. **Cocci:** Spherical. (e.g., *Streptococcus* forms chains, *Staphylococcus* forms grape-like clusters).
2. **Bacilli:** Rod-shaped. (e.g., *E. coli*, *Bacillus anthracis*).
3. **Spirilla:** Spiral-shaped. (e.g., *Treponema pallidum* causing syphilis).
4. **Vibrio:** Comma-shaped. (e.g., *Vibrio cholerae*).

### C. Gram Staining Technique
- **Gram-Positive:** Thick peptidoglycan layer. Retains the primary crystal violet stain. **Color: Purple/Blue.**
- **Gram-Negative:** Thin peptidoglycan layer + an outer lipopolysaccharide membrane. Loses primary stain but takes up the secondary counterstain (safranin). **Color: Pink/Red.**

### D. Reproduction & Genetics
- **Asexual:** **Binary Fission** (DNA replicates, cell elongates and divides. No mitosis/spindles).
- **Genetic Recombination (Not true sexual reproduction):**
 - **Conjugation:** Transfer of DNA (usually plasmids) via a sex pilus.
 - **Transformation:** Uptake of naked DNA from the surrounding environment.
 - **Transduction:** Transfer of DNA from one bacterium to another by a **Bacteriophage** (virus).

### E. Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae)
- **Concept:** They are true bacteria, NOT algae. They perform oxygenic photosynthesis.
- **Heterocysts:** Specialized, thick-walled cells in cyanobacteria (like *Nostoc* and *Anabaena*) that perform **Nitrogen Fixation** (converting N₂ into ammonia).

## 2. Kingdom Protista (The "Junk Drawer" Kingdom)
*A polyphyletic group consisting of all eukaryotes that are NOT plants, animals, or fungi.*

### A. Plant-like Protists (Algae)
- **🔥 High-Yield - Difference from Plants:** Algae differ from true plants because algae have **unicellular sex organs** and **lack a sterile jacket** of cells around their reproductive structures. Zygote is not protected inside the parent body.
- **Major Groups:** 
 - **Chlorophyta (Green Algae):** Ancestors to modern plants. (e.g., *Spirogyra*, *Chlamydomonas*).
 - **Diatoms:** Have glass-like cell walls made of silica. Major producers in oceans.
 - **Phaeophyta (Brown Algae):** Giant kelps.

### B. Animal-like Protists (Protozoans)
- **Amoebas:** Move using **pseudopodia** (false feet).
- **Ciliates:** Move using cilia (e.g., *Paramecium*). Have a macronucleus (metabolism) and micronucleus (reproduction).
- **Sporozoans:** Non-motile parasites.
 - **🔥 High-Yield:** *Plasmodium* causes malaria. It uses the female *Anopheles* mosquito as a vector. It infects the liver first, then RBCs.

### C. Fungi-like Protists
Historically misclassified as fungi due to their appearance.
- **Oomycota (Water Molds) & Myxomycota (Slime Molds):**
- **🔥 EXTREMELY HIGH-YIELD:** Why are they NOT true fungi? Because they possess **Centrioles** and their cell walls contain **Cellulose**. *(True fungi have chitin cell walls and lack centrioles).*

## 3. Kingdom Fungi
*Heterotrophic, eukaryotic organisms that digest their food externally before absorbing it (Absorptive Heterotrophs).*

### A. Structure & Features
- **Body:** Made of thread-like filaments called **Hyphae**. A network of hyphae is called a **Mycelium**.
- **Septate vs. Coenocytic:** Septate hyphae have cross-walls (septa) dividing cells. Coenocytic hyphae lack septa and are multinucleated.
- **Cell Wall:** Made of **Chitin** (the same substance found in the exoskeleton of arthropods).
- **Organelles:** They lack chloroplasts and **lack centrioles** (spindle pole bodies act as MTOCs instead).

### B. Classification (Based on Sexual Spores)
1. **Zygomycota:** Conjugating fungi (e.g., *Rhizopus* / bread mold). Form zygospores.
2. **Ascomycota:** Sac fungi (e.g., *Yeast*, *Penicillium*, truffles). Form ascospores inside a sac (ascus). Yeast is the only unicellular fungus.
3. **Basidiomycota:** Club fungi (e.g., Mushrooms, Puffballs, Rusts, Smuts). Form basidiospores on a club-shaped basidium.
4. **Deuteromycota:** Imperfect fungi. **Sexual reproduction has never been observed** in this group.

### C. Symbiotic Relationships
- **Lichens:** Mutualism between a **Fungus** (usually Ascomycota) and a **Photosynthetic partner** (cyanobacteria or green algae). They are excellent bioindicators of air pollution.
- **Mycorrhizae:** Mutualism between **Fungi** and **Plant Roots**. Fungus increases surface area for water/mineral absorption; plant provides sugars.

## 4. Kingdom Plantae (High-Yield Short Notes)
*Eukaryotic, multicellular, autotrophic (photosynthetic) organisms with cellulosic cell walls.*

### A. Bryophytes (Non-Vascular Plants)
- **Concept:** The "Amphibians of the plant world". They require water for fertilization (sperm must swim to egg).
- **Structure:** Lack true roots, stems, and leaves (lack vascular tissue: xylem & phloem). 
- **Generation:** Gametophyte (n) is dominant; sporophyte (2n) is dependent.

### B. Tracheophytes (Vascular Plants)
- **Concept:** Possess **Xylem** (water/minerals) and **Phloem** (sugars). 
- **Generation:** Sporophyte (2n) is dominant.

#### 1. Gymnosperms (Naked Seeds)
- Seeds are not enclosed in an ovary (no flowers or fruits).
- E.g., Conifers (Pines, Firs).

#### 2. Angiosperms (Flowering Plants)
- Seeds enclosed within an ovary (which becomes the fruit).
- Divided into two heavily tested classes:
 - **🔥 High-Yield - Monocots vs. Dicots:**
 - **Monocots:** **Lack Cambium** (hence, no secondary growth or wood), have **parallel leaf venation**, **fibrous root system**, floral parts in 3s, and scattered vascular bundles.
 - **Dicots:** Have **Cambium** (allowing secondary growth), **reticulate (net-like) venation**, **taproot system**, floral parts in 4s or 5s, and vascular bundles arranged in a ring.
