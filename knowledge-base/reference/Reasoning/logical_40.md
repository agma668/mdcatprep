## Cause and Effect

### Q1. Statement I: Heavy rainfall occurred in the region. Statement II: The rivers are flooded. Which is correct?
A. I is the effect, II is the cause
B. I is the cause, II is the effect
C. Both are effects
D. Independent events
**Answer: B** — Heavy rainfall causes rivers to flood.

### Q2. Statement I: The price of petrol increased. Statement II: Transportation costs increased. Which is correct?
A. Independent
B. I is effect, II is cause
C. Both are effects
D. I is cause, II is effect
**Answer: D** — Increased petrol prices directly lead to higher transportation costs.

### Q3. Statement I: The government increased taxes. Statement II: Consumer spending decreased. What is the relationship?
A. Independent events
B. Both effects
C. Both causes
D. I is cause, II is effect
**Answer: D** — Increased taxes reduce disposable income, leading to decreased consumer spending.

### Q4. Statement I: A new shopping mall opened in the city. Statement II: A famous actor visited the city. What is the relationship?
A. Independent events
B. I causes II
C. II causes I
D. Common cause
**Answer: A** — These are likely independent events with no causal connection.

### Q5. Statement I: India won the cricket match. Statement II: Stock market prices rose. These events are most likely:
A. Independent
B. I causes II
C. II causes I
D. Common cause
**Answer: A** — A cricket result and stock market movements are generally independent events.

### Q6. Statement I: The school introduced a new curriculum. Statement II: A new restaurant opened near the school. These are:
A. I causes II
B. Unrelated events
C. II causes I
D. Common cause
**Answer: B** — A curriculum change and a restaurant opening are unrelated events.

### Q7. Statement: 'Whenever I study at night, I get better grades.' Conclusion: 'Night studying causes better grades.' This argument is:
A. Valid
B. Invalid; correlation ≠ causation
C. Sound
D. Strong
**Answer: B** — The effect may result from total study time; personal observation cannot establish causation.

### Q8. Statement: Every time unemployment rises, crime rates rise. Which conclusion is valid?
A. Unemployment and crime are correlated/associated
B. Unemployment causes crime
C. Crime causes unemployment
D. No relation exists
**Answer: A** — Co-occurrence establishes correlation; causation requires further evidence.

### Q9. Statement: In controlled trials, Drug A significantly reduced blood pressure vs placebo. Conclusion: Drug A is effective in treating hypertension. This conclusion is:
A. Valid (RCTs give highest-level causal evidence)
B. Invalid
C. Weak
D. Unprovable
**Answer: A** — Randomised controlled trials provide the highest level of evidence for causal claims.

## Course of Action

### Q10. Statement: Traffic accidents have increased at an intersection. Course of Action: A traffic signal should be installed.
A. Irrelevant course
B. Reasonable, practical response
C. Extreme response
D. No action needed
**Answer: B** — Installing a traffic signal is a reasonable and practical response.

### Q11. Statement: 'Smoking should be banned in public places.' Argument: 'Passive smoking harms non-smokers.' This argument is:
A. Weak
B. Irrelevant
C. Strong — direct health impact on innocent bystanders
D. Fallacious
**Answer: C** — It highlights a direct health impact on innocent bystanders.

### Q12. Statement: 'The government should make education free for all.' Argument: 'Free education will burden the economy.' This argument is:
A. Strong — economic impact is a legitimate feasibility concern
B. Weak
C. Irrelevant
D. Fallacious
**Answer: A** — The economic impact directly relates to feasibility of the proposal.

### Q13. Statement: Heavy rains have caused flooding. Action I: Evacuate residents. Action II: Improve drainage systems.
A. Only I is appropriate
B. Both are appropriate
C. Only II is appropriate
D. Neither
**Answer: B** — Both immediate evacuation and long-term drainage improvement are appropriate.

### Q14. Statement: A school notices a significant drop in attendance. Action I: Investigate reasons. Action II: Expel all absent students.
A. Only I is logical
B. Only II is logical
C. Both
D. Neither
**Answer: A** — Investigating is logical; expelling is extreme and counterproductive.

### Q15. Statement: A company's sales have dropped for three consecutive quarters. Action I: Conduct market research. Action II: Immediately close down.
A. Only I is reasonable
B. Only II is reasonable
C. Both
D. Neither
**Answer: A** — Market research is reasonable; closing immediately is extreme.

### Q16. Statement: A new virus detected in a neighboring country. Action I: Screen at all entry points. Action II: Permanently seal all borders.
A. Only II is appropriate
B. Both
C. Only I is proportional/practical
D. Neither
**Answer: C** — Screening is proportionate; sealing borders is extreme and impractical.

## Critical Thinking

### Q17. Which of the following is a logical fallacy?
A. Testable claim
B. Valid syllogism
C. Sound argument
D. Attacking the person instead of the argument (ad hominem)
**Answer: D** — Ad hominem is a well-known logical fallacy.

### Q18. 'Everyone I know likes pizza, so everyone in the world must like pizza.' This is:
A. Valid deduction
B. Sound reasoning
C. Strong argument
D. Hasty generalization
**Answer: D** — Generalizing from a small sample to the entire world is hasty generalization.

### Q19. 'This medicine is good because a famous cricketer endorses it.' This reasoning is flawed because:
A. The medicine is cheap
B. A cricketer is not a medical authority (appeal to irrelevant authority)
C. The medicine tastes bad
D. Cricket is irrelevant to medicine
**Answer: B** — Using a celebrity endorsement is an appeal to irrelevant authority.

### Q20. All cats are animals. All animals need food. Which conclusion is valid?
A. Some cats are animals
B. All cats need food
C. All animals are cats
D. All food is animal
**Answer: B** — Syllogism: if all cats are animals and all animals need food, then all cats need food.

### Q21. 'If it rains, the ground gets wet.' The ground is wet. Which is true?
A. It may not have rained (affirming the consequent is a fallacy)
B. It definitely rained
C. It must not have rained
D. No conclusion possible
**Answer: A** — Other causes could make the ground wet.

### Q22. All doctors are educated. Some educated people are rich. Which conclusion follows?
A. Some doctors are rich
B. All rich people are doctors
C. No definite conclusion about doctors follows
D. All doctors are rich
**Answer: C** — Cannot determine which educated people are rich.

### Q23. If all A are B, and all B are C, then:
A. All A are C
B. All C are A
C. Some C are not A
D. None follows
**Answer: A** — Transitive syllogism.

### Q24. Premise 1: If it is a holiday, the office is closed. Premise 2: The office is open. Conclusion?
A. It is a holiday
B. It is not a holiday (modus tollens)
C. The office may be closed
D. Cannot determine
**Answer: B** — By modus tollens: if holiday then closed; not closed, therefore not a holiday.

### Q25. Which of the following is a valid argument form?
A. If P then Q; not Q; therefore not P (modus tollens)
B. If P then Q; Q; therefore P
C. If P then Q; P; therefore Q is false
D. P; Q; therefore if P then Q
**Answer: A** — Modus tollens is a valid argument form.

### Q27. What comes next: 5, 10, 15, 20, ?

**Answer: B** — Arithmetic series with common difference 5.

### Q28. What is the next term in: 2, 6, 18, 54, ?
A. 162
B. 108
C. 216
D. 150
**Answer: A** — Geometric progression with common ratio 3.

### Q29. Find the next term: 5, 20, 80, 320, ?
A. 640
B. 1000
C. 1280
D. 960
**Answer: C** — Geometric progression with common ratio 4.

### Q30. What comes next: A, C, E, G, ?
A. I
B. H
C. J
D. K
**Answer: A** — Alternate letters (skip one): A(+2)C(+2)E(+2)G(+2)I.

### Q31. What comes next: Z, Y, X, W, ?
A. U
B. X
C. Y
D. V
**Answer: D** — Alphabet in reverse order (Z→V... after W comes V).

## Logical Deductions

### Q32. Statement: All players in the team are over 20. Statement: Raza is a player. Conclusion: Raza is over 20. The conclusion:
A. Follows necessarily
B. Does not follow
C. Is weak
D. Is unverifiable
**Answer: A** — Since all players are over 20 and Raza is a player, Raza must be over 20.

### Q33. A is the father of B. B is the sister of C. C is the husband of D. How is A related to D?
A. Father
B. Uncle
C. Brother
D. Father-in-law
**Answer: D** — A is C's father; C is D's husband, making A D's father-in-law.

### Q34. Pointing to a photograph, a woman says, 'He is the son of my father's only daughter.' How is the person related to her?
A. Her son
B. Her brother
C. Her nephew
D. Her cousin
**Answer: A** — The woman's father's only daughter is the woman herself; the person is her son.

### Q35. Ali is 8th from the left and 12th from the right in a row. How many children are in the row?
A. 20
B. 18
C. 21
D. 19
**Answer: D** — Total = 8 + 12 - 1 = 19.

### Q36. Sana is 10th from the front and 15th from the back in a queue. How many people are in the queue?
A. 25
B. 23
C. 26
D. 24
**Answer: D** — Total = 10 + 15 - 1 = 24.

## Logical Problems

### Q37. If all Bloops are Razzies and all Razzies are Lazzies, then all Bloops are definitely:
A. Razzies only
B. Not Lazzies
C. Only Bloops
D. Lazzies
**Answer: D** — By transitive reasoning, all Bloops are Lazzies.

### Q38. Ahmad is taller than Bilal. Bilal is taller than Chaudhry. Dawood is taller than Ahmad. Who is the tallest?
A. Dawood
B. Ahmad
C. Bilal
D. Chaudhry
**Answer: A** — Dawood > Ahmad > Bilal > Chaudhry.

### Q39. A man facing north turns 90° clockwise, then 180° anticlockwise, then 45° clockwise. Which direction is he facing?
A. North
B. North-West
C. South
D. South-East
**Answer: B** — North + 90 CW = East; + 180 ACW = West; + 45 CW = North-West.

### Q40. A man starts at A, walks 4 km East, 3 km North, 4 km West. How far is he from A?
A. 5 km
B. 3 km
C. 4 km
D. 7 km
**Answer: B** — East 4 then North 3 then West 4 → directly 3 km North of start.
